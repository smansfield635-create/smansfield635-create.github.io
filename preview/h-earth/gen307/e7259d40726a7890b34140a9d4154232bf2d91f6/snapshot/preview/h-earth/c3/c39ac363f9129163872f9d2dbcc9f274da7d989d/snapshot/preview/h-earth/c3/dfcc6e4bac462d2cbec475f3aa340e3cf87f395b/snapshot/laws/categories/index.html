Laws Category 01

Flow

Flow reads movement, transition, pressure, and change. It asks how a system moves from one state into another and what breaks when movement loses orientation.

Movement Transition Pressure Direction
Platform

Flow explains how change behaves. It is the category for motion, transition, timing, direction, pressure, and state movement.

  • What is moving?
  • What pressure is driving movement?
  • What transition must be survived?
  • What direction is being preserved or lost?
Engineering

Flow maps to pipelines, queues, event streams, state transitions, timing windows, process movement, routing, and load propagation.

  • Track input, transition, and output state.
  • Monitor bottlenecks and backpressure.
  • Separate movement from completion.
  • Detect where transition becomes instability.
Evidence

Evidence for Flow requires observable movement through time, not isolated static claims.

  • Strong evidence: event history, transition logs, timing records, throughput changes.
  • Useful evidence: process traces, queue behavior, before / after state records.
  • Boundary: a single snapshot does not prove a flow pattern.

Laws Category 02

Integrity

Integrity reads whether a system remains true across time, pressure, transfer, and consequence.

Consistency Accountability Continuity Coherence
Platform

Integrity asks whether a system can remain itself while time, pressure, responsibility, memory, and consequence change around it.

  • Consistency: alignment across time.
  • Accountability: ownership of outcomes.
  • Continuity: what carries forward.
  • Coherence: no internal contradiction.
Engineering

Integrity maps to deterministic behavior, auditability, state continuity, version discipline, repair loops, contradiction detection, and historical traceability.

  • Track standards across changing incentives.
  • Attach ownership to produced outcomes.
  • Preserve memory through transfer.
  • Detect contradictions between claims, actions, and results.
Evidence

Evidence for Integrity requires stable records over time and under changing pressure.

  • Strong evidence: repeatable outcomes, ownership records, continuity records, contradiction reviews.
  • Useful evidence: audit trails, repair logs, lineage records, version history.
  • Boundary: stated values are not evidence unless execution and outcome remain connected.

Laws Category 03

Reality

Reality reads what is actually present, observable, bounded, and testable. It separates claim from condition.

Observation Evidence Boundary Condition
Platform

Reality checks whether the system is being described as it is, not as it is desired, marketed, remembered, or projected.

  • What is actually present?
  • What is observable?
  • What is bounded?
  • What remains unknown or unproven?
Engineering

Reality maps to telemetry, measurement, state inspection, test boundaries, source provenance, empirical status, and falsifiable condition checks.

  • Separate claim from measurement.
  • Keep provenance attached to evidence.
  • Declare unknowns and unevaluated states.
  • Prevent unsupported promotion of evidence status.
Evidence

Evidence for Reality requires records that can be inspected, bounded, and distinguished from inference.

  • Strong evidence: measurements, source records, reproducible checks, bounded claims.
  • Useful evidence: observations, logs, review notes, uncertainty statements.
  • Boundary: a plausible interpretation is not the same thing as validated reality.

Laws Category 04

Structure

Structure reads what gives a system durable shape. It defines limits, contact points, separation, and rule preservation.

Constraints Interfaces Boundaries Governance
Platform

Structure keeps force from becoming noise. It tells the system what limits action, where exchange happens, what separates domains, and how rules stay active.

  • Constraints define what limits action.
  • Interfaces define where systems meet and translate.
  • Boundaries define what separates domains and prevents leakage.
  • Governance defines how rules stay real under pressure.
Engineering

Structure maps to capacity planning, contract surfaces, domain boundaries, governance gates, ownership records, access rules, and system containment.

  • Measure limits before overload.
  • Define contracts before exchange.
  • Protect domain boundaries before leakage.
  • Keep governance active after pressure arrives.
Evidence

Evidence for Structure requires proof that limits, interfaces, boundaries, and governance rules are visible and enforced.

  • Strong evidence: capacity records, contract tests, boundary logs, gate records.
  • Useful evidence: route maps, ownership records, validation results, audit records.
  • Boundary: structure is not proven by naming parts; the parts must hold under pressure.
