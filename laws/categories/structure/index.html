Laws Category · Structure

What holds.

Structure is the category for what gives a system durable shape. It names the limits, meeting points, separations, and rule systems that prevent action from dissolving into confusion.

Four-track gate

Structure contains four major constellation tracks: Constraints, Interfaces, Boundaries, and Governance.

  • Constraints define what limits action.
  • Interfaces define where systems meet and translate.
  • Boundaries define what separates domains and prevents leakage.
  • Governance defines how rules stay real under pressure.
Registry rule

These four tracks are the Structure constellation stops. Internal sections such as Capacity, Translation, Containment, and Gates remain inside their track pages.

Structure Track 01

Constraints

Constraints define the limits that make structure real: capacity, rules, thresholds, and tradeoffs.

Capacity Rules Thresholds Tradeoffs
Platform

Constraints prevent fantasy structure. They state what can happen, what cannot happen, and when action becomes unsafe or impossible.

  • Capacity defines what can be carried.
  • Rules define what is allowed.
  • Thresholds define when a state changes.
  • Tradeoffs define what must yield when limits conflict.
Engineering

Constraints map to resource budgets, validation rules, saturation points, error budgets, admission criteria, guard clauses, and tradeoff analysis.

  • Measure load against capacity.
  • Encode hard rules where enforcement is possible.
  • Bind thresholds to action, not decoration.
  • Track cost across both gained and sacrificed dimensions.
Evidence

Evidence for Constraints requires measurable limits, explicit rules, threshold history, and named tradeoffs.

  • Strong evidence: load tests, rule logs, failure correlation, tradeoff records.
  • Failure mode: promises exceed measurable carrying capacity.
  • Boundary: a constraint is not a preference unless enforcement and crossing conditions are defined.

Structure Track 02

Interfaces

Interfaces define where systems meet, translate, exchange, fail at the edge, and confirm contact.

Contract Translation Failure Surface Handshake
Platform

Interfaces are structure at the point of contact. They determine what can pass, how meaning is preserved, and how contact becomes valid transfer.

  • The contract defines the exchange.
  • Translation preserves meaning.
  • The failure surface protects the edge.
  • The handshake confirms whether contact succeeded.
Engineering

Interfaces map to API contracts, schemas, adapters, validation errors, fallback policy, acknowledgments, response codes, and commit confirmations.

  • Version contracts when meaning changes.
  • Track lossy transformations.
  • Return actionable errors.
  • Use explicit acknowledgement rather than assumed success.
Evidence

Evidence for Interfaces requires schema records, mapping tests, negative tests, no-match records, and handshake receipts.

  • Strong evidence: contract tests, round-trip checks, failure logs, receipt events.
  • Failure mode: successful demo hides broken edge behavior.
  • Boundary: contact is not valid transfer until meaning and state are confirmed.

Structure Track 03

Boundaries

Boundaries define what separates domains, controls crossing, prevents leakage, and contains failure.

Separation Crossing Rule Leakage Control Containment
Platform

Boundaries preserve identity at the edge. They make clear what belongs inside, what remains outside, what may cross, and what must never leak.

  • Separation defines the domain.
  • Crossing controls movement.
  • Leakage exposes undeclared transfer.
  • Containment prevents spread.
Engineering

Boundaries map to namespaces, access domains, authorization gates, tenant isolation, trust zones, leakage monitoring, sandboxing, and blast-radius control.

  • Declare scope explicitly.
  • Gate crossing through permission checks.
  • Detect privilege and state bleed.
  • Use isolation to stop propagation.
Evidence

Evidence for Boundaries requires domain records, crossing logs, leakage reports, and containment receipts.

  • Strong evidence: access maps, transfer receipts, violation records, quarantine logs.
  • Failure mode: adjacent domains are treated as interchangeable.
  • Boundary: lawful crossing is not leakage when permission, provenance, and containment are preserved.

Structure Track 04

Governance

Governance defines how rules stay real under pressure through authority, incentives, gates, and stability.

Authority Incentives Gates Stability
Platform

Governance is structure after pressure arrives. It keeps rules active when incentives, urgency, scale, or power make violation useful.

  • Authority defines who may decide.
  • Incentives reveal what the system will protect.
  • Gates make rules operational.
  • Stability proves governance survives time and pressure.
Engineering

Governance maps to ownership records, approval rights, KPI alignment, policy checks, release gates, governance-as-code, audit cadence, and drift monitoring.

  • Separate read access from change authority.
  • Detect incentives that reward bypassing gates.
  • Record manual overrides explicitly.
  • Monitor governance drift over time.
Evidence

Evidence for Governance requires approval records, incentive records, gate logs, override history, and repeated enforcement over time.

  • Strong evidence: ownership registry, violation consequences, gate logs, audit history.
  • Failure mode: the rule is public but bypassed privately.
  • Boundary: governance that works once is not yet stable governance.

Narrative Separation Rule

Structure tracks must not collapse into each other.

Constraints, Interfaces, Boundaries, and Governance are four different Structure tracks. Each may contain internal sections, but those internal sections are not new constellation stops.

Constraints What limits action?

Capacity, rules, thresholds, and tradeoffs.

Interfaces Where do systems meet?

Contracts, translation, failure surfaces, and handshakes.

Boundaries What separates domains?

Separation, crossing rules, leakage control, and containment.

Governance How do rules stay real?

Authority, incentives, gates, and stability.
