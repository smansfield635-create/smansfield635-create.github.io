# LAWS CAROUSEL REFERENCE-PORT MASTER STRATEGY v1

Status: FROZEN CONSTRUCTION AUTHORITY — AMENDED 2026-08-16
Scope: Laws destination carousel and future carousels using the same contextual layout
Canonical reference implementation: `laws/research/methods-and-models/`

## 1. Governing decision

The Methods & Models carousel is the normative implementation, not merely an inspirational reference.

For any carousel developed with this contextual layout, Methods & Models owns the complete spatial and behavioral interaction standard. A consuming page may vary content, information grouping, destination semantics, visual language, stage placement, and page-specific context. It MUST NOT reinterpret or simplify the Methods carousel's physical behavior merely from verbal descriptions.

Canonical equation:

`METHODS & MODELS CAROUSEL SYSTEM + PAGE CONTEXT/CONTENT/PRESENTATION = CONTEXTUAL CAROUSEL`

For Laws specifically:

`METHODS & MODELS CAROUSEL SYSTEM + LAWS DATA + LAWS VISUAL LANGUAGE + LAWS MINI-PAGE CONTEXT = LAWS CAROUSEL`

When a verbal interaction rule conflicts with the functioning Methods implementation, the functioning Methods implementation controls unless the repository standard is explicitly amended first.

## 2. Canonical Methods behavior

The standard includes the complete functioning physical model observed in Methods & Models:

- one shared Euclidean ring/orbit;
- one authoritative continuous spatial coordinate;
- direct pointer/finger custody of that coordinate;
- continuous ring rotation during horizontal drag;
- drag-time transition suppression;
- gesture travel and velocity as physical inputs;
- momentum/inertial continuation when sufficient gesture energy is imparted;
- the ability for a sufficiently strong swipe to rotate/spin through more than one object;
- natural release resolution and canonical settlement;
- normalized circular seam traversal;
- persistent neighboring-card depth and spatial legibility;
- one shared selection primitive for keyboard/card/programmatic selection;
- inspection/open exclusion while spatial settlement is active;
- spatial continuity through inspection and Return to Orbit;
- responsive behavior and vertical document traversal;
- reduced-motion treatment.

The momentum/spin behavior is canonical even though it emerged unintentionally during the original Methods construction. Proven functioning behavior is part of the standard once admitted here.

## 4. Superseded verbal rule

The rule `ONE HORIZONTAL FINGER SWIPE = EXACTLY ONE NEIGHBORING ROTATION` is SUPERSEDED as a governing carousel law.

It was useful diagnostically because it exposed release nondeterminism, but it overconstrained the canonical Methods physical model.

The correct law is:

`HORIZONTAL GESTURE -> CONTINUOUS DIRECT ORBIT MANIPULATION -> VELOCITY/MOMENTUM RESPONSE -> NATURAL CANONICAL SETTLEMENT`

A controlled swipe may commonly settle on an adjacent object. A sufficiently energetic swipe may continue through multiple objects/spin, exactly as the Methods reference permits. Multi-object traversal caused by legitimate momentum is not a defect.

Defects include discontinuous geometry, competing spatial authorities, unintended second settlement, loss of finger custody, incorrect direction, broken seam traversal, noncanonical final settlement, or behavior that materially diverges from Methods under an equivalent gesture.

## 5. Accounted deficiencies and binding remedies

### D01 — Architectural drift
Laws diverged from the proven Methods carousel and became a separate engine.
Remedy: Methods is the implementation authority; Laws must be a contextual adaptation.

### D02 — Legacy Laws contamination
Legacy Laws mechanics were retained beyond contextual necessity.
Remedy: retain Laws context/content/page responsibilities only where they do not compete with Methods mechanics.

### D03 — Multiple spatial authorities
Per-card geometry and independent orbit/detent/target systems competed.
Remedy: one shared ring and one continuous spatial coordinate.

### D04 — Reinvented gesture law
Direction-only, atomic-step, and later one-swipe-one-step rules overrode the functioning Methods model.
Remedy: inherit Methods direct manipulation, velocity, momentum, spin, and settlement behavior without page-local reinterpretation.

### D05 — Settlement discontinuity
Gesture and settlement became separate geometry systems.
Remedy: the same spatial coordinate survives drag, momentum, and settlement.

### D06 — Excess state-machine complexity
Defensive transaction states accumulated around derivative mechanics.
Remedy: use the Methods state grammar; additional state requires a page-context justification.

### D07 — Spatial/semantic divergence
Visible geometry and selected identity could disagree.
Remedy: semantic identity follows the canonical Methods selection/settlement law.

### D08 — Circular seam ambiguity
Alternate detent identities complicated circular traversal.
Remedy: normalized circular identity and continuous orbit behavior.

### D09 — Competing interaction ownership
Pointer, focus, click, keyboard, restore, and tabs could own separate geometry paths.
Remedy: one spatial kernel; non-pointer pathways route through its selection primitive.

### D10 — Stage/carousel coupling
Page-stage logic gained spatial responsibilities.
Remedy: stage owns context; Methods kernel owns carousel physics.

### D11 — Content-integrity risk
A reference port could flatten Laws information architecture.
Remedy: preserve Laws data and contextual delivery while replacing mechanics.

### D12 — Visual-language confusion
Mechanical porting could transplant page styling or suppress spatial legibility.
Remedy: Laws retains its visual language, but neighboring depth/presence must remain sufficiently legible to express the canonical orbit.

### D13 — Synthetic qualification bias
Tests certified implementation-specific telemetry rather than physical equivalence.
Remedy: comparative Methods/Laws behavior is controlling.

### D14 — Physical-device evidence underweighted
Synthetic PASS overrode contradictory touch behavior.
Remedy: physical comparison remains controlling evidence for touch interaction.

### D15 — Runtime identity ambiguity
Executed code could not always be tied to the candidate.
Remedy: deterministic contract/build identity remains required during qualification.

### D16 — Cache/deployment ambiguity
Merged source did not prove executed source.
Remedy: immutable candidate/build identity and executed-runtime evidence.

### D17 — Stale instrumentation
Verifiers encoded superseded laws.
Remedy: every verifier is classified `REFERENCE_VALID`, `PAGE_CONTEXT_VALID`, `REWRITE_REQUIRED`, or `SUPERSEDED`.

### D18 — Audit implementation bias
Tests focused on detents and transaction internals.
Remedy: test physical/observable invariants and direct comparison with Methods.

### D19 — Construction-cycle truncation
Audit failures were treated as stopping points.
Remedy: `CONSTRUCT -> AUDIT -> DEFICIENCY -> REPAIR -> RE-AUDIT` repeats until qualified and merged or genuinely terminal.

### D20 — Future reference drift
Page-local modifications could gradually create new carousel species.
Remedy: future contextual carousels inherit Methods behavior exactly. Spatial/behavioral changes require an explicit standard amendment.

### D21 — Neighbor suppression
Laws can be technically ring-based while visually reading as isolated card replacement if neighbors are excessively suppressed.
Remedy: preserve enough neighboring presence, depth, and traversal visibility to make the shared orbit perceptible, using Methods as the comparative standard.

### D22 — Momentum suppression
Bounding every swipe to one index removes a canonical affordance of the Methods implementation.
Remedy: restore equivalent velocity/momentum and inertial spin behavior.

### D23 — Mini-page underutilization
Treating the Laws carousel as a small subordinate widget unnecessarily weakens the lower page stage.
Remedy: allow the lower carousel region to operate as a complete contextual mini-page while preserving Compass dominance above.

## 8. Comparative qualification law

Methods & Models is the oracle.

Equivalent gesture classes must be compared across Methods and the contextual carousel:
- slow controlled drag;
- short drag/release;
- normal swipe;
- fast swipe;
- high-energy spin gesture;
- reverse gesture;
- repeated gestures;
- seam traversal;
- vertical gesture;
- keyboard selection;
- inspection/return;
- reduced motion.

Qualification evaluates:
- direction;
- finger/ring coupling;
- continuous spatial response;
- velocity response;
- momentum/inertial continuation;
- number and character of objects traversed under comparable energy;
- neighboring depth/visibility;
- final canonical settlement;
- absence of unintended post-settlement correction;
- content identity and page-context integrity.

Exact numerical equality with Methods is not required where page geometry differs, but behavioral character and governing mechanics must be equivalent. Any deliberate behavioral deviation requires the deviation procedure below.

## 9. Verifier classification

- `REFERENCE_VALID`: proves a Methods-standard spatial/behavioral invariant.
- `PAGE_CONTEXT_VALID`: proves preserved contextual content/page behavior without redefining carousel physics.
- `REWRITE_REQUIRED`: useful intent but superseded implementation assumptions.
- `SUPERSEDED`: no longer applicable and cannot block or certify the successor.

Any verifier asserting `one swipe = exactly one index` as universal correctness is now `SUPERSEDED` or `REWRITE_REQUIRED`.

## 12. Construction-cycle law

`REFERENCE -> CONSTRUCT -> AUDIT -> DEFICIENCY -> REPAIR -> RE-AUDIT -> ... -> QUALIFIED -> MERGE`

An audit failure is construction input, not a reporting boundary. The cycle ends only at merge or a documented genuinely terminal blocker.

## 13. Deviation rule

Any intentional departure from Methods behavior requires, before implementation:
1. exact Methods behavior;
2. page-specific requirement that cannot be satisfied by it;
3. smallest proposed deviation;
4. deterministic invariant for the deviation;
5. comparative qualification method;
6. explicit amendment/admission into this authority.

Undocumented behavioral or spatial deviation is a defect.
