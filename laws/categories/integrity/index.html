Integrity · Four-Track Category Gate

What stays true.

Integrity is the Laws category for whether a system remains true across pressure, audience, time, consequence, and contradiction. This page separates the four Integrity constellation tracks without turning their internal subfacets into additional compass points.

Category standing

Integrity has four major tracks in the sixteen-track Laws constellation: Consistency, Accountability, Continuity, and Coherence.

  • Four major tracks only.
  • Internal subfacets stay inside their parent track.
  • The linear path starts at Consistency and continues through Accountability, Continuity, and Coherence.
Do not mix the narratives

Consistency is alignment. Accountability is ownership. Continuity is persistence through time. Coherence is no internal contradiction.

Integrity Track 01

Consistency

Consistency is alignment across time. It is the first visible sign of integrity because actions, claims, and outcomes remain aligned regardless of pressure, audience, or circumstance.

Alignment across time Repeatable behavior Consistency Principle Constraint Preservation
Platform

Consistency means the system does not rewrite its standard to justify the latest behavior. It should reduce exceptions, explanations, and contradictions across contexts.

  • Same claim, same standard.
  • Same condition, same expectation.
  • Same pressure class, same integrity requirement.
  • Values do not shift to excuse behavior.
Engineering

In engineering terms, Consistency maps to deterministic behavior, stable classification, repeatable outputs, bounded variance, and preserved constraints under comparable inputs.

  • Identical conditions should produce identical outcomes.
  • Observers should classify the same record the same way.
  • Configuration and environment drift should be detectable.
  • Exceptions should be named, logged, and bounded.
Evidence

Evidence for Consistency requires repeated observations across time and context. A single good result is not enough.

  • Strong evidence: repeated same-class outcomes under same-class conditions.
  • Useful evidence: exception logs and variance records.
  • Failure mode: one-off success presented as stable behavior.
  • Boundary: consistency does not prove truth; it proves repeatability under declared conditions.
Canonical Law Consistency Principle

Identical conditions must produce identical outcomes.

Canonical Law Constraint Preservation

Integrity requires the constraint to remain active when pressure or incentive changes.

Integrity Track 02

Accountability

Accountability is ownership of outcomes. It rejects excuse transfer and requires fast ownership, clear correction, and measurable repair.

Ownership of outcomes Measurable repair Accountability Principle Repair Requirement
Platform

Accountability means a claim has an owner, a consequence, and a repair path. It is not apology language; it is closed-loop responsibility.

  • Own the outcome without moving blame to the audience, pressure, or environment.
  • Name what failed.
  • Correct the condition, not only the explanation.
  • Measure whether recurrence declines.
Engineering

In engineering terms, Accountability maps to issue ownership, root-cause tracking, incident closure, repair bandwidth, recurrence detection, and post-correction verification.

  • Every failure class should have an owner.
  • Every incident should distinguish symptom, cause, correction, and verification.
  • Repairs should be observable in future behavior.
  • Unowned failure should be treated as an active risk.
Evidence

Evidence for Accountability requires more than admitting fault. It requires traceable correction and measurable change.

  • Strong evidence: owner, corrective action, timestamp, verification, reduced recurrence.
  • Useful evidence: postmortems, repair receipts, reopened issue count.
  • Failure mode: apology without measurable repair.
  • Boundary: accountability evidence does not require perfection; it requires owned correction.
Canonical Law Accountability Principle

Claims require ownership of measurable outcomes.

Canonical Law Repair Requirement

Ownership is incomplete until correction can be inspected.

Integrity Track 03

Continuity

Continuity is what persists across time. Its internal subfacets are Memory, Transfer, Trajectory, and Decay, but those are sections inside Continuity, not extra constellation points.

Persistence through time Memory Transfer Trajectory Decay
Platform

Continuity asks whether the system still carries the same truth after time, pressure, context change, and delayed correction.

  • Memory: what remains after explanation fades.
  • Transfer: whether standards survive environment change.
  • Trajectory: the direction implied by repeated decisions.
  • Decay: uncorrected exceptions becoming structure.
Engineering

In engineering terms, Continuity maps to retained state, portability, time-ordered behavior, drift detection, correction latency, and maintenance discipline.

  • State should survive restart, migration, and handoff.
  • Standards should survive runtime and environment change.
  • Trajectory should be read from time series, not isolated points.
  • Known exceptions should not become permanent operating mode.
Evidence

Evidence for Continuity requires ordered records across time. It must show retained state, context transfer, direction, and repair or decay.

  • Strong evidence: version history, retained logs, cross-context tests, time-series behavior.
  • Useful evidence: issue age, recurrence, correction latency, migration outcomes.
  • Failure mode: treating a remembered story as retained evidence.
  • Boundary: Continuity subfacets remain internal to this track.
Internal Laws Persistence / Context / Direction

Continuity holds when state persists, standards transfer, and direction remains inspectable.

Internal Laws Evidence Retention / Stability / Maintenance

Continuity fails when records vanish, contexts distort standards, or uncorrected exceptions accumulate.

Integrity Track 04

Coherence

Coherence is no internal contradiction. It asks whether what is known, learned, historically carried, and visibly impactful can exist together without fragmentation.

No internal contradiction Known Learned History Impact
Platform

Coherence means the system does not require contradiction to continue. What it says, knows, learns, remembers, and causes must fit together.

  • Known: what the system openly recognizes.
  • Learned: what becomes obvious after failure or cost.
  • History: what the system carries forward.
  • Impact: what the system actually causes.
Engineering

In engineering terms, Coherence maps to contradiction detection, state reconciliation, consistent source-of-truth boundaries, dependency alignment, and repair after fragmentation.

  • Detect inconsistent records across systems.
  • Reconcile declared state against observed behavior.
  • Prevent one subsystem from preserving a contradiction another subsystem denies.
  • Treat unresolved contradiction as integrity debt.
Evidence

Evidence for Coherence requires cross-checking claims, records, outcomes, and learned corrections. A coherent system can explain itself without hiding contradiction.

  • Strong evidence: aligned records, outcomes, correction history, and source-of-truth reconciliation.
  • Useful evidence: contradiction logs, no-match cases, issue closure records.
  • Failure mode: narrative unity without factual reconciliation.
  • Boundary: Coherence subfacets remain internal to this track.
Canonical Law Coherence Constraint Law

A system cannot remain integral while depending on unresolved contradiction.

Canonical Law Fragmentation Detection Law

The first visible contradiction often reveals a deeper split in memory, ownership, or consequence.
