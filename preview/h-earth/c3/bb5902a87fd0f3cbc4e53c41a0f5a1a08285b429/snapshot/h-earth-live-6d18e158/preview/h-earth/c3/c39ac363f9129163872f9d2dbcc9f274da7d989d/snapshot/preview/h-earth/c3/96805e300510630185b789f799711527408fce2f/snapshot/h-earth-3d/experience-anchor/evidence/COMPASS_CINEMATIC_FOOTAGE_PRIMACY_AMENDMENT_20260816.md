# Compass Cinematic Footage Primacy Amendment

Status: CANONICAL AMENDMENT
Date: 2026-08-16
Applies to: `COMPASS_CINEMATIC_RESEARCH_AND_PRODUCTION_STANDARD_20260816.md` and all current/future Awards Chapter 01 Compass film construction

## Purpose

This amendment corrects the final major failure mode identified after the cinematic research program: allowing narrative structure to become so dense that it suppresses the value of the footage itself.

The Awards page exists to support Diamond Gate Bridge and its award campaign. The Compass film therefore has a dual duty:

1. communicate the established narrative about navigation, orientation, connection, reality, home, and the Compass; and
2. spotlight the quality, originality, atmosphere, motion, objects, environments, and cinematic potential of the website itself.

The second duty is not subordinate decoration. The footage is the evidence.

## Governing principle

FOOTAGE PRIMACY LAW:

The story exists to organize and intensify the footage. The footage does not exist merely to illustrate a dense story.

The film must never become so narratively busy that the viewer loses the opportunity to experience the website's strongest visual work.

Canonical priority:

FOOTAGE -> PERCEPTION -> MEANING -> NEXT IDEA

not:

SCRIPT DENSITY -> CONSTANT EXPLANATION -> VISUAL SUPPRESSION

## Awards-page authority

This is an awards-supporting presentation. The film is part of the evidence for the site's visual, experiential, interaction, and worldbuilding quality.

Therefore:

- hero footage must receive uncontested screen time;
- major reveals must be allowed to breathe;
- the film must contain moments where no new textual proposition is being introduced;
- text may frame the meaning of a visual, but must not continuously compete with it;
- the strongest site experiences must be shown at their strongest moments rather than reduced to transitional filler;
- visual quality, movement, coherence, detail, and atmosphere are themselves part of the argument for award worthiness;
- the film must feel like a short cinematic showcase of a living website, not a narrated explainer with background imagery.

## Interaction with the READ -> LOOK law

The existing attention-bandwidth law remains mandatory:

READ -> LOOK -> READ -> LOOK

This amendment strengthens the LOOK state.

LOOK does not mean a brief pause between statements. LOOK means a protected cinematic interval in which the audience is expected to inspect, absorb, and enjoy the image.

For a hero visual, the LOOK interval must be long enough for the subject, motion, scale, atmosphere, and intended reveal to register.

Text should frequently arrive at the edge of a scene: before the reveal, during a settling interval, or as the current visual prepares to transition. It should then clear so the next visual event can dominate.

## Hero-shot law

A HERO shot is any shot whose primary purpose is to demonstrate the quality or distinctiveness of the website itself.

A hero shot must:

- have a clear subject;
- begin from a legible state;
- contain intentional motion, transformation, reveal, or atmospheric hold;
- receive sufficient duration to be perceived;
- remain visually unobstructed during its strongest moment;
- not be arbitrarily accelerated to satisfy runtime;
- not be subordinated to text placement convenience;
- not be cropped in a way that removes the visual feature being showcased.

The picture cut must contain enough hero intervals that the film remains memorable even with all text removed.

## Story-density ceiling

Narrative structure is required, but narrative density has a ceiling.

Reject a cut if:

- every scene exists only to service a sentence;
- there is no sustained visual pleasure or discovery;
- the audience is asked to process a new proposition every few seconds without recovery;
- the film feels like an illustrated essay rather than a cinematic experience;
- the best footage appears only under text or during transitions;
- important environmental or object detail is visible for too little time to register;
- the script dictates cuts that damage stronger visual continuity;
- the viewer could understand the words but not remember the imagery.

## Silent-picture qualification

Before text and final music are admitted, the picture cut must pass a silent-picture test.

Qualification question:

IF ALL TEXT AND MUSIC WERE REMOVED, WOULD THIS STILL FEEL LIKE A DELIBERATELY SHOT ONE-MINUTE CINEMATIC SHOWCASE OF DIAMOND GATE BRIDGE?

If the answer is no, the picture cut fails.

This test does not require literal silence in the final film. It is a construction gate used to prove that cinematic value is present in the footage and edit themselves rather than being manufactured by overlays or soundtrack.

## Awards-film hierarchy

For this film, authority is layered as follows:

1. RAW FOOTAGE QUALITY
2. CINEMATIC PICTURE SELECTION AND PACING
3. VISUAL NARRATIVE COHERENCE
4. ATTENTION CHOREOGRAPHY
5. TEXT
6. MUSIC / SOUND DESIGN
7. FINISHING / POLISH

A later layer may elevate an earlier layer but may never compensate for its failure.

## Runtime law

Approximately one minute remains the target, but runtime is not an excuse to destroy hero footage.

If the eight-beat narrative cannot coexist with sufficient hero-shot dwell inside the target runtime, reduce presentation density before accelerating or truncating the footage.

Permissible methods include:

- split text appearing at quieter edges of existing shots;
- combining conceptual beats through one strong visual passage where causally appropriate;
- allowing one visual to carry more than one narrative implication;
- shortening textual exposure where comprehension remains safe;
- reducing decorative transition time.

Impermissible methods include:

- arbitrary speed-up of hero material;
- cutting away before a reveal resolves;
- stacking text on top of the strongest visual event;
- replacing cinematography with explanatory density.
