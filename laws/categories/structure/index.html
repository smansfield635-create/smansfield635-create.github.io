Constraints

  1. Capacity

    Practical
    Constraints prevent fantasy structure. They state what can happen, what cannot happen, and when action becomes unsafe or impossible. Capacity defines what can be carried.
    Engineering
    Constraints map to resource budgets, validation rules, saturation points, error budgets, admission criteria, guard clauses, and tradeoff analysis. Measure load against capacity.
    Empirical
    For Constraints requires measurable limits, explicit rules, threshold history, and named tradeoffs. Strong evidence: load tests, rule logs, failure correlation, tradeoff records.

  5. Battery study relationship

    Practical
    Case selection, a fixed event, a fixed horizon, comparators, and required controls shaped what the study could conclude. This relationship connects the law subject to an observed battery study without changing the study's evidence standing.
    Engineering
    Study: Battery health. Primary Frontier domain: Power and Energy. The complete execution owner remains the Frontier battery study.
    Empirical
    Current evidence: domain-specific cell-disjoint held-out empirical support. The combined model reached AUROC 0.9394 across 1,653 held-out cycle records; the burden comparator reached AUROC 0.9704. Claim boundary: Changing those constraints creates a different study requiring new evaluation.
    Study: Battery health · Primary Frontier domain: Power and Energy

    What this law explains: Case selection, a fixed event, a fixed horizon, comparators, and required controls shaped what the study could conclude.

    What the study observed: The combined model reached AUROC 0.9394 across 1,653 held-out cycle records from three cells; the burden comparator reached AUROC 0.9704.

    Where the claim stops: Changing those constraints creates a different study requiring new evaluation.
