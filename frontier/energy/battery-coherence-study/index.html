Applied empirical track · Battery degradation

A framework entered a real physical system and accepted the result it earned.

Batteries gave Diamond Gate Bridge a disciplined first test of whether the coherence architecture could be translated into measurable variables, evaluated on unseen systems, compared with conventional predictors, and governed without turning every outcome into a validation claim.

01 · Why batteries

Why begin with a degrading physical system?

Batteries provide repeated measurements, visible capacity loss, meaningful thresholds, comparable cells, and direct engineering and economic consequence.

A battery makes degradation understandable: it stores less, supports less work, and eventually crosses a threshold that matters. That made it a practical place to ask whether system-wide deterioration could be detected before the threshold was crossed.

The domain offers longitudinal cycles, retained-capacity trajectories, cell-level separation, and a near-term event target. Those properties support a genuine held-out test rather than a descriptive illustration.

Evaluation used 1,653 cycles from CS2_34, CS2_36, and CS2_38. All three batteries were excluded from development. The target was crossing below 80% retained capacity within the next 20 cycles.

02 · Coherence in this domain

What does coherence mean in a battery study?

Not consciousness, harmony, or a metaphor. It is an analytical representation of whether multiple dimensions remain mutually supportive as degradation approaches.

The question was whether approaching degradation appears only as one worsening number, or as a coordinated loss of support across burden, pressure, energy, integrity, and viability.

The B/P/E/I/V axes were treated as derived domain representations. Combined, component, summary, weakest-axis, and hard-threshold formulations were tested separately so one success could not automatically validate the rest.

03 · The held-out test

Development ended before the final batteries were judged.

The model was constructed upstream, then evaluated on batteries it had not seen.

This is the difference between recognizing the examples used to teach a system and succeeding on new examples after teaching has stopped.

The split was cell-disjoint rather than cycle-random. The event horizon was 20 cycles. Performance was compared across the combined axes, burden, H*, weakest-axis risk, the hard MQ threshold, a raw physical baseline, and raw plus H*.

04 · What survived

The combined representation carried strong transferable signal.

Across unseen batteries, the axis model ranked approaching degradation substantially better than chance and above the raw physical baseline.

The model was often able to rank which observations were closer to the defined degradation event, even though the batteries had not participated in development.

AUROC measures ranking discrimination across thresholds. The axis model reached 0.9394 versus 0.7850 for the raw physical baseline. That supports transferable domain-specific prediction, not mechanism by itself.

05 · Strongest rival explanation

Burden performed even better—and therefore became the central confound.

A strong result can narrow a claim as much as it strengthens one.

Burden may be capturing a meaningful accumulation process. It may also be repackaging ordinary battery age, use, or throughput. The current study cannot yet separate those explanations.

The decisive follow-up controls are cycle number, cumulative throughput, current capacity, recent degradation slope, burden ablation, and raw-plus-axes incremental comparison.

06 · What did not survive

The study produced failures, not automatic confirmation.

Negative findings are part of the result, not footnotes beneath it.

Some parts of the framework helped, some were only preliminary, and some failed in this realization. That is evidence the study could discriminate among claims rather than simply affirm the framework.

H* showed standalone discrimination but did not improve the raw model. Weakest-axis risk was above chance but insufficient for a law claim. The hard MQ threshold produced zero sensitivity.

07 · How this positions Diamond Gate Bridge

The study established a reusable empirical pathway—not a universal conclusion.

Future studies no longer have to invent the basic scientific machinery from scratch.

Diamond Gate Bridge can now show a complete journey from concept to a real held-out test: translation, measurement, prediction, comparison, failure, confound identification, and a bounded conclusion.

The reusable layer is the study architecture: domain translation, frozen hypotheses, unseen-system evaluation, baseline comparison, component tests, negative-result custody, rival-explanation analysis, and claim ceilings. Battery variables and battery conclusions do not automatically transfer.
