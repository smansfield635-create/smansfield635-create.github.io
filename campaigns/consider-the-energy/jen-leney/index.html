A career built around making service operational.

6 Stones describes Jen as bringing 20 years of executive leadership in nonprofit ministry together with a decade in corporate America. Across those environments, the recurring work has been practical: strengthen organizations, develop people, steward resources, and build systems that can serve neighbors with both excellence and compassion.

The work is not only to care. It is to organize care so that more people can carry it together.

The framing above is Diamond Gate Bridge’s appreciation of the documented career record; the underlying career facts are sourced below.

Finance, operations, development, and nonprofit leadership.

Corporate foundation

Jen spent a decade in corporate America, including work at Verizon. Her published professional profile connects that period to financial management, staff development, and operational efficiency.

Volunteers of America Texas

Before 6 Stones, Jen served as Vice President of Development for Volunteers of America Texas, adding fundraising and development leadership to an already broad operating background.

Christian Community Action

Jen also served as Chief Operating Officer and Development Director for Christian Community Action, where her work included programs serving vulnerable populations such as seniors, veterans, and people in recovery.

Education

Her published professional profile lists both a BBA in Finance and an MBA with honors from Texas Tech University’s Rawls College of Business.

Leading 6 Stones from community need toward community capacity.

The 6 Stones board selected Jennifer Leney as Executive Director in February 2019. Since then, the organization has continued expanding a model that mobilizes people and institutions around practical needs rather than expecting one organization to carry the entire burden alone.

900th CPR homeIn April 2025, 6 Stones completed work on the 900th home in the history of Community Powered Revitalization. That Spring Blitz mobilized 765 volunteers across 24 homes.
Home for HopeUnder Jen’s leadership, 6 Stones opened a larger Home for Hope facility designed to expand access to the New Hope Center, home repair, school initiatives, and other community programs.
Operation Back 2 School6 Stones describes one of its largest community efforts as equipping approximately 3,000 students with backpacks and school supplies while bringing partners into direct contact with the mission.
Catalyst of Hope6 Stones defines its role as helping individuals and families move from surviving toward thriving by transforming lives, homes, and neighborhoods through community mobilization.

The strongest overlap is not a program. It is a way of seeing community.

Jen’s work at 6 Stones is built around coalition: churches, businesses, cities, nonprofits, schools, volunteers, donors, and neighbors each carry different forms of capacity. The organization’s value is partly in making those capacities visible to one another and coordinating them around human need.

See the real condition.Start with what a household, student, neighborhood, or organization is actually facing—not what an isolated metric says should be true.
Mobilize the available capacity.Community changes what any one person, family, church, business, school, or city must carry alone.
Build toward durable agency.Relief matters most when it helps widen the path from crisis toward stability, participation, confidence, and a life with more room to move.

This is where 6 Stones and Diamond Gate Bridge’s Consider the Community work appear to share meaningful ground: both treat community as an active system of responsibility rather than a passive audience.
