## Material findings

1. Current stage is not a valid difficulty proxy. Difficulty inversions are substantial across Stages 3–8.
2. Stage 6 is the clearest inversion: its eight practical-comprehension items range provisionally from difficulty 2 to 5 despite being grouped as one higher stage.
3. Stage 5 contains difficulty-3 circumlocution beside difficulty-5 paraphrase. These test related competency families but not equivalent demand.
4. Stage 8 contains some difficulty-5/6 material alongside difficulty-8 material, so "integrated transfer" is not itself a difficulty level.
5. Instruction burden is frequently MATERIAL in higher items. The directive can therefore become an unintended part of the English test.
6. No item is rejected solely from this audit. The dominant need is recalibration: retain useful constructs, simplify directives, and move items onto an independent difficulty continuum.

## Instruction simplification law

Use the shortest natural English directive that preserves the measured construct.

Examples:
- "Choose the best meaning of ‘borrow.’" -> "What does ‘borrow’ mean?"
- "Which sentence correctly describes yesterday?" -> "Which is correct?"
- "Which sentence best paraphrases..." -> "Which means the same?"
- "What is the required action?" -> "What should you do?"

Native/help-language explanation remains fallback support rather than the default test language.

## Audit conclusion

The existing 64-item bank has enough raw material to continue development, but it is not yet a calibrated progressive placement bank.

Before runtime mutation:
1. calibrate each retained item to an independent difficulty stratum;
2. simplify MATERIAL instruction burden;
3. rebalance each difficulty stratum across relevant competencies;
4. freeze the calibrated bank;
5. simulate progressive traversal against the frozen matrix.
