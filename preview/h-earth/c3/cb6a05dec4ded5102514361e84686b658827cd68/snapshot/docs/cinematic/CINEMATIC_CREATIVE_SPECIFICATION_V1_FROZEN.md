# Global editorial-space grammar

Editorial space is cinematic language integrated with the image, not a subtitle or conventional caption layer.

## Editorial-space laws

1. **Immediate readability.** Spatial treatment may add depth, perspective, parallax, focus relationship, environmental integration, or bounded occlusion, but may never require deciphering.
2. **Sentence integrity.** Canonical statements remain coherent language objects. Emphasis may occur within them, but words do not become disconnected caption fragments.
3. **Camera independence.** A sentence must remain legible without a camera maneuver being required to reveal, assemble, or rescue it.
4. **World continuity.** Editorial events occur within or against a continuous world image unless the directing contract explicitly requires otherwise.
5. **Controlled depth.** Editorial depth supports meaning and integration. It does not become a demonstration of 3D text technology.
6. **Bounded occlusion.** Any occlusion must preserve immediate semantic comprehension and must serve integration rather than novelty.
7. **Focus discipline.** Focus may separate or reunite world and language, but never at the cost of readable canonical text.
8. **READ → LOOK is selective.** Attention may move from world to language and back to world where it serves comprehension. It is not a mechanical rhythm imposed on every line.
9. **Entry → inhabit → release.** Language enters for a semantic reason, inhabits the composition long enough to be experienced, then releases attention cleanly back to the world or next proposition.
10. **Audio owns timing.** Editorial-space timing is governed by the frozen master audio plus canonical script, not by camera-move completion.
