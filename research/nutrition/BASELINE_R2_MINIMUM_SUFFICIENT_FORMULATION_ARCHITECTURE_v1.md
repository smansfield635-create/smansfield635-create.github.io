**Date:** 2026-09-22

---

## 1. PURPOSE

Define the smallest useful set of R2 prototype architectures needed to determine whether an acid-mediated Baseline product can preserve the qualified mechanism while remaining low-burden, palatable, stable, and distinguishable from water.

These are formulation-development objects only.

They are **not recipes for self-experimentation or human consumption**.

No prototype receives a human-use dose authorization from this artifact.

---

## 2. DESIGN LAW

R2 is not:

**R1 + more ingredients**

R2 development follows:

> **minimum sufficient architecture = evidence-relevant mechanism + only the supporting components required to make that mechanism reproducible and usable**

Every retained ingredient must have one explicit role.

Ingredient-count inflation is prohibited.

---

## 3. RESEARCH EXPOSURE ENVELOPE

Published human vinegar/acetic-acid studies establish that the mechanism operates in a meal-proximal, meaningful acid-exposure range rather than trace exposure.

However, this artifact deliberately does not convert literature exposures into a consumer formulation dose.

Prototype development must first characterize:

- acid identity/source,
- assay,
- concentration,
- pH,
- titratable acidity,
- dilution behavior,
- sensory burden,
- material compatibility.

Only after those data exist can a specific candidate exposure be taken through qualified safety review.

---

# 4. ARCHITECTURE P0 — WATER CONTROL

## Composition concept

**Water only**

### Role

Negative/mechanistic comparator.

### Purpose

Establish:

- baseline taste,
- baseline pH,
- fluid-preload effects,
- container/process control.

### Calories

0

### Mechanism

Fluid preload only.

### Status

**CONTROL — RETAIN**

---

# 5. ARCHITECTURE P1 — ACID + WATER MINIMUM

## Composition concept

- water
- qualified acetic-acid source

Nothing else.

### Purpose

Determine the unmasked physicochemical and sensory behavior of the minimum acid-mediated system.

### Questions

- What pH results across the research exposure envelope?
- What is titratable acidity?
- Is the solution physically clear/stable?
- Is odor/taste inherently unacceptable?
- Does container contact alter the system?
- How severe is oral/throat sensory burden in non-ingestive bench/sensory-development work allowed by the development setting?

### Calories

Negligible/very low depending on source.

### Advantages

- cleanest mechanism,
- easiest attribution,
- lowest ingredient burden,
- simplest stability system.

### Risks

- harsh acidity,
- vinegar aroma,
- dental-contact concern,
- GI concern if ever advanced to human testing.

### Status

**PRIMARY BENCH PROTOTYPE**

---

# 6. ARCHITECTURE P2 — ACID + PALATABILITY SYSTEM

## Composition concept

- water
- qualified acetic-acid source
- minimum flavor/aroma system necessary to improve usability

No electrolytes or dextrose by default.

### Purpose

Determine whether the mechanism can be made sensorially acceptable without materially changing acid exposure.

### Palatability-system design constraints

The system must not:

- meaningfully neutralize the acid,
- add a large caloric load,
- obscure acid assay,
- create major GI burden,
- create a new active mechanism that confounds interpretation.

Potential formulation categories may include:

- noncaloric/very-low-calorie flavor systems,
- aroma masking,
- sweetness adjustment if scientifically/product-wise justified,
- serving-temperature optimization.

Specific additives require separate ingredient/safety qualification.

### Critical measurements

Compare P2 with P1:

- pH,
- titratable acidity,
- acid assay,
- physical stability,
- aroma,
- taste burden,
- aftertaste.

### Status

**PRIMARY PRODUCT-FIT PROTOTYPE**

---

# 7. ARCHITECTURE P3 — ACID + SELECTIVE R1 SUPPORT

## Composition concept

- water
- qualified acetic-acid source
- only an R1-derived component that independently earns retention

No R1 ingredient is automatically included.

### Candidate retention questions

#### NaCl

Retain only if bench/product analysis establishes a specific role such as:

- palatability at a low justified level,
- formulation function,
- future evidence-supported hydration role.

Do not retain merely because R1 contains it.

#### KCl

Retain only if it solves a defined formulation/mechanism problem with acceptable taste/safety burden.

#### Magnesium citrate

Current presumption:

**DO NOT RETAIN**

No earned acid-pathway role and unresolved R1 material identity.

#### Dextrose

Current presumption:

**DO NOT RETAIN**

A glycemic-transition product should not add simple carbohydrate without a specific earned reason.

### Purpose

Test whether a minimal support component improves usability or formulation performance enough to justify added complexity.

### Status

**SECONDARY PROTOTYPE — ONLY AFTER P1/P2 DATA**

---

# 8. ARCHITECTURE P4 — FULL R1 + ACID

## Composition concept

Frozen R1 plus acid.

### Purpose

Reference architecture only.

### Scientific problem

It adds:

- NaCl,
- KCl,
- magnesium citrate,
- dextrose,

without evidence that all four are needed for the acid mechanism.

It also makes causal attribution substantially worse.

### Status

**DO NOT PRIORITIZE**

Construct only if later evidence creates a specific reason.

This architecture is not the default R2.

## 10. BENCH CHARACTERIZATION PANEL

For every acid-containing prototype record:

- exact acid source,
- supplier/specification,
- assay,
- preparation ID,
- water source,
- acid concentration,
- pH,
- titratable acidity,
- appearance,
- clarity/turbidity,
- odor observations,
- container,
- preparation temperature,
- storage time,
- repeat measurement after defined storage interval.

Where relevant later:

- acid assay after storage,
- precipitation,
- package/closure effects,
- microbial/stability requirements.

No human efficacy inference is made from bench chemistry.

---

## 11. DENTAL-EXPOSURE DEVELOPMENT PANEL

Before human-use formulation review, characterize:

- pH,
- titratable acidity,
- intended contact time,
- serving format,
- drinking duration,
- likely tooth-contact pattern.

Candidate mitigation concepts should be reviewed with appropriate dental expertise before being converted into consumer instructions.

A prototype that requires prolonged sipping fails the Baseline use model.

---

## 12. GI / ESOPHAGEAL DEVELOPMENT PANEL

Before human testing, the selected prototype requires a qualified review of:

- acid exposure,
- dilution,
- reflux/esophageal risk,
- upper-GI intolerance,
- exclusion criteria,
- stopping rules.

No bench prototype is labeled “gentle” or “GI safe” without human evidence.

---

## 13. PRODUCT-FIT REQUIREMENTS

A prototype fails Baseline fit if it becomes:

- a large-volume beverage,
- a thick shake,
- a high-calorie preload,
- a product requiring prolonged consumption,
- a multi-step preparation burden,
- a strongly aversive acid shot,
- a kitchen-sink supplement.

The target experience remains:

**small intervention → short controlled transition → normal meal**

---

## 14. INGREDIENT RETENTION LAW

For every non-water/non-acid ingredient proposed later, complete:

| Question | Required answer |
|---|---|
| What exact problem does it solve? | explicit |
| Is that problem demonstrated in P1/P2? | yes |
| Does it preserve acid exposure? | yes |
| Does it add a new mechanism/confound? | bounded |
| Does it materially increase calories? | quantified |
| Does it increase GI burden? | assessed |
| Does it increase safety burden? | assessed |
| Can its effect be measured? | yes |

If these cannot be answered, do not add the ingredient.

---

## 15. PROTOTYPE ADVANCEMENT GATES

### P1 advances if:

- chemistry is reproducible,
- exposure can be characterized,
- no immediate material/packaging incompatibility makes the concept impractical.

### P2 advances if:

- palatability system materially improves usability,
- acid exposure remains materially intact,
- calories/burden remain low,
- stability remains tractable.

### P3 is opened only if:

P1/P2 reveal a defined problem that one R1-derived component can plausibly solve.

### P4 remains on hold unless:

Evidence demonstrates a reason to preserve the full R1 stack.

---

## 16. KILL CRITERIA

Drop the acid-mediated architecture if:

- evidence-relevant acidity cannot be delivered without an unacceptable sensory burden,
- dental-exposure mitigation is not credible,
- GI/esophageal risk cannot be bounded,
- palatability requires mechanism-destroying neutralization,
- stable/reproducible formulation is impractical,
- serving burden ceases to be minimalist,
- a distinct mechanism beyond water cannot be maintained.

---

## 17. CURRENT DEVELOPMENT RESULT

The minimum sufficient R2 program is now:

**P0 — Water control**  
**P1 — Acid + water**  
**P2 — Acid + minimal palatability system**

P3 is conditional.

P4 is held.

This is materially simpler than inheriting R1.

---

## 18. R1 / R2 RELATIONSHIP

R1 remains:

**electrolyte/dextrose minimalist preload candidate**

R2 development is now:

**acid-mediated minimalist transition candidate**

They are separate scientific objects.

A future empirical program may show:

- R1 beats water,
- R2 beats water,
- both,
- neither.

The architecture does not assume the answer.
