Reality

  1. Claim

    Practical
    Reality begins when a claim can be tested against something outside the claimant. The first question is not whether the claim sounds persuasive. The first question is whether it can be made answerable. Separate claim from appearance.
    Engineering
    Reality is the classification state assigned after a claim survives constraint testing. Define the claim.
    Empirical
    For Reality requires a claim that can be inspected, bounded, measured, and challenged. Strong evidence: testable claim, recordable condition, traceable review, defined outcome state.

  2. Condition

    Practical
    Reality begins when a claim can be tested against something outside the claimant. The first question is not whether the claim sounds persuasive. The first question is whether it can be made answerable. Separate confidence from condition.
    Engineering
    Reality is the classification state assigned after a claim survives constraint testing. Expose the claim to evidence.
    Empirical
    For Reality requires a claim that can be inspected, bounded, measured, and challenged. Useful evidence: claim inventory, review criteria, challenge record, disposition receipt.

  3. Test

    Practical
    Reality begins when a claim can be tested against something outside the claimant. The first question is not whether the claim sounds persuasive. The first question is whether it can be made answerable. Separate preference from tested result.
    Engineering
    Reality is the classification state assigned after a claim survives constraint testing. Declare measurement and limit conditions.
    Empirical
    For Reality requires a claim that can be inspected, bounded, measured, and challenged. Failure mode: the claim survives only as private certainty.

  4. Disposition

    Practical
    Reality begins when a claim can be tested against something outside the claimant. The first question is not whether the claim sounds persuasive. The first question is whether it can be made answerable. Do not let presentation outrank constraint.
    Engineering
    Reality is the classification state assigned after a claim survives constraint testing. Assign a disposition state: proceed, revise, reject, hold, retest, recalibrate, or escalate.
    Empirical
    For Reality requires a claim that can be inspected, bounded, measured, and challenged. Boundary: a claim is not Reality because it is strongly believed. Claim boundary: A claim is not Reality because it is strongly believed.

02 / 06

Theory

  1. Model

    Practical
    A theory helps people move through complexity, but it becomes dangerous when the map is defended after the terrain proves it wrong. Theory earns trust when it predicts what happens next.
    Engineering
    Theory is a constrained explanatory model. It must declare assumptions, define variables, generate testable predictions, expose falsification conditions, and revise or fail when evidence contradicts the model. Model assumptions must be explicit.
    Empirical
    For Theory requires more than explanation. The theory must risk mismatch with the world. Strong evidence: successful prediction, contradiction handling, revised model history, explicit assumptions.

    Continue to Theory

  2. Assumption

    Practical
    A theory helps people move through complexity, but it becomes dangerous when the map is defended after the terrain proves it wrong. Theory loses trust when it needs excuses to stay right.
    Engineering
    Theory is a constrained explanatory model. It must declare assumptions, define variables, generate testable predictions, expose falsification conditions, and revise or fail when evidence contradicts the model. Predictions must be observable.
    Empirical
    For Theory requires more than explanation. The theory must risk mismatch with the world. Useful evidence: model diagrams, variable definitions, prediction logs, failed-case records.

    Continue to Theory

  3. Prediction

    Practical
    A theory helps people move through complexity, but it becomes dangerous when the map is defended after the terrain proves it wrong. The model must not pretend to be the world.
    Engineering
    Theory is a constrained explanatory model. It must declare assumptions, define variables, generate testable predictions, expose falsification conditions, and revise or fail when evidence contradicts the model. Falsification conditions must exist.
    Empirical
    For Theory requires more than explanation. The theory must risk mismatch with the world. Failure mode: the model explains everything after the fact but predicts nothing before the test.

    Continue to Theory

  4. Revision

    Practical
    A theory helps people move through complexity, but it becomes dangerous when the map is defended after the terrain proves it wrong. The lawful move after contradiction is revision, not defense.
    Engineering
    Theory is a constrained explanatory model. It must declare assumptions, define variables, generate testable predictions, expose falsification conditions, and revise or fail when evidence contradicts the model. Contradiction must trigger revision, rejection, or hold-state classification.
    Empirical
    For Theory requires more than explanation. The theory must risk mismatch with the world. Boundary: theory may guide inspection, but it does not outrank Reality. Claim boundary: Theory may guide inspection, but it does not outrank Reality.

Evidence

  1. Observation

    Practical
    Moves a claim out of private insistence. It gives others something to inspect instead of asking them to trust the loudest voice in the room. Good evidence is observable.
    Engineering
    Requires observable data, provenance, collection method, sampling context, source reliability, bias exposure, and independent checkability. Sampling method must be named.
    Empirical
    For Evidence sounds redundant, but it is the core discipline: the evidence itself must carry enough information to be checked. Strong evidence: raw record, metadata, provenance, collection method, independent check path.

    Continue to Evidence

  2. Record

    Practical
    Moves a claim out of private insistence. It gives others something to inspect instead of asking them to trust the loudest voice in the room. Good evidence is recorded.
    Engineering
    Requires observable data, provenance, collection method, sampling context, source reliability, bias exposure, and independent checkability. Measurement method must be named.
    Empirical
    For Evidence sounds redundant, but it is the core discipline: the evidence itself must carry enough information to be checked. Useful evidence: logs, observations, chain-of-custody notes, replication posture.

    Continue to Evidence

  3. Provenance

    Engineering

    Empirical

    Continue to Evidence

    Practical

Measure

    Engineering

    Empirical
    For Measure requires proof that different qualified observers can classify the same condition under the same measurement contract. Strong evidence: unit definitions, calibration record, sampling record, uncertainty range, observer agreement.

Limits

  1. Boundary

    Practical
    Limits are where honest certainty stops. Naming limits does not weaken a claim; it protects the claim from pretending to know more than it knows. Limits prevent false certainty.
    Engineering
    Limits define uncertainty, applicability boundaries, model mismatch, sampling weakness, unknown variables, unresolved contradiction, and hold-state conditions. Error bounds define claim strength.
    Empirical
    For Limits requires named uncertainty and visible boundaries around the claim. Strong evidence: uncertainty ranges, boundary conditions, sampling limits, unresolved contradiction records.

Audit

  1. Proceed

    Practical
    Audit is the final pressure test. What holds may proceed. What fails must be revised. What remains unknown must be named. Separate established condition from open question.
    Engineering
    Audit determines claim disposition: proceed, revise, reject, hold, retest, recalibrate, or escalate. If theory fails, revise the model.
    Empirical
    For Audit requires a traceable conclusion path. Strong evidence: audit trail, disposition receipt, contradiction handling, evidence review, limit declaration.

  2. Revise

    Practical
    Audit is the final pressure test. What holds may proceed. What fails must be revised. What remains unknown must be named. Separate unstable claim from false claim.
    Engineering
    Audit determines claim disposition: proceed, revise, reject, hold, retest, recalibrate, or escalate. If evidence fails, hold or reject the claim.
    Empirical
    For Audit requires a traceable conclusion path. Useful evidence: review notes, retest records, escalation logs, revision records.

  3. Reject

    Practical
    Audit is the final pressure test. What holds may proceed. What fails must be revised. What remains unknown must be named. Separate audit-ready claim from unreviewed claim.
    Engineering
    Audit determines claim disposition: proceed, revise, reject, hold, retest, recalibrate, or escalate. If measurement fails, recalibrate before proceeding.
    Empirical
    For Audit requires a traceable conclusion path. Failure mode: the claim proceeds without review because it sounds complete.

  4. Hold

    Practical
    Audit is the final pressure test. What holds may proceed. What fails must be revised. What remains unknown must be named. Do not let certainty theater replace conclusion state.
    Engineering
    Audit determines claim disposition: proceed, revise, reject, hold, retest, recalibrate, or escalate. If limits are missing, name the uncertainty before proceeding.
    Empirical
    For Audit requires a traceable conclusion path. Boundary: audit is not hostility; it is the lawful closure of a claim. Claim boundary: Audit remains an inspection subject on this page; no separate Audit route is invented.
