Plain-language reading

Consistency is doing the same right thing for the same reason across comparable contexts without hiding meaningful variance or switching the standard when pressure changes.

The rule that remains active.

Context

Controls

Define invariant rules, tolerated variation, case separation, normalization, context differences, and sources of drift before comparing cases.

Failure behavior

Consistency fails when exceptions are hidden, comparison conditions change silently, or a standard changes with audience or incentive.

Reading layers

Practical, Engineering, and Empirical claims remain separate.

Practical reading

Explain why dependable behavior across cases matters to the real system.

Engineering reading

Define invariant rules, tolerated variation, case separation, normalization, and sources of drift.

Empirical reading

Show held-out performance, cross-case variation, outliers, and whether the relationship survives outside development cases.

Method

Compare cases under declared common rules, retain outliers, and separate ordinary variability from drift or rule changes.

Audit boundary

Fewer explanations, fewer exceptions, bounded variance, and stable observer classification are signatures of consistency; they do not establish transfer beyond the tested contexts.
