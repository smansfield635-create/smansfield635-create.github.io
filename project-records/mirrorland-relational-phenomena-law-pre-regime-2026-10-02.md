This record defines the independent relational/conversational phenomena that require state.

It does **not** define visitor-facing gauges, named relationship levels, or the renewed matrix.

## 1. Governing correction

Relationship is not one number and not one ladder.

The old `PUBLIC -> ENGAGED -> GUARDED` machine combines distinct phenomena that can coexist or move independently.

A believable interaction must permit conditions such as:

- strong conversational engagement while a specific subject remains protected;
- substantial familiarity without current conversational momentum;
- a local caution increase without global relational collapse;
- good continuity after a boundary was respected;
- recovery from pressure without erasing that pressure occurred;
- a new visitor having a productive conversation before any deeper familiarity exists.

Therefore no single phase may serve as the source of truth for all character behavior.

## 2. State only what has future consequence

A relational phenomenon deserves durable session state only when remembering it can materially change a later turn.

Do not create state merely because something can be measured.

The minimum successor model requires five distinct phenomena:

1. **Familiarity / accumulated history**
2. **Conversational momentum**
3. **Disclosure disposition**
4. **Subject-specific caution**
5. **Boundary recovery / repair**

These are internal cognition/context signals, not visitor scores.

## 3. Familiarity / accumulated history

### Meaning

How much meaningful interaction has occurred between this character and this visitor within the authorized memory boundary.

This is not approval.

It represents accumulated shared conversational history.

Evidence may include:

- substantive topics discussed;
- prior handoffs;
- prior returns;
- remembered questions;
- offers made;
- boundaries previously established;
- unresolved/resolved threads;
- prior assistance or participation in an encounter.

### Properties

- generally accumulative within the active authorized session/context;
- does not fall merely because the visitor changes subject;
- does not imply unrestricted disclosure;
- does not override privacy/authority;
- may allow the character to skip redundant orientation;
- may permit callbacks, shorthand, humor, or more personally situated explanation.

### Non-example

A visitor asking many shallow questions should not mechanically maximize familiarity.

The state represents meaningful shared history, not click count.

## 4. Conversational momentum

### Meaning

Whether the current interaction has an active, coherent conversational thread that is naturally developing.

This is closer to **flow** than trust.

Momentum can arise from:

- a sequence of related questions;
- following a character-introduced thread;
- a meaningful follow-up;
- an active in-world situation;
- returning to an unresolved subject;
- a natural handoff opportunity.

Momentum can decline through:

- abrupt unrelated pivots;
- exhausted topic;
- repeated loops;
- ignored offered context;
- a completed thread.

### Properties

- short-horizon and context-sensitive;
- may rise quickly even with a new visitor;
- may be high while familiarity is still low;
- may be low despite high familiarity;
- should influence continuation, initiative, and handoff timing more than disclosure authority.

### Consequence

High momentum may make the character more likely to:

- volunteer a relevant connection;
- continue without returning to a menu;
- ask a natural clarifying question;
- introduce a related character/place;
- resume an unresolved thread.

It does not itself authorize protected information.

## 5. Disclosure disposition

### Meaning

Within information the character **already knows and is authorized to disclose**, how readily is the character currently inclined to volunteer or elaborate?

This is not disclosure authority.

Authority is a hard ceiling from the information/disclosure law.

Disposition operates only beneath that ceiling.

### Properties

Disclosure disposition may vary by:

- relationship history;
- subject;
- character disposition;
- conversational context;
- prior respect for boundaries;
- active situation.

It may affect:

- amount of voluntary detail;
- whether the character offers a related story;
- whether the character answers narrowly or expansively;
- whether a deeper but still authorized subject is volunteered.

It may not:

- reveal protected information;
- reveal information the character does not know;
- replace another character's self-authority;
- convert a handoff-owned story into the speaker's story.

### Scope

The architecture should permit both:

- a general conversational disclosure tendency; and
- subject-specific availability where necessary.

Do not assume one global scalar is sufficient.

## 6. Subject-specific caution

### Meaning

A local state indicating that a particular subject/domain requires more careful handling in the current interaction.

Caution can exist because:

- a boundary was discovered;
- a boundary was pressured;
- the topic concerns protected people;
- the topic touches information owned by another character;
- the active situation makes disclosure unusually sensitive.

### Properties

- keyed to subject/domain where possible;
- does not imply global guardedness;
- can coexist with high momentum on another subject;
- can coexist with high familiarity;
- can remain high permanently for information whose authority never changes;
- may affect phrasing, amount of detail, redirection, or willingness to continue that thread.

### Important distinction

Some caution comes from **world/authority conditions**, not visitor behavior.

Auren is careful with protected residents because protection is part of his role, even if the visitor has behaved perfectly.

Do not interpret all caution as negative relationship evidence.

## 7. Boundary recovery / repair

### Meaning

The interaction's ability to normalize after a boundary has been pressured.

Recovery is not a reward state and not deletion of history.

It answers whether the prior pressure is still materially affecting current behavior.

### Recovery evidence may include

- visitor accepts the boundary;
- visitor asks for permissible context;
- visitor changes to a lawful subject;
- visitor follows an offered handoff;
- subsequent interaction demonstrates that the protected line is no longer being pressed.

### Properties

- may reduce visitor-caused caution over time/context;
- does not remove the underlying authority boundary;
- does not erase that pressure occurred from bounded session history;
- may be subject-local before becoming broader;
- should permit the conversation to become natural again.

### No ritual apology requirement

The visitor must not be forced through a scripted apology or redemption sequence.

Normal conversational behavior can repair normal conversational pressure.

## 8. Boundary state decomposition

A protected subject should not be represented simply as `GUARDED`.

At minimum, cognition should be able to distinguish:

- **authority sensitivity** — the subject is inherently bounded;
- **boundary known** — the visitor has been told the limit;
- **visitor pressure active/recent** — the visitor pressed beyond a known limit;
- **repair underway/established** — subsequent behavior has normalized the interaction.

This prevents the runtime from confusing a private subject with a problematic visitor.

## 9. Relationship and conversation are overlapping, not identical

The successor architecture distinguishes:

### Relational memory

What has accumulated between character and visitor.

### Conversational condition

What is happening in the active thread right now.

A familiar visitor can start a cold/new topic.

A new visitor can immediately create strong conversational momentum.

A visitor can have excellent momentum on one subject while encountering a hard privacy boundary on another.

The system must not flatten these into one phase.

## 10. Nonlinear interaction examples

These are architecture examples, not a matrix.

### New visitor, strong curiosity

- familiarity: low
- momentum: rising/high
- disclosure disposition: normal or expanding within authority
- subject caution: low

Result: lively conversation is possible immediately.

### Familiar visitor asks about protected residents

- familiarity: high
- momentum: high
- disclosure disposition: high on lawful subjects
- protected-resident caution: inherently high

Result: Auren remains warm/familiar while keeping names private.

### Visitor presses a known protected boundary

- familiarity: whatever history exists
- momentum: may stall locally
- disclosure disposition: may narrow near that subject
- subject caution: increased
- pressure: active/recent

Result: firmer response without global conversational shutdown.

### Visitor redirects normally after pressure

- familiarity: retained
- momentum: can rebuild
- protected subject caution: still authoritative
- visitor-caused caution: decays/repairs

Result: conversation can normalize without pretending the boundary vanished.

### Familiar visitor returns after a handoff

- familiarity/history: recognizes prior journey
- momentum: can resume originating thread
- disclosure authority: unchanged
- character perspective: preserved

Result: continuity without cross-character omniscience.

## 11. What should not be durable relationship state

Do not create persistent state for every:

- question asked;
- option clicked;
- topic word;
- archetype observation;
- emotional adjective;
- momentary pause.

Those may be event/history data where useful, but the relational model should store only what has future behavioral consequence.

Archetype evidence remains a separate diagnostic/cognition concern and must not be treated as relationship state.

## 12. Character-specific weighting

Mirrorland-wide phenomena are shared architecture.

Their importance and behavioral expression are character-specific.

For Auren:

- boundaries, custody, responsibility, and protection are highly salient;
- subject caution may therefore affect his behavior strongly;
- familiarity may show through practical candor, callbacks, responsibility-sharing, or dry confidence rather than generic friendliness;
- momentum may cause him to connect curiosity to places/people/responsibilities he considers relevant.

Another character may express the same underlying phenomena differently.

Therefore the relational state model must not prescribe universal dialogue tone.

## 13. Handoff eligibility consequence

A handoff must not require a single `ENGAGED` phase.

Handoff relevance should be resolved from factors such as:

- topic relevance;
- conversational momentum;
- disclosure/self-authority boundary;
- character purpose;
- unresolved thread;
- destination validity;
- prior offer/visit history.

Familiarity or disclosure disposition may influence how the handoff is offered, but should not automatically be mandatory gates.

## 14. State-dependent delivery consequence

The old direct mapping:

`PUBLIC -> PUBLIC_MASK`
`ENGAGED -> OPEN_MASK`
`GUARDED -> PROTECTIVE_MASK`

is superseded as a target architecture.

Successor selection should not choose an entire dialogue personality band from one relationship phase.

Instead, relational phenomena may influence bounded aspects such as:

- whether Auren volunteers additional authorized context;
- whether he recognizes prior history;
- whether he resumes a thread;
- whether a local boundary is stated gently or firmly;
- whether a relevant handoff is introduced;
- whether a previously delivered explanation is abbreviated/deepened.

Auren's canonical personality remains the delivery source.

## 15. No derived regimes yet

This record does not name four levels.

It does not rename `PUBLIC`, `ENGAGED`, or `GUARDED`.

It does not establish a new finite-state machine.

A later architecture operation may determine that a small set of **derived conversational regimes** is useful for authoring, verification, or presentation.

If so, those regimes must be derived from the underlying phenomena and must not replace them as source-of-truth state.

## 16. Gauge consequences

A visitor-facing gauge is still unresolved.

This record establishes an important constraint:

No single gauge labeled as relationship/trust/progress can faithfully represent the phenomena above.

If any visible conversational indicator is eventually authorized, it must have a clearly defined visitor utility and must not imply:

- one-dimensional progress;
- that curiosity earns points;
- that protected information unlocks at a high score;
- that caution always means the visitor did something wrong;
- that conversation has a terminal completion state.

Presentation design remains blocked.

## 17. Minimum successor state semantics

Without choosing implementation representation, the successor runtime must eventually be capable of answering:

- What meaningful shared history exists?
- What thread is active?
- Is the current conversation developing or exhausted?
- What authorized information is Auren currently inclined to elaborate on?
- Which subjects require caution and why?
- Has a boundary on this subject been communicated?
- Was that known boundary pressured?
- Is the effect of that pressure still active?
- Has normal interaction resumed?
- What unresolved thread/handoff context should be resumed?

This may be implemented through events plus derived state rather than mutable numeric gauges.

## 18. Relationship-event principle

Prefer semantically meaningful events over arbitrary score increments.

Examples:

- topic meaningfully pursued;
- character volunteered deeper authorized context;
- boundary established;
- boundary acknowledged;
- boundary pressured;
- subject redirected;
- handoff offered;
- handoff returned;
- unresolved thread resumed.

The final implementation may derive phenomena from bounded events.

Do not begin with `+1 trust`, `-1 trust`, or equivalent hidden arithmetic unless later evidence proves a specific need.
