Theory and sources

The map must face the terrain

Theory is a constrained explanatory model. Assumptions must be explicit, predictions must be observable, falsification conditions must exist, and contradiction must trigger revision, rejection, or a hold state.

Evidence precedence

Explanation may organize evidence, but it cannot replace observable, recorded, provenance-bearing evidence.

Source control

Sampling context, collection method, source reliability, bias exposure, and independent checkability remain part of the record.

Checkpoint correspondence

  • Reality: define claim, condition, test, and disposition.
  • Theory: declare model, assumptions, predictions, and revision conditions.
  • Evidence: preserve observation, record, provenance, and checkability.
  • Measure: define unit, calibration, sampling frame, and uncertainty.
  • Limits: name boundary, unknowns, error, and applicability.
  • Audit: assign proceed, revise, reject, hold, retest, recalibrate, or escalate.

Reading layers

Practical, Engineering, and Empirical readings

Practical reading

State what was observed and what decision the result might eventually inform.

Engineering reading

Expose sample identity, target horizon, metrics, comparators, missing controls, and validation state.

Empirical reading

Preserve all measured results, including the stronger burden comparator and negative or mixed findings.

Procedure

Measurement is a shared contract

Measurement requires defined units, calibration, sampling frame, error bounds, uncertainty range, coordinate system, and classification rule. The result should not change merely because a different observer wants a different answer.

Required controls

Sample identity, target horizon, comparator identity, threshold behavior, missing controls, and validation state remain visible.

Failure behavior

If measurement fails, recalibrate before proceeding. If evidence cannot be checked, hold or reject rather than upgrade the claim.

Audit method

Audit is the final pressure test: what holds may proceed, what fails must be revised, and what remains unknown must be named. A traceable conclusion path requires evidence review, contradiction handling, limitation declaration, and a disposition receipt.

Claim boundary

Where this family interpretation stops

The held-out result is domain-specific support, not proof of cause, deployment fitness, or universal law.

Limits and claim ceiling

Limits are where honest certainty stops. They include uncertainty, applicability boundaries, model mismatch, sampling weakness, unknown variables, unresolved contradiction, and explicit hold-state conditions.

Failure pattern

Partial truth is presented as total certainty, or a claim proceeds because it sounds complete while theory, evidence, measurement, limits, or audit remain unresolved.

Law-family boundary

Only materially relevant children are shown. The complete study remains in Frontier, and no Laws relationship upgrades its evidence status.

Disposition and preserved receipt boundary

  • If theory fails, revise the model.
  • If evidence fails, hold or reject the claim.
  • If measurement fails, recalibrate before proceeding.
  • If limits are missing, name uncertainty before proceeding.
