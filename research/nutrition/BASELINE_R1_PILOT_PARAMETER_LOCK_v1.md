## 2. EVIDENCE BASIS FOR TIMING CHOICES

Published preload studies commonly use randomized/crossover designs and controlled pre-meal intervals. Water-preload work has used approximately 375–500 mL 30 minutes before a meal, while other controlled preload studies have used 330–500 mL and 30-minute intervals. Immediate pre-meal water can also affect appetite/intake, showing that timing itself is an experimental variable.

Accordingly, this pilot locks a **30-minute preload-to-meal interval**. The purpose is not to claim that 30 minutes is uniquely optimal; it is to create a literature-consistent, reproducible window in which R1 can be compared with the identical water volume.

## 3. CORE TEST STATE — LOCKED

### Food-free interval

**10 hours minimum, overnight.**

Permitted range for an analyzable session:

**10–14 hours since last caloric intake.**

Rationale:

- produces a defined fasted-to-meal transition,
- avoids requiring an unusually prolonged fast,
- supports morning standardization,
- reduces uncontrolled intervening food exposure.

The paired visits for a participant should match fasting duration as closely as practical.

Difference between paired-session fasting durations:

**target ≤ 60 minutes.**

A difference >60 minutes is a protocol deviation requiring adjudication.

---

## 4. PRE-SESSION WATER POLICY — LOCKED

From the start of the food-free interval until **2 hours before baseline**:

- plain water permitted,
- no caloric beverages,
- no electrolyte beverages.

During the final **2 hours before baseline**:

- no discretionary fluid intake except a small amount needed for essential medication if permitted by the safety protocol.

All water consumed during the controlled interval must be recorded.

This rule is intended to reduce immediate pre-baseline gastric-volume confounding while avoiding a deliberate dehydration challenge.

Participants must not intentionally restrict water during the preceding day to amplify the intervention.

---

## 5. CAFFEINE / NICOTINE / ALCOHOL / EXERCISE — LOCKED

### Caffeine

No caffeine during the food-free interval and before completion of the primary meal observation.

### Nicotine

No nicotine during the food-free interval and before completion of the primary meal observation.

Where abstention would be inappropriate or unsafe for a candidate, that candidate should not be enrolled under this pilot specification without qualified review.

### Alcohol

No alcohol for **24 hours before baseline**.

### Exercise

No vigorous exercise for **12 hours before baseline**.

Normal light daily activity is permitted but should be comparable across paired visits.

## 6. TIME-OF-DAY CONTROL — LOCKED

Testing occurs in the **morning after the overnight food-free interval**.

For each participant, paired baseline start times should be within:

**±60 minutes.**

## 11. STANDARDIZED MEAL — LOCKED DESIGN

The meal must be a single standardized breakfast supplied identically at both sessions.

For the pilot, use one pre-portioned mixed breakfast with:

- carbohydrate,
- protein,
- fat,
- moderate rather than deliberately extreme sodium,
- no unusually high fiber load,
- no spicy challenge,
- no caffeine,
- no alcohol.

### Portioning rule

The meal must provide enough food that participants can stop voluntarily before consuming the full portion.

The exact recipe, ingredients, nutrition calculation, allergen profile, batch/brand identifiers, and offered mass must be frozen in a separate meal specification before participant 001.

### Intake rule

Meal is **ad libitum from the standardized offered portion**.

Participants receive the neutral instruction to eat until comfortably satisfied.

They are not instructed to eat less, eat more, or test the intervention.

### Meal observation window

Maximum:

**30 minutes.**

Participants may stop earlier.

## 18. PRACTICAL-MEANINGFULNESS RULE — PILOT LOCK

This is an exploratory pilot. It is not powered to establish a definitive clinical minimum important difference.

Therefore:

**No single arbitrary numerical cutoff will be treated as proof of efficacy.**

A signal requires convergence across the prespecified domains rather than a lone favorable measurement.

### Signal architecture

An R1 advancement signal requires:

1. acceptable safety/tolerability,
2. no major systematic protocol asymmetry favoring R1,
3. a consistent within-participant directional advantage in at least one primary transition domain,
4. no coherent worsening across other primary domains,
5. an effect magnitude large enough to justify a powered follow-up rather than being attributable solely to trivial measurement noise.

Pilot results will be reported with raw paired differences, distribution, central tendency, and uncertainty.

The observed variance/effect distribution—not a post hoc efficacy declaration—will inform any later confirmatory threshold and sample-size calculation.

## 19. SESSION EXCLUSION / SENSITIVITY RULES — LOCKED

### Exclude from primary paired analysis if:

- wrong condition administered,
- <95% intervention volume consumed,
- standardized meal materially differs between paired sessions,
- food-free interval <10 hours,
- prohibited caloric intake occurred,
- critical outcome/meal-intake data are absent,
- safety event terminates the session before meaningful primary measurement.

### Sensitivity-only / adjudication candidate:

- fasting interval >14 hours,
- paired fasting-duration mismatch >60 minutes,
- paired baseline-time mismatch >60 minutes,
- preload-to-meal timing outside 30 ± 2 minutes,
- T3/T4 outside timing window,
- exercise/caffeine/nicotine/water-policy deviation,
- session >14 days from paired visit.

All classifications are frozen before X/Y is unmasked.
