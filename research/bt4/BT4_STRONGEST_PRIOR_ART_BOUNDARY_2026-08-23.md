# BT4 Strongest Prior-Art Boundary — 2026-08-23

Status: `BT4_STRONGEST_PRIOR_ART_BOUNDARY_CLOSED`

Purpose: test the strongest surviving BT4 proposition against older and contemporary systems before constructing any new Diamond Gate mechanism.

## Surviving proposition under test

The strongest remaining BT4 formulation before this boundary was:

`evidence degradation -> epistemic-state degradation -> mandatory public-claim contraction -> evidence-bearing requalification`

with the public representation itself compelled to follow the governed evidentiary state.

## Prior-art result 1 — automatic belief/claim contraction is old prior art

Jon Doyle's 1979 Truth Maintenance System (TMS) explicitly records reasons for program beliefs and revises the current set of beliefs when discoveries contradict assumptions. Dependency-directed backtracking changes assumptions and their dependent beliefs. This is direct prior art for the abstract computational mechanism:

`support/justification changes -> dependent belief status changes`

Source: Jon Doyle, "A truth maintenance system," Artificial Intelligence 12(3), 1979, DOI 10.1016/0004-3702(79)90008-0.

Disposition:

`AUTOMATIC_SUPPORT_LOSS_TO_BELIEF_RETRACTION_NOVELTY = REJECTED`

Diamond Gate cannot claim novelty merely for maintaining claim dependencies and retracting/downgrading a claim when its justifications fail.

## Prior-art result 2 — evidence change causing guidance/recommendation change is established

Living systematic reviews continuously incorporate new evidence and revise findings accordingly. Living guidelines explicitly define their unit of update as the individual recommendation and are designed so recommendations can change as soon as relevant new evidence becomes available.

Sources:

- Elliott et al., "Living systematic review: 1. Introduction—the why, what, when, and how," Journal of Clinical Epidemiology 91 (2017), 23–30.
- Akl et al., "Living systematic reviews: 4. Living guideline recommendations," Journal of Clinical Epidemiology 91 (2017), 47–53, PMID 28911999.

Disposition:

`EVIDENCE_CHANGE_TO_RECOMMENDATION_CHANGE_NOVELTY = REJECTED`

This prior art is usually human- or workflow-mediated rather than a fully autonomous public entitlement engine, but it occupies the scientific-governance semantics of evidence-driven revision.

## Prior-art result 3 — contemporary evidence graphs now govern retained claims and downstream research outputs

EviGraph (Ren et al., August 2026) represents the research process as an operational typed evidence graph containing Problem, Gap, Hypothesis, Experiment, Finding, and Claim nodes. It checks evidence chains for missing dependencies and result-claim inconsistencies, localizes the earliest weak node, regenerates the affected downstream subgraph, protects validated checkpoints, and generates manuscripts only after retained claims are grounded in validated evidence chains.

Source: Ren et al., "EviGraph: Evidence-Guided Autonomous Research Agents," arXiv:2608.04738, 2026-08-05.

Disposition:

`OPERATIONAL_CLAIM_EVIDENCE_GRAPH_WITH_DOWNSTREAM_REGENERATION_NOVELTY = REJECTED`

This is especially strong contemporary prior art against any BT4 formulation based only on an operational claim/evidence graph whose downstream scientific output is regenerated when support becomes invalid.

## Prior-art result 4 — Stencila remains provenance/relationship prior art but does not close the final public-entitlement route

Current Stencila explicitly represents Supports / SupportedBy / Opposes / OpposedBy relationships among research objects and can verify whether signed assets still match their provenance. However, Stencila's own documentation explicitly states that Content Credentials do not determine whether a scientific claim is correct, validate the analysis, or replace peer review.

Sources:

- https://stencila.io/docs/schema/research-object-relation-kind/
- https://stencila.io/docs/content-credentials/

Disposition:

`STENCILA_PUBLIC_SCIENTIFIC_ENTITLEMENT_ENGINE = NOT_FOUND`

## What survives after this attack

The primitive and intermediate mechanisms no longer support a novelty claim:

- automatic belief retraction from failed justification — old TMS prior art;
- evidence-driven revision of scientific findings/recommendations — living evidence/guideline prior art;
- operational claim/evidence graph with downstream repair/regeneration — EviGraph prior art;
- provenance, signed assets, executable research objects, workflow gates and claim/evidence relations — extensive prior art including Stencila and Whole Tale.

The only materially narrower route not closed by this search is the complete public institutional composition:

`persistent served public environment`
`+ machine-readable claim entitlement`
`+ current evidence / qualification / provenance / authority inputs`
`+ fail-closed entitlement recomputation`
`+ compulsory contraction of the served public representation`
`+ no manual override that can preserve a stronger unsupported public state`
`+ evidence-bearing requalification required before restoration`
`+ same law operating across heterogeneous research objects`

This is not equivalent to a TMS because a TMS governs internal beliefs, not necessarily a public institution's served assertions. It is not equivalent to living guidelines because their recommendation updates remain a publication/governance workflow rather than a demonstrated continuously enforced public computational surface. It is not equivalent to EviGraph because EviGraph governs autonomous research generation and manuscript production rather than a persistent public institution whose present authority to speak is itself the governed object.

The surviving result is therefore not a claim that Diamond Gate has invented evidence-based belief revision. It is a much narrower architectural hypothesis: whether the same governed entitlement logic can be made constitutive of a persistent public computational institution, such that the institution cannot continue serving a stronger assertion once the supporting state falls below its declared threshold.

## Required next experiment

The next lawful boundary is implementation-and-falsification, not more conceptual comparison.

Build an isolated BT4 entitlement harness around one existing public credibility claim. The harness must consume actual evidence/qualification/provenance state, derive the maximum permissible public status, and emit the public representation from that derived state. Then run at least four adversarial interventions:

1. source/provenance mismatch;
2. qualification/reproduction failure;
3. adverse evidence crossing the declared claim boundary;
4. presentation/source correspondence failure.

For each intervention, the harness must prove that the stronger public state becomes unreachable. Restoration must require fresh admissible evidence and requalification, producing a new receipt. A manually edited display string must not be able to override the computed entitlement.

Only after that passes across more than one heterogeneous claim/object class should BT4 be described as empirically demonstrated at the system-composition level.
