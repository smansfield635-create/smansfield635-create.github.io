Flow Track 01

Signals

Signals govern what is transmitted, what is received, and what becomes distorted before response. They show what is emitted or received and how movement becomes visible to a person or system.

Platform

Receive and distinguish

A system can only respond to what it can receive, distinguish, and interpret.

  • What is being sent?
  • Who or what receives it?
  • What was noticed, missed, amplified, filtered, or ignored?
Engineering

Trace the transmission

Signals map to event emission, telemetry, detection thresholds, routing, noise filtering, state notification, and observable change.

  • Declare the signal source.
  • Declare the receiver and route.
  • Preserve the distinction between emitted, received, and interpreted state.
Evidence

Show what arrived

Evidence requires records showing that an emitted signal was received, missed, changed, amplified, or filtered.

  • Strong: event logs, telemetry, timestamped signal records, receiver state.
  • Useful: alert history, routing traces, missed-signal reports, noise analysis.
Boundary

Signal presence is not interpretation proof.

This page preserves the Flow track’s human, engineering, and evidence distinctions. It does not claim scientific validation, production validation, or universal proof.
