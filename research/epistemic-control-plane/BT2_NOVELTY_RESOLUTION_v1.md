## Question

Does prior art disclose the same integrated executable state-transition architecture as the surviving BT2 candidate?

The surviving candidate combination is:

1. state-transition governance of scientific claim entitlement over time;
2. multidimensional entitlement;
3. negative-evidence custody;
4. promotion / hold / restriction / retraction;
5. claim-targeted contradiction;
6. machine-resolvable provenance receipts.

## Material prior art

### Micropublications

Clark et al., *Micropublications: a semantic model for claims, evidence, arguments and annotations in biomedical communications* (2014/2015 publication record). The model treats scientific claims as defeasible and explicitly represents supporting and challenging evidence, argument structure, attribution and provenance. This materially anticipates claim/evidence/provenance structure and negative evidence as a challenge to claims.

### SEPIO and related evidence/provenance ontologies

SEPIO represents scientific assertions, the propositions they express, evidence, provenance and supporting relationships in computable knowledge networks. This materially anticipates machine-resolvable claim/evidence/provenance representation.

### Evidence Graphs

*Evidence Graphs: Supporting Transparent and FAIR Computation, with Defeasible Reasoning on Data, Methods, and Results* treats data, methods and results as defeasible assertions and makes evidence relationships first-class computational objects. This materially anticipates computational defeasibility, provenance, and evidence-driven revision pressure.

### Scientific claim verification systems

SciFact and successor work formalize support/refute adjudication of scientific claims against literature and provide expert-annotated evaluation corpora. These systems do not by themselves disclose BT2's full state-transition governance, but they materially anticipate automated scientific claim adjudication.

### ClaimFlow

Pramanick et al., *ClaimFlow: Tracing the Evolution of Scientific Claims in NLP* (2026) explicitly models scientific claims as evolving across papers through support, extension, qualification, refutation and background relations. This directly anticipates temporal claim evolution and qualification/refutation semantics, though not necessarily fail-closed executable entitlement control.

### Preregistered Belief Revision Contracts (PBRC)

Alqithami, *Preregistered Belief Revision Contracts* (2026), fixes admissible evidence triggers and revision operators in advance, requires externally validated evidence tokens for substantive epistemic change, and makes belief change enforceable and auditable. This is a close conceptual and executable neighbor to BT2's evidence-gated promotion/contraction and provenance-backed state transition.

### Proof-or-Stop

*Proof-or-Stop: Don't Trust the Agent, Trust the Evidence Loop* (2026) implements evidence-gated claim admissibility and lifecycle transition semantics: actor output may propose a claim, but lifecycle advancement is controlled by admitted evidence. This is a direct prior/contemporary neighbor to BT2's executable state-transition governance.

### Workflow verification and provenance

Formal workflow-verification work such as Pritchard & Wicenec (2024) defines reproducibility tenets, cryptographic workflow signatures and automatic formal verification of scientific workflow quality. These systems strongly anticipate machine-resolvable provenance receipts and fail-closed verification behavior, although their governed object is primarily workflow execution rather than multidimensional scientific claim entitlement.

## Component and combination ruling

### Known components

The following are plainly not novel in isolation:

- machine-readable claims;
- provenance-linked assertions;
- evidence support/refutation;
- defeasible reasoning;
- belief revision and contraction;
- claim qualification/refutation over time;
- preregistered evidence triggers;
- evidence-gated state/lifecycle transitions;
- cryptographic or machine-verifiable provenance/audit traces;
- workflow-level fail-closed verification.

### Known combinations

Several combinations overlap materially with BT2:

- claim + evidence + provenance + support/challenge: Micropublications / SEPIO / Evidence Graphs;
- claim evolution + qualification/refutation over time: ClaimFlow;
- admissible evidence + enforced epistemic revision + audit trail: PBRC;
- evidence admission + lifecycle transition control: Proof-or-Stop;
- provenance + executable verification + workflow signatures: formal reproducibility/workflow systems.

### Diamond Gate implementation differences

The current BT2 implementation combines, in one scientific-governance runtime:

- multidimensional entitlement dimensions rather than a single confidence or support label;
- explicit promotion, hold, restriction and retraction dispositions;
- preservation of adverse/negative evidence rather than simple replacement;
- claim-targeted contradiction rather than global contradiction alone;
- fail-closed provenance and threshold-integrity gates;
- state-transition receipts tying evidentiary state to permitted scientific claim state.

These differences are real implementation characteristics. They are not, by themselves, evidence of novelty.

## Novelty ruling

The broad claim that Diamond Gate invented executable scientific claim admissibility is rejected.

The intermediate claim that Diamond Gate uniquely combined claim-state evolution, evidence/provenance, negative evidence, and executable revision is also not presently defensible. Contemporary and prior systems already cover substantial portions of that combination, including very close evidence-gated lifecycle and belief-revision architectures.

No located source in this audit was shown to disclose, in one scientific system, the exact full BT2 tuple of:

```text
multidimensional entitlement
+ promotion/hold/restriction/retraction
+ negative-evidence custody
+ claim-targeted contradiction
+ machine-resolvable provenance receipts
+ executable state-transition enforcement
```

However, absence of a located exact equivalent is not evidence sufficient for novelty.
