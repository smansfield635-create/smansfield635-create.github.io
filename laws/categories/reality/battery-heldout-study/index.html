Reality · Theory, Evidence, Measure, Limits

A real study must show the idea, the observation, the measurement, and the stopping point.

The battery result matters because it survived a held-out test in part, failed in part, exposed a conventional rival explanation, and still produced a reusable empirical pathway.

Theory

Why this domain and what coherence proposed.

Batteries offered a visible physical story of declining support: less retained capacity, greater proximity to a meaningful threshold, and real consequences for reliability.

The theory predicted that a combined B/P/E/I/V representation could retain information about approaching degradation across batteries excluded from development.

The study did not assume every equation would succeed. Combined axes, burden, H*, weakest-axis risk, the hard MQ threshold, and conventional baselines were tested separately.

Evidence

What was actually observed.

The combined representation strongly ranked observations closer to the degradation event on unseen batteries.

Cell-disjoint holdout supports transfer within this domain. It does not establish a universal mechanism or law.

Measure

How the positive, negative, and unresolved findings were separated.

The main model performed strongly, but the study also preserved the parts that did not improve prediction or failed outright.

Burden dominated; H* failed to add incremental value; weakest-axis risk remained preliminary; MQ failed at the hard threshold.

Limits

Where the inference must stop—and what position remains.

The strongest conventional explanation remains open: burden may largely represent age, use, or accumulated throughput.

Future studies inherit the empirical governance, not the battery conclusion: domain translation, frozen hypotheses, unseen-system testing, baseline comparison, component tests, negative-result custody, confound analysis, and claim ceilings.
