Capacity

Shared premise

Capacity is not desire. It is the real amount of burden a system can carry before promises become distortion.

  1. Available resource

    Story focus. This story layer narrows Capacity around one explicit distinction: name what the system can hold.

    Practical

    Lens frame. Capacity is not desire. It is the real amount of burden a system can carry before promises become distortion.

    What this selection adds. Name what the system can hold. This selection isolates available resource within Capacity. It shows which decision changes at this layer and prevents the card-level premise from standing in for the unresolved detail.

    Engineering

    Lens frame. In engineering terms, capacity maps to throughput, memory, bandwidth, queue depth, processing limits, resource budgets, latency tolerance, and saturation margins.

    What this selection adds. Measure load against available capacity. Treat available resource as a distinct control point inside Capacity. Give it an owner, explicit state, transition rule, and failure output so the implementation cannot hide it inside a general label.

    Empirical

    Lens frame. For capacity requires measured limits, not optimism. The system must show what it can sustain and where degradation begins.

    What this selection adds. Strong evidence: load tests, utilization records, saturation curves, stress results, recovery measurements. Evidence for available resource must identify the observed unit, context, comparison, and boundary. Without those records, Capacity remains a conceptual or procedural classification at this layer. Require records showing how Capacity behaved at ordinary load and at its boundary: crossings, rejected inputs, exceptions, enforcement, and resulting state.
