## RMA0 — Frozen authority

RMA0 froze pass G, pass H, the permanent eight scenes, cameras, viewport, metric grid, seven material families, one-family causal gate, one top-two combination fallback, and the three-result outcome law.

## RMA1 — Seven isolated family ablations

Each family was neutralized alone across all eight scenes. All 72 outputs were deterministic and retained metric coverage. No single family passed every causal term.

The impact ranking was:

1. Manor-local material and contact cues.
2. Contour and slope-rake.
3. Strata, cross-grain, and face bands.
4. Cavern-local material and contact cues.
5. Base-color and role blend.
6. Crest, terrace, and contact signals.
7. Broad, medium, grain, macro, meso, and detail fields.

The highest-ranked family reduced the aggregate score by only `0.577973%` and preserved all 24 dominant band orientation/lag matches to G. The third-ranked family changed more grid structure but increased the aggregate score and reduced only three scene scores. No family met the complete causal law.

## RMA3 — Practical result

The research ends in Result 3.

The whole CP2 material pass reproduces the scalar metric, but no isolated family or permitted bounded combination carries enough of the measured signature to justify suppressing it. The material attribution therefore does not narrow to a product-safe causal target.

Useful-cue retention is not executed as an authorization test because no suppression passed the causal gate. Testing whether a noncausal suppression retains Manor, Cavern, contact, slope, or route cues cannot justify implementing it.
