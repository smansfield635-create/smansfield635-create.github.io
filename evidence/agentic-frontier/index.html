Research · Agentic Software Frontier

Governed agency under one operator.

Diamond Gate Bridge is testing a specific 2026 frontier: whether a single operator can coordinate multiple software agents through repository-native authority, qualification, provenance, recovery, and publication controls while still producing substantial material work.

Claim boundary. The evidence supports describing Diamond Gate as operating at the frontier and as a strong publicly documented candidate in governed single-operator agentic software production. It does not establish an unconditional global #1 ranking.

Longitudinal audit

Material work, not activity volume.

The corrected fixed-population classification is 17 PARAMOUNT + 682 STANDARD + 207 SUPPORT = 906. PARAMOUNT and STANDARD together produce 699 material pull requests, or 77.15% of the audited population.

The original classifier experiment remains preserved separately. Its row-level v0 map did not survive, so row-level agreement, Cohen's kappa, and a disagreement ledger cannot legitimately be reconstructed. That limitation is part of the result, not a footnote to erase.

Governance architecture

Agency is separated from authority.

Repository operations distinguish intent, exact-state identity, mutation scope, qualification, receipts, merge, deployment, live verification, and recovery. An agent can propose or construct without automatically acquiring the authority to merge or publish.

Operational law: exact head → bounded authority → construction → qualification → approved commit → explicit deployment → live exact-head verification. Failures remain evidence and can stop the chain.

The frontier question is not whether agents can write code. It is whether many agentic actions can remain state-bound and inspectable under one operator without collapsing authorship, authority, evidence, and publication into one opaque event.

AF-IR-01

One controlled comparative data point.

AF-IR-01 used a frozen paired task with a common model/runtime/task/verifier frame. Under those tested conditions, the Diamond Gate lane passed while the stock OpenHands lane ended FAIL/INACTIVE. This is evidence for the tested configuration only; it is not a general superiority claim about OpenHands.

The next meaningful step is a frozen multi-task battery with normalized conditions and enough repetitions to distinguish a system effect from a single comparison.

Recorded execution media. The supplied recording is documentary evidence texture. This page embeds a lightweight still so the full recording does not become a first-paint dependency.

Coordination topology

Multiple agents, one operator, one repository state.

The repository documents multi-room and multi-agent collaboration under a single operator, including explicit routing, single-flight locks, independent verifier roles, exact-head rebinding, handoffs, and fail-closed stopping rules.

That topology is documented. An efficiency advantage is not yet claimed. Demonstrating that it is faster, cheaper, or more reliable than simpler agent workflows requires a separate measured comparison.

Readiness + assessment

Maturity is supporting evidence, not the frontier thesis.

Software TRL 7Strongly demonstrated operational prototype maturity. TRL 8 remains unclaimed because complete and stable system-wide verification and validation is a separate threshold.
Assessment Level 4 · BENCHMARKEDInternal benchmarking and controlled experiments strengthen the evidence base. Level 5 remains dependent on genuine external validation.

Neither scale substitutes for the agentic-frontier claim. They answer different questions: how mature the software is, how strong the assessment basis is, and what the governed operating model appears capable of doing.

Methodological limits

What this evidence does not prove.

  • No unconditional global leadership ranking is established.
  • AF-IR-01 is one controlled comparison, not a general benchmark victory.
  • The v0 audit survives only as aggregate totals, preventing legitimate reconstruction of row-level agreement statistics.
  • Documented multi-agent topology does not by itself prove efficiency gains.
  • Comparator asymmetries and external-validation gaps must remain visible as the research expands.
