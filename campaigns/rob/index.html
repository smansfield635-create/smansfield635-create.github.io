Rise Over Bullying.

Bullying operates wherever repeated power, isolation, intimidation, and silence are allowed to reinforce one another.

Rob bullies of their ability to bully.

Break the isolation.

Bullying gets stronger when a person is isolated and everyone else stays silent.

R.O.B. changes the equation. Make the harm visible. Move support toward the person. Take away the conditions that let bullying operate unchecked.

Rob them back.

Not with revenge. Rob bullying of what it depends on: isolation, silence, fear, confusion, and unchecked power.

Rise. Rob them back. Let’s rob them together.

Bullying is not a place.

Bullying is not defined by where it happens. It is defined by the pattern.

Repeated power. Isolation. Intimidation. Humiliation. Silence.

It can wear a uniform, sit behind a desk, hide behind a screen, call itself a joke, or disguise itself as “just how things are.”

Recognize the pattern wherever it appears.

Where can the pattern appear?
WorkplaceIntimidation, humiliation, exclusion, professional sabotage, coercive group pressure, or misuse of authority.
Public spacesHarassment, targeting, degrading treatment, threats, or bystander-enabled intimidation in ordinary community settings.
Online and institutionalCoordinated ridicule, rumor, exclusion, repeated abuse, or systems that make harmful conduct difficult to challenge.
Families and social groupsRepeated power imbalances can be disguised as tradition, humor, loyalty, or “just how things are.”

A person should not have to rise alone.

The moment someone else sees the harm, believes the person, remembers what happened, or refuses to normalize it, isolation begins to break.

Community changes the operating environment.

Visibility replaces isolation.
Documentation replaces ambiguity.
Shared standards replace silence.
Due process protects integrity.

The bully may target one person. The response does not have to belong to one person.

Stand with the person. Address the pattern.

Respond to what the situation requires without taking control away from the person being targeted.

In the moment

When it is safe, interrupt degrading conduct, move support toward the targeted person, and make clear that the behavior is being witnessed.

After the incident

Check on the person, preserve relevant facts, identify available reporting channels, and ask what support they want rather than taking control away from them.

When a pattern exists

Bring together responsible witnesses, documentation, policy, supervision, community leadership, or professional support appropriate to the setting.

When danger is immediate

Prioritize physical safety and contact appropriate emergency or protective services rather than escalating the confrontation.

Support

Standing up does not mean taking over.

Center the person being targeted. Ask what support they want. Preserve their agency. Preserve the facts. Escalate responsibly when the situation requires it.

Stand with the person before you make the story about fighting the bully.

A person should not have to rise alone.

Bullying depends on isolation. Community changes that condition.

See the harm. Support the person. Preserve the truth. Refuse to normalize the pattern.

Rob bullies of their ability to bully.
