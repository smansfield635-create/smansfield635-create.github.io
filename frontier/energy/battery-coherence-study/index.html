Applied empirical track · Battery degradation

Strong held-out signal. Universal proof withheld.

The frozen B/P/E/I/V representation was evaluated across 1,653 cell-disjoint held-out cycles from three unseen batteries—CS2_34, CS2_36, and CS2_38—to predict crossing below 80% retained capacity within the next 20 cycles.

What this page is for

One result, three readable layers.

The battery study asks whether the Diamond Gate Bridge axis representation can warn that a battery is approaching a serious capacity threshold—even when the battery was not used to develop the model.

The evaluation is cell-disjoint: development and test cells do not overlap. The target is a binary event defined as crossing below 80% retained capacity within a 20-cycle horizon. AUROC is used to compare ranking performance across representations and baselines.

Frozen test record: 1,653 held-out cycles; cells CS2_34, CS2_36, CS2_38; target horizon 20 cycles; threshold 80% retained capacity. This page preserves both positive and negative outcomes.

Study design

The test excluded all three evaluation batteries from development, then asked whether the model could recognize near-term degradation on those unseen batteries.

Evaluation design: cell-disjoint holdout. Target: retained capacity crossing below 80% within 20 cycles. Evaluation population: 1,653 cycles from CS2_34, CS2_36, and CS2_38.

Confound analysis

The strongest open question is whether “burden” is discovering a distinct structural signal or mostly restating how old and heavily used the battery already is.

Burden AUROC 0.9704 may encode cycle number, cumulative throughput, present capacity, or recent degradation slope. Independent predictive value requires controls and ablations against those variables.

UNRESOLVED_CONTROLS = cycle_number; cumulative_throughput; current_capacity; degradation_slope; burden_ablation; raw_plus_axes_incremental_test

Required next tests
