Laws Category · Reality

What holds after the claim is tested.

Reality separates claim from condition. It asks what remains after theory, evidence, measurement, limits, and audit have been applied.

Category role

Reality is used when a statement needs to become answerable. It does not reward confidence, performance, preference, or presentation before the claim survives testing.

  • Theory must face terrain.
  • Evidence must be checkable.
  • Measurement must define coordinates.
  • Limits and audit decide what may proceed.

Checkpoint 01

Reality

Make the claim answerable.

Claim Condition Test Disposition
Platform

Reality begins when a claim can be tested against something outside the claimant. The first question is not whether the claim sounds persuasive. The first question is whether it can be made answerable.

  • Separate claim from appearance.
  • Separate confidence from condition.
  • Separate preference from tested result.
  • Do not let presentation outrank constraint.
Engineering

Reality is the classification state assigned after a claim survives constraint testing.

  • Define the claim.
  • Expose the claim to evidence.
  • Declare measurement and limit conditions.
  • Assign a disposition state: proceed, revise, reject, hold, retest, recalibrate, or escalate.
Evidence

Evidence for Reality requires a claim that can be inspected, bounded, measured, and challenged.

  • Strong evidence: testable claim, recordable condition, traceable review, defined outcome state.
  • Useful evidence: claim inventory, review criteria, challenge record, disposition receipt.
  • Failure mode: the claim survives only as private certainty.
  • Boundary: a claim is not Reality because it is strongly believed.
Checkpoint 02

Theory

The map must face the terrain.

Model Assumption Prediction Revision
Platform

A theory helps people move through complexity, but it becomes dangerous when the map is defended after the terrain proves it wrong.

  • Theory earns trust when it predicts what happens next.
  • Theory loses trust when it needs excuses to stay right.
  • The model must not pretend to be the world.
  • The lawful move after contradiction is revision, not defense.
Engineering

Theory is a constrained explanatory model. It must declare assumptions, define variables, generate testable predictions, expose falsification conditions, and revise or fail when evidence contradicts the model.

  • Model assumptions must be explicit.
  • Predictions must be observable.
  • Falsification conditions must exist.
  • Contradiction must trigger revision, rejection, or hold-state classification.
Evidence

Evidence for Theory requires more than explanation. The theory must risk mismatch with the world.

  • Strong evidence: successful prediction, contradiction handling, revised model history, explicit assumptions.
  • Useful evidence: model diagrams, variable definitions, prediction logs, failed-case records.
  • Failure mode: the model explains everything after the fact but predicts nothing before the test.
  • Boundary: theory may guide inspection, but it does not outrank Reality.
Checkpoint 03

Evidence

Evidence is what can be checked.

Observation Record Provenance Checkability
Platform

Evidence moves a claim out of private insistence. It gives others something to inspect instead of asking them to trust the loudest voice in the room.

  • Good evidence is observable.
  • Good evidence is recorded.
  • Good evidence does not depend only on who says it.
  • Explanation may organize evidence, but it cannot replace it.
Engineering

Evidence requires observable data, provenance, collection method, sampling context, source reliability, bias exposure, and independent checkability.

  • Sampling method must be named.
  • Measurement method must be named.
  • Source posture must be traceable.
  • Evidence must be inspectable beyond the original claimant.
Evidence

Evidence for Evidence sounds redundant, but it is the core discipline: the evidence itself must carry enough information to be checked.

  • Strong evidence: raw record, metadata, provenance, collection method, independent check path.
  • Useful evidence: logs, observations, chain-of-custody notes, replication posture.
  • Failure mode: proof is asserted but not inspectable.
  • Boundary: evidence without provenance is weak support for durable inference.
Checkpoint 04

Measure

Measurement turns observation into coordinates.

Unit Calibration Sampling Uncertainty
Platform

Measurement gives people shared coordinates. Without measurement, arguments stay trapped in opinion. With measurement, people can compare what happened, how it happened, and whether enough has been shown.

  • What are we measuring?
  • How are we measuring it?
  • When are we measuring it?
  • What counts as enough?
Engineering

Measurement requires defined units, calibration, sampling frame, error bounds, uncertainty range, coordinate system, and classification rule.

  • Units define the scale.
  • Calibration defines instrument trust.
  • Sampling frame defines observation scope.
  • Uncertainty defines the boundary of confidence.
Evidence

Evidence for Measure requires proof that different qualified observers can classify the same condition under the same measurement contract.

  • Strong evidence: unit definitions, calibration record, sampling record, uncertainty range, observer agreement.
  • Useful evidence: measurement protocol, threshold definition, instrument notes, error-bound statement.
  • Failure mode: the measurement changes depending on who wants the answer.
  • Boundary: measurement is a shared contract, not personal interpretation.
Checkpoint 05

Limits

Limits are where certainty stops.

Boundary Unknown Error Applicability
Platform

Limits are where honest certainty stops. Naming limits does not weaken a claim; it protects the claim from pretending to know more than it knows.

  • Limits prevent false certainty.
  • Limits protect decision quality.
  • Limits keep unknowns visible.
  • Limits separate what is known from what is still open.
Engineering

Limits define uncertainty, applicability boundaries, model mismatch, sampling weakness, unknown variables, unresolved contradiction, and hold-state conditions.

  • Error bounds define claim strength.
  • Model mismatch defines transfer risk.
  • Unknown variables define hold states.
  • Applicability boundaries prevent overreach.
Evidence

Evidence for Limits requires named uncertainty and visible boundaries around the claim.

  • Strong evidence: uncertainty ranges, boundary conditions, sampling limits, unresolved contradiction records.
  • Useful evidence: hold-state logs, limitation statements, applicability maps, error bars.
  • Failure mode: partial truth is presented as total certainty.
  • Boundary: certainty without boundaries is narrative, not stable Reality classification.
Checkpoint 06

Audit

Audit decides what may proceed.

Proceed Revise Reject Hold
Platform

Audit is the final pressure test. What holds may proceed. What fails must be revised. What remains unknown must be named.

  • Separate established condition from open question.
  • Separate unstable claim from false claim.
  • Separate audit-ready claim from unreviewed claim.
  • Do not let certainty theater replace conclusion state.
Engineering

Audit determines claim disposition: proceed, revise, reject, hold, retest, recalibrate, or escalate.

  • If theory fails, revise the model.
  • If evidence fails, hold or reject the claim.
  • If measurement fails, recalibrate before proceeding.
  • If limits are missing, name the uncertainty before proceeding.
Evidence

Evidence for Audit requires a traceable conclusion path.

  • Strong evidence: audit trail, disposition receipt, contradiction handling, evidence review, limit declaration.
  • Useful evidence: review notes, retest records, escalation logs, revision records.
  • Failure mode: the claim proceeds without review because it sounds complete.
  • Boundary: audit is not hostility; it is the lawful closure of a claim.

Evidence outranks explanation. Explanation may organize evidence, but it cannot replace it.

Measurement is a shared contract, not personal interpretation.

Canonical Law Bounded Certainty Law

Every claim carries uncertainty bounds; what remains unknown must be named.

Signature Tested condition

Failure Pattern Certainty theater

A claim proceeds because it sounds complete while theory, evidence, measurement, limits, or audit remain unresolved.
