## Adjudication standard

Each live placement item was read individually for:
- grammatical validity and naturalness;
- whether the prompt says exactly what the keyed answer answers;
- instruction simplicity;
- English-proficiency construct purity;
- difficulty fit within D1-D8;
- distractor plausibility;
- discrimination quality;
- suitability for a real entry-level teaching platform.

Verdicts:
- KEEP — usable as written.
- REWORD — construct/difficulty broadly useful, wording or distractors need repair.
- RELEVEL — usable English item, but assigned difficulty is materially wrong.
- REBUILD — item is not reliable enough to preserve as currently designed.

## Cross-bank findings

1. The bank is strongest where it tests direct meaning, connected sentence comprehension, formal-to-common paraphrase, temporal/concessive relations, and modality.
2. The largest lower/middle-band defect is distractor quality. Many wrong answers are broken in several ways at once, allowing test-taking elimination instead of English discrimination.
3. Several direct vocabulary items were assigned too high: umbrella, pharmacy, fragile, appointment, refund, postponed, temporarily, extended.
4. Several basic grammar/instruction items were also over-leveled: worker derivation, regular past tense, simple deadline/instruction comprehension.
5. The clearest grammatical/content rebuild is s3a.
6. The clearest advanced rebuilds are s8a, s8e, and s8g because they remain too easy through broken distractors or partly test analytical/epistemic judgment.
7. Difficulty must be recalibrated after repairs. Releveling cannot simply create holes; replacements/hardening must restore exactly eight qualified items per D1-D8.
8. Placement hints are outside item validity and should not be used to rescue weak items. A placement item must stand on its own.
