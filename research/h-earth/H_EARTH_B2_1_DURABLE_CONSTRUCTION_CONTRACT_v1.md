## 4. WWW-AUDIT DESIGN FINDINGS

The B2.1 construction shall follow these evidence-derived principles:
- mountain morphology is organized by coupled ridge/drainage topology;
- spurs descend from ridge carriers rather than existing as independent positive masses;
- drainage occupies and shapes inter-spur lows, with downstream widening/deepening;
- erosion-like segmentation/lobing should break large smooth masses into legible landforms;
- smooth analytic crest regularity is insufficient for geological plausibility;
- adding undifferentiated noise is not an acceptable substitute for structural morphology.

These are design constraints, not empirical claims that H-Earth simulates real geomorphology.

## 5. CURRENT CODE FAILURE MAPPING

In B2, the primary ridges, meso ridges, hill, foothill taper and four spurs are expressed primarily as independent elliptical Gaussian positive-relief functions.

Positive composition remains:
`strongestPositiveRelief + secondaryPositiveRelief * 0.22`

Drainage remains expressed as independent negative Gaussian functions added after positive-relief composition.

This permits overlapping positive components to inflate a broad mass while drainage subtracts locally without controlling ridge topology.

B2.1 MUST change this relationship.

## 6. B2.1 CONSTRUCTION LAW

B2.1 shall replace the B2 meso-feature composition with a connected:

`RIDGE CARRIER -> ATTACHED TAPERED SPURS -> INTER-SPUR DRAINAGE -> DOWNSTREAM TRANSITION`

system.

Required behavior:

### Ridge carriers
- Preserve the established approximate macro-range location and overall envelope.
- Connect relief longitudinally rather than expressing each meso ridge as an isolated radial peak.
- Vary crest elevation along the carrier.
- Avoid a single dominant near-field wall.

### Spurs
- Attach explicitly to ridge carriers.
- Inherit elevation from their ridge attachment zone.
- Taper continuously toward lowland/foothill terrain.
- Separate laterally enough to produce readable inter-spur space.
- Do not exist as independent mountain blobs.

### Drainage
- Occupy inter-spur low corridors.
- Follow coherent downhill trajectories.
- Increase width and/or incision toward downstream terrain.
- Participate in shaping adjacent relief rather than acting only as post-hoc negative bumps.

### Saddles
- Remain meaningful transitions between ridge sections.
- Must not become isolated pits.
- Must contribute to ridge segmentation and depth.

### Macro envelope
- Preserve coastline and existing large-scale range placement.
- Reduce excessive local peak dominance and monolithic steep mass.
- Improve near/mid/far layering under the fixed Variant-B observer.
