Rise Over Bullying.

Bullying operates wherever repeated power, isolation, intimidation, and silence are allowed to reinforce one another.

Rob bullies of their ability to bully.

Rise Over Bullying.

R.O.B. turns a familiar word into a community instruction: rob bullies of their ability to bully by ending isolation, increasing visibility, and creating responsible accountability.

Rise. Rob them back. Let’s rob them together.

Not retaliation: R.O.B. does not authorize bullying, revenge, public shaming, mob punishment, or unsupported accusation.

Bullying has evolved beyond the playground.

WorkplaceIntimidation, humiliation, exclusion, professional sabotage, coercive group pressure, or misuse of authority.
Supermarkets, stores, and public spacesHarassment, targeting, degrading treatment, threats, or bystander-enabled intimidation in ordinary community settings.
Online and institutionalCoordinated ridicule, rumor, exclusion, repeated abuse, or systems that make harmful conduct difficult to challenge.
Families and social groupsRepeated power imbalances can be disguised as tradition, humor, loyalty, or “just how things are.”
Cognitive dissonance at scale

People can experience conflict between what they believe is right and what their group permits. When that tension is repeatedly rationalized, normalized, or silenced across a workplace or community, it becomes an added toxic factor that helps bullying continue.

Community accountability changes the operating environment.

Visibility replaces isolation.The targeted person is seen, heard, and supported.
Documentation replaces ambiguity.Patterns, dates, witnesses, and responsible reporting make repeated conduct harder to deny.
Shared standards replace silence.Peers, managers, families, institutions, and bystanders clarify what will not be normalized.
Due process protects integrity.Accountability remains evidence-aware, proportionate, and free from retaliatory harm.

Stand with the person. Address the pattern.

In the moment

When it is safe, interrupt degrading conduct, move support toward the targeted person, and make clear that the behavior is being witnessed.

After the incident

Check on the person, preserve relevant facts, identify available reporting channels, and ask what support they want rather than taking control away from them.

When a pattern exists

Bring together responsible witnesses, documentation, policy, supervision, community leadership, or professional support appropriate to the setting.

When danger is immediate

Prioritize physical safety and contact appropriate emergency or protective services rather than escalating the confrontation.
