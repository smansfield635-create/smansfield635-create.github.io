---

## 1. PURPOSE

Provide a qualified food/beverage formulator with the minimum complete brief required to develop R2 Candidate 1 without forcing unsupported chemistry, flavor, packaging, or shelf-life decisions upstream.

The formulator's job is to convert a scientifically bounded product architecture into manufacturable prototypes.

This specification is not:

- a final recipe,
- a manufacturing master formula,
- a consumer dosing instruction,
- authorization for tasting or human use,
- a claim substantiation package.

---

## 2. PRODUCT IDENTITY

**Working program name:** BASELINE  
**Development object:** R2 Candidate 1  
**Format:** single-serve liquid concentrate requiring dilution in water  
**Use concept:** meal-proximal pre-meal transition beverage  
**Primary mechanism under investigation:** acetic-acid-mediated modulation of the postprandial meal transition  
**Primary future physiological endpoint:** postprandial glucose excursion

The product is not positioned as a diabetes treatment, glucose-lowering drug, dehydration treatment, meal replacement, weight-loss treatment, or disease-prevention product.

---

## 3. CORE CONSUMER WORKFLOW

Target eventual workflow:

1. open one single-serving concentrate,
2. empty into a defined amount of water,
3. mix briefly,
4. consume promptly,
5. proceed to the normal meal within a future validated meal-proximal window.

The product should not require:

- shaker bottle,
- blender,
- multiple scoops,
- multiple pills,
- prolonged sipping,
- refrigeration at point of use if avoidable,
- complicated sequencing.

---

## 4. ACTIVE-MECHANISM DEVELOPMENT ENVELOPE

Human literature reviewed by the program supports investigation of meal-proximal acetic-acid exposures in approximately the:

**0.5–1.25 g acetic-acid region**

This is a development envelope derived from published human research.

It is **not** a final consumer dose.

The formulator should propose prototypes within or scientifically justified around this region, subject to:

- ingredient identity,
- acid assay,
- dilution,
- palatability,
- dental considerations,
- GI/esophageal considerations,
- regulatory/safety review.

Do not silently exceed the development envelope.

Any proposed departure should be documented with rationale.

---

## 5. ACTIVE-SOURCE REQUIREMENTS

The formulator should recommend an appropriate standardized food-grade acid source.

The proposal must identify:

- ingredient name,
- manufacturer/supplier,
- specification,
- acetic-acid assay/concentration,
- relevant carrier/matrix,
- grade,
- documentation available,
- allergen status where applicable,
- storage requirements.

If vinegar-derived rather than a more standardized acetic-acid source, identify other constituents likely to affect:

- flavor,
- aroma,
- color,
- pH,
- titratable acidity,
- stability,
- mechanism attribution.

Different vinegar sources should not be treated as automatically interchangeable.

---

## 6. REQUIRED FORMAT

**Single-use liquid concentrate**

Mandatory:

- designed to be diluted before consumption,
- single-serving quantity,
- accurate serving delivery,
- clear dilution instructions in future consumer design,
- packaging that discourages direct concentrate consumption,
- compatible with acidic contents.

The formulator may recommend the concentrate volume.

Do not optimize toward the smallest possible concentrate if doing so materially worsens safe handling, packaging, sensory burden, or misuse risk.

---

## 7. FINAL DILUTION

The exact final beverage volume remains open.

Formulator should propose practical dilution volumes that balance:

- acid concentration,
- palatability,
- drinking time,
- oral contact,
- GI burden,
- portability,
- consumer convenience.

R1's 350 mL is not a mandatory R2 inheritance.

The selected prototype set should make the dilution-volume tradeoff explicit.

---

## 8. CALORIC REQUIREMENT

Target:

**very low calorie**

No added caloric ingredient should be included without a defined functional reason.

Avoid adding sugar merely to hide acidity.

If sweetness is needed, the formulator should compare low-calorie options appropriate to the product and document:

- ingredient,
- amount,
- calorie contribution,
- sensory role,
- stability impact,
- pH/acidity impact,
- regulatory considerations.

---

## 9. DEFAULT INGREDIENT EXCLUSIONS

Do not include by default:

- sodium chloride,
- potassium chloride,
- magnesium citrate,
- dextrose,
- protein,
- viscous fiber,
- caffeine,
- stimulants,
- botanicals,
- broad vitamin/mineral premix.

These are not prohibited forever.

They are excluded from the initial prototype because none has earned a required role in Candidate 1.

Any proposed addition must state:

1. exact problem being solved,
2. why formulation cannot solve it more simply,
3. effect on mechanism,
4. effect on calories,
5. effect on tolerability,
6. effect on stability,
7. effect on regulatory/safety burden.

---

## 10. PALATABILITY OBJECTIVE

Develop the minimum flavor/aroma system needed to make a diluted acidic pre-meal beverage realistically usable.

Desired sensory direction:

- clean,
- light,
- not syrupy,
- not medicinal,
- not aggressively vinegary,
- no prolonged aftertaste where avoidable.

The formulator should not neutralize the acid merely to make the product taste easier.

Mechanism preservation has priority over cosmetic flavor improvement.

---

## 11. MECHANISM-PRESERVATION REQUIREMENT

For each prototype report:

- calculated acetic-acid amount per serving,
- final dilution volume,
- calculated final acid concentration,
- measured pH,
- titratable acidity,
- any buffering/neutralization introduced by support ingredients.

A flavor system fails if it materially removes the acid exposure the architecture is intended to test.

---

## 12. DENTAL-CONTACT CONSTRAINT

Acid exposure creates a known formulation concern for tooth enamel.

The formulator should therefore minimize unnecessary oral acid contact through product design.

Required considerations:

- dilution,
- consumption duration,
- no prolonged sipping design,
- acid concentration,
- pH,
- titratable acidity,
- flavor choices that do not encourage prolonged oral holding.

The formulator is not expected to independently establish dental safety.

Candidate prototypes should be suitable for later qualified dental review.

---

## 13. GI / ESOPHAGEAL CONSTRAINT

The formulation should avoid designing a harsh acid bolus.

Formulator should identify:

- expected final acidity,
- dilution assumptions,
- ingredients likely to worsen or improve sensory throat burden,
- any formulation issue relevant to reflux/upper-GI tolerability.

No prototype should be labeled “GI safe,” “gentle,” or “reflux safe” without appropriate human evidence.

---

## 14. PRODUCT BURDEN LIMIT

Candidate 1 should remain materially lighter than protein/fiber preload architectures.

Avoid:

- thickening unless essential,
- gram-scale protein,
- gram-scale viscous fiber,
- high sugar,
- large ingredient stack,
- excessive final beverage volume.

The product identity is:

**minimal intervention before an ordinary meal.**

---

## 15. PROTOTYPE REQUEST

Formulator should return a small bounded prototype family rather than dozens of variants.

Preferred initial set:

### Prototype F1 — mechanism minimum
Acid system + water dilution architecture with minimal/no flavor support.

### Prototype F2 — primary palatability candidate
Same active architecture + minimal flavor/aroma system.

### Prototype F3 — alternate palatability direction
Same active architecture + one meaningfully different flavor approach.

Optional:

### Prototype F4
Only if the formulator identifies a specific technical problem that requires one additional functional ingredient.

Do not create a broad supplement stack.

---

## 16. REQUIRED OUTPUT FOR EACH PROTOTYPE

For each prototype provide:

- complete ingredient list,
- ingredient suppliers/specifications,
- exact concentrate composition,
- concentrate serving volume,
- acetic-acid amount,
- dilution volume,
- final beverage composition,
- calories,
- carbohydrate/sugar,
- sodium,
- potassium,
- measured pH,
- titratable acidity,
- appearance,
- flavor concept,
- preparation instructions,
- packaging recommendation,
- known stability concerns,
- known ingredient interactions,
- allergen information,
- preliminary cost/manufacturing considerations,
- any safety/regulatory concern identified.

---

## 17. STABILITY OUTPUT

At this stage, full commercial shelf-life validation is not required before architecture selection.

The formulator should provide enough preliminary stability information to determine whether the concept is practical.

At minimum identify:

- likely chemical stability concerns,
- likely flavor/aroma stability concerns,
- package compatibility concerns,
- precipitation/separation risk,
- microbiological/preservation considerations,
- recommended next stability work.

Do not launch a full shelf-life program until a prototype is selected.

---

## 18. PACKAGING OUTPUT

Primary concept:

**single-serving liquid unit**

Formulator/manufacturer should propose suitable package classes considering:

- acid compatibility,
- oxygen/light sensitivity if relevant,
- accurate fill,
- leakage,
- portability,
- direct-consumption misuse,
- opening/emptying into water,
- cost,
- manufacturability.

Examples may include appropriate single-dose sachet/ampoule/pod-type systems, but no specific package is mandated upstream.

---

## 19. REGULATORY / CLAIM BOUNDARY

The formulation partner should identify regulatory classification considerations for the proposed product format/ingredients in the intended market.

No prototype should be marketed with claims such as:

- treats diabetes,
- prevents glucose spikes,
- treats dehydration,
- protects the cardiovascular system,
- causes weight loss,
- prevents disease.

Scientific endpoints used in research do not automatically become consumer claims.

---

## 20. ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA FOR FORMULATOR RETURN

A prototype family is acceptable for internal review if:

1. active mechanism remains clearly defined,
2. acid exposure stays within the authorized development envelope or justified deviation,
3. dilution is practical,
4. calories remain very low,
5. unnecessary ingredients are avoided,
6. palatability strategy is credible,
7. dental/GI liabilities are acknowledged rather than hidden,
8. pH and titratable acidity are reported,
9. packaging appears technically feasible,
10. no major unresolved incompatibility makes the concept obviously impractical.

---

## 21. REJECTION CRITERIA

Reject/rework a formulator proposal if it:

- turns Candidate 1 into a sugary beverage,
- converts it into a protein/fiber product,
- adds a large micronutrient stack without mechanism,
- proposes an undiluted acid shot,
- assumes capsule/tablet equivalence without evidence,
- neutralizes the acid enough to undermine the mechanism,
- omits pH/titratable-acidity characterization,
- ignores dental/GI constraints,
- requires prolonged sipping,
- materially exceeds the development envelope without rationale.

---

## 22. WHAT THE FORMULATOR IS FREE TO SOLVE

The professional formulation partner should own:

- exact acid source selection,
- concentrate volume,
- flavor system,
- sweetness system if needed,
- processing method,
- ingredient order of addition,
- preservation strategy,
- package-material recommendation,
- manufacturing tolerances,
- preliminary stability strategy.

These are professional formulation variables, not questions the research architecture needs to pretend to answer in advance.

---

## 23. WHAT REMAINS UNDER PROGRAM AUTHORITY

The program retains authority over:

- product purpose,
- mechanism,
- evidence boundary,
- active-development envelope,
- dilution requirement,
- low-calorie requirement,
- minimalist architecture,
- exclusion of unnecessary actives,
- future comparator design,
- research endpoints,
- advancement/kill decision.

## 24. R1 SEPARATION

Do not modify R1 during Candidate 1 formulation work.

R1 remains an independent candidate and empirical question.

A formulator should not merge R1 and Candidate 1 unless explicitly commissioned in a later version.
