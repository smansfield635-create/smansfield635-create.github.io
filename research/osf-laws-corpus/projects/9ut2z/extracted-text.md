Integral Platform — Scientific Method (Explained)
Purpose
This document explains how to apply the Scientific Method under the Universal Law of Objectivity and Integrity. It is
written for practitioners who need more than a checklist. Each method is explained in terms of intent, mechanism, and
outcome, so that it can be applied correctly rather than followed mechanically.
The Governing Constraint
All methods described here operate under a single constraint: objective truth cannot be violated without contradiction.
The purpose of every step is not to arrive at an answer, but to prevent violation of this constraint while inquiry proceeds.
Step 1: Observation
Observation establishes contact with reality before interpretation begins. At this stage, nothing is explained, justified, or
framed. The goal is to record what is present without embedding assumptions that will later need to be removed.
Step 2: Hypothesis Formation
A hypothesis is a provisional structure that could account for what was observed. It must be testable and reducible. A
hypothesis is not a belief or a commitment; it is an object offered up for reduction.
Step 3: Reduction (1–2–3)
Reduction is the core discipline of the method. It prevents narratives from replacing structure.
1
1. State the claim. The hypothesis must be written as a clear, finite claim. If it cannot be stated cleanly, it cannot be
tested.
2
2. Remove non-essential assumptions. Identify every assumption the claim depends on. Remove any
assumption that is not strictly required for the claim to stand.
3
3. Test the remainder. Examine what remains for internal contradiction or violation of objectivity. If contradiction
appears, the claim collapses correctly.
Step 4: Falsification
Falsification is the moment when a claim is allowed to fail. Failure here is not error; it is confirmation that integrity has
been preserved. A falsified claim does not need to be defended, revised, or justified.
Step 5: Iteration (9-to-5 Principle)
Iteration refers to the ordinary, repetitive nature of real scientific work. Progress does not occur through sudden insight,
but through daily, incremental error removal. The 9-to-5 principle emphasizes discipline over spectacle.
Step 6: Termination
Termination occurs when further inquiry would require violating objectivity. At this point, inquiry must stop—not because
the answer is complete, but because continuation would be incoherent.
Advanced Compression (11–13)
Once multiple iterations have been completed, higher-order compression is applied:
1
11. Consolidate invariants. Identify what has remained unchanged across successful reductions.

What Success Looks Like
A successful application of this method does not produce certainty or authority. It produces clarity. Claims either stand

Exit
When the method terminates correctly, inquiry moves to other surfaces—such as applied systems or exploratory
