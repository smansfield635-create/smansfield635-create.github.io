## 1. PURPOSE

Correct the sequencing error created by advancing too quickly into laboratory infrastructure.

The immediate product-development question is:

> Have we identified a next-generation Baseline candidate that is plausibly more functional than R1 while remaining low-burden?

This artifact defines that candidate at the architecture level and compares it directly with R1.

It does not claim empirical superiority and does not authorize self-experimentation or human dosing.

---

## 2. R1 — CURRENT FROZEN CANDIDATE

R1 remains:

- sodium chloride — 750 mg
- potassium chloride — 250 mg
- magnesium citrate material — 100 mg
- dextrose — 1.5 g
- water — 350 mL

### R1 intended domain

- fluid preload,
- controlled pause,
- hunger/thirst/fullness transition,
- fasted-to-meal transition,
- meal pacing/comfort hypotheses.

### R1 central weakness

Its active ingredients have not yet demonstrated an added benefit over 350 mL matched water.

R1 also has no established mechanism for:

- postprandial glycemic buffering,
- high-sodium buffering,
- high-fat vascular buffering,
- nutritional balancing.

## 3. R2 CANDIDATE 1 — DEVELOPMENT DEFINITION

### Working identity

**Acid-mediated minimalist pre-meal transition candidate**

### Core architecture

1. controlled water vehicle,
2. standardized food-grade acetic-acid/vinegar-derived active system,
3. minimal non-active palatability support only if required.

### Explicitly NOT inherited by default

- NaCl,
- KCl,
- magnesium citrate,
- dextrose.

Each would have to earn re-entry independently.

## 4. EVIDENCE-DERIVED ACTIVE EXPOSURE REGION

Published human trials have reported postprandial effects with meal-proximal vinegar/acetic-acid exposures around the following region:

- approximately 10 g vinegar in a randomized crossover program, with mealtime administration outperforming administration 5 hours earlier;
- approximately 1 g acetic acid in several acute vinegar studies summarized in systematic-review literature;
- approximately 1.25 g acetic acid delivered as 25 g liquid vinegar in a crossover study, where liquid vinegar reduced the glucose excursion while commercial tablets did not show the same effect;
- randomized bread-meal research tested 18–28 mmol acetic acid and reported dose-related changes in early glucose/insulin responses and satiety.

### Candidate 1 development envelope

For product-development purposes, Candidate 1 should therefore investigate a **meal-proximal, approximately 0.5–1.25 g acetic-acid evidence region**, rather than trace acid exposure.

This is an evidence-derived development envelope, not a consumer dose instruction.

No exact final exposure is selected here.

---

## 5. TIMING CONCEPT

Candidate 1 is a **meal-proximal** intervention.

The evidence does not justify treating it as an all-day tonic or something taken hours before eating.

Development should preserve:

**short intervention → meal shortly thereafter**

The eventual timing protocol must be fixed before human testing.

---

## 6. DELIVERY CONCEPT

Preferred development direction:

**diluted liquid / rapidly available acid system**

Reason:

Human evidence indicates delivery form matters. Liquid vinegar has produced a postprandial glucose effect in settings where nominally comparable commercial vinegar tablets did not produce the same result.

Encapsulation is therefore not assumed equivalent merely because the labeled acetic-acid mass is similar.

---

## 7. PRODUCT FORM

Candidate 1 should remain recognizably Baseline:

- modest liquid volume,
- consumed promptly rather than sipped for hours,
- very low calorie,
- no thick texture,
- no protein-shake identity,
- no fiber-slurry identity,
- no large supplement stack.

The exact water volume is **not yet locked**.

R1's 350 mL should not be inherited automatically if a smaller practical volume can preserve safety/product requirements.

---

## 8. PALATABILITY SUPPORT

A final product may require flavor/aroma management.

Allowed design principle:

**minimum support required for realistic use**

A support system must not:

- neutralize the active acid to the point that the evidence-relevant mechanism is lost,
- add a meaningful sugar/calorie burden without justification,
- create another active mechanism that makes interpretation difficult.

Exact flavor ingredients remain an engineering decision.

---

## 9. DENTAL / GI CONSTRAINT

Candidate 1 carries liabilities R1 largely does not:

- acidic tooth contact,
- potential reflux/esophageal irritation,
- GI intolerance,
- sour/aversive sensory burden.

These are not minor details.

Candidate 1 only remains viable if later formulation work can reduce exposure burden without destroying the acid mechanism.

No claim of dental or GI safety is made here.

## 11. IS CANDIDATE 1 POTENTIALLY BETTER THAN R1?

### On mechanism coverage

**YES, POTENTIALLY.**

Candidate 1 adds a mechanism with direct human evidence that R1 does not possess: modulation of postprandial glucose response in meal-proximal vinegar/acetic-acid studies.

### On simplicity

**POTENTIALLY YES.**

Candidate 1 may require fewer active ingredients than R1.

### On safety/tolerability burden

**NO CLEAR ADVANTAGE.**

Candidate 1 creates new acid-specific dental, reflux, GI, and sensory constraints.

### On demonstrated product performance

**UNKNOWN.**

Neither R1 nor Candidate 1 has direct product-specific empirical evidence.

Therefore:

> Candidate 1 is a more mechanistically ambitious and potentially simpler successor, but it has not earned the label “better product” yet.

They may represent two possible Baseline functions:

## 15. WHAT WE NEED NEXT

The next useful development question is commercial/scientific rather than laboratory-heavy:

> Can Candidate 1 be converted into a realistic consumer product format that preserves the evidence-derived acid mechanism while solving the obvious acid taste/dental/GI problems?

That requires evaluating a small number of **delivery concepts**, not running a laboratory program.

Examples of development categories to compare:

- diluted ready-to-drink,
- small concentrate diluted before use,
- powder/reconstitution only if the active chemistry remains legitimate,
- other rapid-delivery food/beverage formats supported by formulation expertise.

Encapsulated delivery should remain secondary because equivalence to liquid cannot be assumed.
