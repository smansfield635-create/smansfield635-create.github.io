## 1. PURPOSE

Separate what is already supported by external evidence from what R1 itself still has to demonstrate.

The register uses five evidence classes:

1. **ESTABLISHED PHYSIOLOGY** — mechanism or phenomenon supported independently of R1.
2. **EXTERNAL INTERVENTION EVIDENCE** — human studies support a related preload/water effect, but not R1 specifically.
3. **R1-SPECIFIC HYPOTHESIS** — plausible proposition requiring direct R1 testing.
4. **DIRECTLY TESTABLE IN PILOT** — the frozen R1-versus-water protocol can adjudicate it.
5. **CURRENTLY UNSUPPORTED / OUT OF SCOPE** — R1 should not be credited with the mechanism.

No category implies clinical efficacy.

---

## 2. EVIDENCE CONTROL PRINCIPLE

The first R1 experiment is not:

**Does pre-meal fluid work?**

The literature already indicates that fluid volume, gastric distension, preload energy, timing, age, and meal context can influence subjective appetite or intake, with heterogeneous results.

The R1 question is narrower:

> **Does the frozen R1 composition add a reproducible benefit beyond the same 350 mL of water under controlled fasted-to-meal conditions?**

That distinction is the scientific center of the program.

---

## 3. DOMAIN A — FLUID PRELOAD

### External evidence

Human studies show that water consumed before a meal can increase fullness and, in some populations/settings, reduce subsequent intake. Effects are not universal. One controlled study found reduced meal energy intake in older adults but not younger adults after a 30-minute water preload. Other work and reviews similarly indicate that water/preload volume can affect appetite sensations without reliably reducing intake in every setting.

### Classification

- Established physiology: **SUPPORTED**
- External intervention evidence: **SUPPORTED, HETEROGENEOUS**
- R1-specific advantage: **UNPROVEN**
- Directly testable: **YES**

### R1 hypothesis

R1 may alter the pre-meal transition beyond the effect produced by 350 mL water alone.

### Pilot outcomes

- hunger
- thirst
- fullness
- meal intake
- meal duration
- eating rate
- GI/post-meal comfort

### Advancement rule

Any R1 effect must exceed matched water. A response shared equally by R1 and water is a preload/water effect, not an R1-specific effect.

---

## 4. DOMAIN B — GASTRIC VOLUME / FULLNESS

### External evidence

Gastric distension and gastric accommodation participate in satiation physiology. Human studies show relationships among gastric/antral distension, hunger, fullness, and intake. However, pure mechanical distension does not consistently reduce subsequent caloric intake, and volume-related subjective appetite changes can occur without an intake change.

### Classification

- Established physiology: **SUPPORTED**
- External intervention evidence: **SUPPORTED BUT VARIABLE**
- R1-specific mechanism: **NOT ESTABLISHED**
- Directly testable subjective consequence: **YES**
- Direct gastric mechanism measurement in current pilot: **NO**

### R1 hypothesis

The 350 mL preload may change subjective fullness/hunger before the meal, but R1's dissolved ingredients may or may not add anything beyond the identical volume of water.

### Critical interpretation

A T1 fullness change is not evidence that R1 has a unique active mechanism unless R1 differs from water.

---

## 5. DOMAIN C — HUNGER / HIGH-HUNGER TRANSITION

### External evidence

Preload volume and nutrient content can affect subjective hunger and fullness. Published results show that subjective appetite and actual subsequent intake are separable outcomes; lower hunger or higher fullness does not guarantee lower intake.

### Classification

- Established physiology: **SUPPORTED**
- External preload evidence: **SUPPORTED**
- R1-specific hunger effect: **UNPROVEN**
- Directly testable: **YES**

### R1 hypothesis

After the controlled food-free interval, R1 may reduce the intensity of the high-hunger-to-meal transition more than water.

### Measurements

- T0 hunger
- T1 hunger
- within-session change
- paired R1-minus-water difference

### Kill condition

If R1 and water produce materially similar hunger changes, an R1-specific hunger-transition advantage is not established.

---

## 6. DOMAIN D — EATING RATE / BEHAVIORAL PAUSE

### External rationale

The protocol itself creates a standardized consumption event and 30-minute interval before eating. That structure can alter behavior independent of ingredient chemistry.

### Classification

- Structured pause exists by protocol: **ESTABLISHED DESIGN FACT**
- Ingredient-specific pacing benefit: **UNPROVEN**
- Directly testable: **YES**

### R1 hypothesis

R1 may influence subsequent meal duration/eating rate relative to matched water.

### Critical confound control

Because both conditions receive the same volume, consumption procedure, and waiting interval, any generic “pause before eating” effect is intentionally shared.

Therefore R1 only earns an ingredient-specific pacing signal if its condition differs from water.

---

## 7. DOMAIN E — MEAL INTAKE

### External evidence

Preload studies demonstrate that subsequent intake can change depending on preload composition, volume, timing, population, and energy content. Water alone has reduced intake in some experiments but not others. Nutrient-containing preloads can also alter subsequent intake.

### Classification

- Preload influence on intake: **SUPPORTED BUT CONTEXT DEPENDENT**
- R1-specific intake effect: **UNPROVEN**
- Directly testable: **YES**

### R1 hypothesis

R1 may change grams consumed from the standardized ad-libitum breakfast relative to matched water.

### Measurement

Primary quantitative measure:

**offered mass − remaining mass**

Supporting:

**grams/minute**

### Interpretation boundary

Reduced intake is not automatically beneficial. It is an experimental outcome, not a weight-loss claim.

---

## 8. DOMAIN F — THIRST / HYDRATION TRANSITION

### External rationale

Water ingestion directly changes immediate fluid exposure. R1 additionally contains sodium, potassium, a small magnesium-citrate material mass, and dextrose, but the current pilot does not contain formal hydration biomarkers.

### Classification

- Fluid exposure: **ESTABLISHED**
- R1 electrolyte-specific hydration advantage: **UNPROVEN**
- Subjective thirst effect: **DIRECTLY TESTABLE**
- Clinical hydration improvement: **NOT TESTED**
- Dehydration treatment: **OUT OF SCOPE**

### R1 hypothesis

R1 may alter subjective thirst or meal-transition comfort beyond matched water.

### Limitation

Without objective hydration biomarkers, the pilot cannot establish improved physiological hydration from subjective thirst alone.

---

## 9. DOMAIN G — GI / POST-MEAL COMFORT

### Evidence state

The current R1 formula has no established evidence demonstrating superior GI or post-meal comfort relative to matched water.

### Classification

- R1-specific comfort effect: **UNPROVEN**
- Directly testable: **YES**
- Mechanistic attribution from current pilot: **LIMITED**

### R1 hypothesis

R1 may improve—or worsen—GI/post-meal comfort relative to water.

### Importance

This is both an efficacy-adjacent and tolerability domain. A formulation that changes hunger but produces worse GI comfort may fail advancement.

---

## 10. DOMAIN H — ELECTROLYTE CONTRIBUTION

### Formulation fact

Under nominal pure-salt assumptions, R1 contains approximately:

- 295 mg elemental sodium from 750 mg NaCl
- 131 mg elemental potassium from 250 mg KCl
- unresolved elemental magnesium from 100 mg magnesium-citrate material

Actual released values remain dependent on material verification.

### Classification

- Presence of Na/K salts in frozen formulation: **FORMULATION FACT**
- unique meal-transition benefit from those salts: **UNPROVEN**
- ability of current pilot to isolate each salt: **NO**
- ability to test combined R1 vs water: **YES**

### Interpretation

If R1 outperforms water, this pilot establishes a formulation-level signal only. It cannot determine whether sodium, potassium, magnesium citrate, dextrose, taste, osmolality, or interactions among them caused the difference.

Component attribution requires later experimental decomposition.

---

## 11. DOMAIN I — SMALL DEXTROSE EXPOSURE

### Formulation fact

R1 contains 1.5 g dextrose material.

### Classification

- presence in R1: **FORMULATION FACT**
- glycemic buffering: **UNSUPPORTED**
- meaningful metabolic priming: **UNPROVEN**
- direct glycemic testing in current pilot: **NO**

### Claim boundary

The 1.5 g dextrose dose must not be described as:

- glucose buffering,
- glycemic stabilization,
- insulin priming with demonstrated benefit,
- prevention of post-meal glucose excursions.

Those require separate evidence.

---

## 12. DOMAIN J — FASTED-TO-MEAL TRANSITION

### Program hypothesis

A defined food-free interval followed by a meal creates a measurable transition across hunger, thirst, fullness, eating behavior, intake, and comfort.

### Classification

- existence of changing pre/post meal physiology: **ESTABLISHED**
- “transition burden” as a unified R1 construct: **MODELING HYPOTHESIS**
- R1 improvement of that construct: **UNPROVEN**
- individual constituent outcomes: **DIRECTLY TESTABLE**

### Scientific rule

Do not manufacture a composite “transition score” after seeing results.

The v1 pilot evaluates the prespecified constituent outcomes separately.

A composite endpoint would require prospective construction and validation in a later protocol.

# 13. EXCLUDED R1 MECHANISMS

## High-sodium meal buffering

**CURRENT STATUS: UNSUPPORTED / NOT TESTED**

R1 itself contributes sodium and cannot be represented as a sodium-load buffering intervention without new evidence and likely a different formulation architecture.

## Glycemic buffering

**CURRENT STATUS: UNSUPPORTED / NOT TESTED**

No glucose endpoint exists in the v1 pilot.

## High-fat vascular buffering

**CURRENT STATUS: UNSUPPORTED / NOT TESTED**

No vascular or lipid endpoint exists in the v1 pilot and no R1-specific mechanism has been established.

## Nutritional balancing of an inadequate meal

**CURRENT STATUS: UNSUPPORTED / NOT A FUNCTION OF R1**

The small R1 ingredient set does not nutritionally complete an imbalanced meal.

These remain outside R1 and do not automatically become R2 claims.

## 15. WHAT A POSITIVE PILOT WOULD ACTUALLY MEAN

A favorable result would support:

> Under the frozen test conditions, the complete R1 formulation produced a measurable difference from 350 mL matched water on one or more prespecified meal-transition outcomes with acceptable tolerability.

It would **not** establish why.

It would justify:

1. replication,
2. effect-size/variance estimation,
3. possibly a larger confirmatory study,
4. later component-decomposition work if scientifically warranted.

---

## 16. WHAT A NULL PILOT WOULD MEAN

If R1 is materially similar to water:

- the experiment may still confirm that the preload procedure itself changes some outcomes,
- those shared changes belong primarily to water/volume/timing/ritual under this design,
- R1's dissolved ingredients have not earned added-effect attribution,
- R2 is not automatically justified.

The correct response would be mechanism review, not rescue-by-reformulation.

---

## 17. WHAT A NEGATIVE / TOLERABILITY RESULT WOULD MEAN

If R1 worsens GI comfort, acceptability, symptoms, or other important outcomes:

- unchanged R1 should not advance,
- the adverse pattern must be investigated,
- ingredient-specific blame cannot be assigned from the combined-formula comparison alone,
- any reformulation becomes a new version requiring prospective review.

## 19. CURRENT SCIENTIFIC POSITION

### Supported independently of R1

- gastric/intestinal physiology participates in satiation and appetite regulation;
- fluid/preload volume can alter subjective appetite;
- pre-meal water can alter fullness and sometimes subsequent intake;
- effects vary by population, timing, volume, and experimental context;
- subjective appetite and actual intake need not move together.

### Plausible but unproven for R1

- additional hunger-transition effect beyond water;
- additional thirst-transition effect beyond water;
- eating-rate/pacing effect beyond water;
- intake effect beyond water;
- GI/post-meal comfort effect beyond water.

### Not presently supported for R1

- sodium buffering;
- glycemic buffering;
- vascular protection from high-fat meals;
- nutritional balancing;
- disease prevention/treatment;
- clinical dehydration treatment.

---

## 20. DETERMINISTIC ADVANCEMENT RULE

R1 earns advancement only through evidence that survives the matched-water comparator.

No effect shared with water is credited to R1's active ingredients.

No unmeasured mechanism is inferred from a favorable subjective outcome.

No R2 mechanism is authorized merely because R1 fails.
