# Preregistration — Independent Constitutive Route Identification Challenge v1

## Scope

This is the first adversarial dependency test. It precedes scientific credit for the IMI scalar, trajectory, restoration, or successor equations. The frozen claim scope is `UNIVERSAL_IDENTIFIABILITY`.

## System admission

Before outcome inspection, an admission team separate from analysts and scorers must verify detailed pre-intervention documentation, a frozen documentation cutoff, observable internal components and relations, at least one real documented intervention or failure, outcome separability, functional differentiation for selective-loss prediction, no essential undocumented proprietary mechanism, and analyst screening for prior outcome knowledge.

Selection because a known outcome appears favorable to IMI is prohibited.

## Custody separation

Required functions are `SOURCE_ACQUISITION_CUSTODIAN`, `OUTCOME_CUSTODIAN`, `ANALYST_COORDINATOR`, and `BLINDED_SCORING_TEAM`. Incompatible functions may not be held by the same person or team.

The pre-outcome package and sealed outcome package receive separate SHA-256 hashes. Outcome leakage includes post-intervention reports, revealing filenames, retrospective summaries, issue resolutions, repair descriptions, later diagrams, revealing metadata, prior analyst familiarity, and unrestricted external search. A material leak yields `STUDY_INVALID`.

## Analysts

Eligibility must be frozen before recruitment and may not depend on agreement with IMI. Analysts require relevant documented expertise, demonstrated technical-document interpretation ability, no IMI development role, no prior target-outcome access, and format-only training on an unrelated system.

Analysts may learn the schema but may not be trained toward intended routes. They work independently and may not communicate about the target until all maps and predictions are hashed and frozen.

## Submission

Every analyst submits a machine-readable system boundary, directed typed relations, constitutive routes, criticality classifications, intervention-specific predictions, explicit falsifiers, source citations, uncertainty, and an independence/no-search attestation. Prose-only submissions are incomplete.

## Normalization and matching

Alias normalization uses frozen source-grounded component identifiers while preserving boundary decisions, direction, required-versus-associated status, and route structure. Every decision is logged while blind to outcomes and any theory-preferred map. Post-outcome route merging or splitting is prohibited.

Route alignment is label-invariant and uses a frozen maximum-weight bipartite assignment based only on endpoints, required-relation membership, and topology.

## Estimands

Primary estimands are boundary Jaccard and chance-corrected agreement, matched-route required-relation Jaccard, structured Krippendorff alpha, normalized graph-edit similarity, ordinal criticality agreement, intervention-specific prediction agreement and calibration, and constrained permutation-null exceedance.

Permutation nulls preserve analyst component counts, route sizes, component frequencies where feasible, edge counts, and criticality marginals. Unconstrained randomization is prohibited.

## Threshold calibration

Target thresholds must be fixed from unrelated calibration systems or preregistered simulation before any target score is viewed. Candidate numeric regions in `protocol.v1.json` remain non-operative until calibration is completed.

## Comparators

Independent arms receive the same lawful pre-outcome information budget: `GENERIC_DEPENDENCY_GRAPH`, `OUTPUT_HISTORY_TRAJECTORY_ONLY`, `OUTPUT_HISTORY_INTERVENTION_CONDITIONED`, and `BLACK_BOX_FEATURE_MODEL`.

The same analyst may not produce both constitutive-route and generic-dependency maps for the same target. Training periods, variables, intervention encodings, forecast horizons, hyperparameters, model-selection rules, and deadlines are frozen before unblinding.

## Support–falsification asymmetry

Low reproducibility can falsify independent identifiability. High reproducibility cannot by itself establish constitutive validity.

Constitutive validity requires `reproducible route map + selective held-out prediction + advantage over generic comparators`.

## Terminal logic

`ROUTE_IDENTIFICATION_SUPPORTED` requires valid custody, preregistered support on boundary, relations, routes, and criticality, exceedance of the chance null, and robustness to lawful normalization and route-label permutation. It does not establish constitutive validity.

`ROUTE_IDENTIFICATION_AMBIGUOUS` is limited to estimates crossing frozen regions, insufficient precision, representational-level conflict, or dependence on a preregistered unresolved equivalence rule.

`CONSTITUTIVE_ROUTE_PREMISE_FALSIFIED` is permitted after a valid study when qualified analysts produce materially incompatible maps; fail to distinguish required relations from ordinary dependencies; perform no better than generic graph identification; require post-outcome revision; fail comparator prediction tests; repeatedly permit unrelated compensation; or depend on exposure to the intended IMI interpretation.

`STUDY_UNEVALUABLE` applies only when an uncontaminated admitted package cannot support the required analysis.

`STUDY_INVALID` applies to leakage, custody failure, material deviation, retrospective threshold selection, collusion, or post-outcome reinterpretation.

Precedence is `INVALID > UNEVALUABLE > VALID_NUMERIC_RESULT`.

## Authorization

Authorized now: protocol hashing, exact-head verification, public registration, candidate-system acquisition, role recruitment, unrelated calibration-system preparation, and sealed-package infrastructure.

Not authorized now: target analyst access, target mapping, target scoring, outcome unblinding, scientific credit, or successor-equation finalization.
