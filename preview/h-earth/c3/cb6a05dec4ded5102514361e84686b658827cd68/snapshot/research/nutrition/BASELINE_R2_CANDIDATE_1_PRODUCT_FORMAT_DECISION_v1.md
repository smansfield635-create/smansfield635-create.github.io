## 1. PURPOSE

Select the most appropriate development format for R2 Candidate 1 without reopening unnecessary laboratory infrastructure.

The format must preserve:

- meal-proximal acid delivery,
- low calories,
- low ingredient burden,
- rapid consumption,
- portability,
- reasonable taste-management potential,
- minimal preparation friction,
- clear separation from protein/fiber preload products.

This is a product-format decision, not a final formulation or dosing instruction.

---

## 2. CANDIDATE 1 CORE

Candidate 1 remains:

**acid-mediated minimalist pre-meal transition architecture**

Development mechanism:

**meal-proximal acetic-acid exposure**

Evidence-derived development region:

**approximately 0.5–1.25 g acetic acid**

This remains a research/development envelope rather than consumer dosing guidance.

No R1 electrolyte, magnesium, or dextrose component is inherited automatically.

# 3. FORMAT A — FULL READY-TO-DRINK BOTTLE

## Concept

A pre-diluted beverage containing the complete Candidate 1 system.

### Advantages

- no user mixing,
- consistent dilution if manufactured correctly,
- easiest consumer instruction,
- predictable consumption format,
- potential flavor optimization.

### Disadvantages

- transports mostly water,
- larger package,
- higher shipping/storage burden,
- more packaging material,
- less portable,
- more expensive distribution,
- shelf-life and beverage preservation become larger commercial problems.

### Baseline fit

**GOOD FUNCTIONALLY / MODERATE COMMERCIALLY**

### Decision

**HOLD AS SECONDARY FORMAT**

Useful if convenience ultimately outweighs logistics.

---

# 4. FORMAT B — SINGLE-SERVE LIQUID CONCENTRATE + WATER

## Concept

A small, measured single-use concentrate designed to be diluted into a defined amount of water shortly before a meal.

### Advantages

- low shipping mass,
- portable,
- single-serving dose control,
- allows substantial dilution before oral exposure,
- keeps final drink volume flexible within a controlled instruction,
- flavor/aroma system can travel with concentrate,
- does not require protein/fiber-sized packaging,
- consumer adds ordinary water,
- preserves rapid liquid delivery.

### Disadvantages

- one preparation step,
- dilution compliance matters,
- concentrate itself must not be treated as a direct-consumption “shot,”
- packaging must clearly prevent misuse,
- user needs water.

### Dental/GI implication

Potentially favorable relative to an undiluted shot because the intended use explicitly requires dilution.

Still requires later qualified review; dilution does not itself prove safety.

### Baseline fit

**HIGH**

### Decision

**SELECT AS PRIMARY DEVELOPMENT FORMAT**

---

# 5. FORMAT C — UNDILUTED ACID SHOT

## Concept

Small-volume direct-consumption acidic shot.

### Advantages

- highly portable,
- no mixing,
- fast.

### Disadvantages

- concentrated oral contact,
- harsh sensory burden,
- greater dental-contact concern,
- greater throat/esophageal burden,
- conflicts with the product's safety/tolerability objectives.

### Baseline fit

**LOW**

### Decision

**DROP**

Baseline should not solve portability by concentrating the main liability.

---

# 6. FORMAT D — POWDER / DRY STICK PACK

## Concept

Dry packet reconstituted in water.

### Scientific issue

Acetic acid is naturally a liquid/volatile acid. A dry format would require an alternative acid-delivery chemistry, carrier, salt, encapsulation, or precursor system.

That cannot be assumed mechanistically equivalent to liquid acetic acid/vinegar evidence.

### Advantages

- excellent portability,
- low shipping weight,
- familiar supplement format.

### Disadvantages

- changes active chemistry/delivery,
- equivalence to human vinegar evidence uncertain,
- may require additional excipients,
- reopens mechanism-validation questions.

### Baseline fit

**HIGH LOGISTICALLY / UNCERTAIN SCIENTIFICALLY**

### Decision

**HOLD**

Do not choose convenience over mechanism preservation.

---

# 7. FORMAT E — CAPSULE / TABLET

## Concept

Solid oral dosage form.

### Advantages

- portability,
- taste avoidance,
- simple use.

### Scientific weakness

Published evidence indicates that nominally similar vinegar-tablet exposure should not be assumed equivalent to liquid vinegar.

Delivery kinetics and actual acid exposure become different scientific objects.

### Baseline fit

**LOW FOR CURRENT CANDIDATE**

### Decision

**DROP AS PRIMARY R2 FORMAT**

May only reopen with independent evidence demonstrating equivalence.

---

# 8. FORMAT F — MULTI-SERVE CONCENTRATE BOTTLE

## Concept

Bottle containing multiple measured concentrate servings, each diluted before use.

### Advantages

- less packaging per serving,
- low shipping mass,
- potentially lower cost.

### Disadvantages

- consumer measuring error,
- dose consistency depends on user behavior,
- greater risk of accidental undiluted use,
- portability weaker than single-serve,
- contamination/closure stability after opening.

### Baseline fit

**MODERATE**

### Decision

**HOLD AS FUTURE ECONOMY FORMAT**

Not the first development object.

## 10. SELECTED DEVELOPMENT FORMAT

# SINGLE-SERVE DILUTION CONCENTRATE

The R2 Candidate 1 product-development concept becomes:

> **A small single-serving liquid concentrate that is added to a defined amount of water and consumed shortly before a meal.**

The concentrate is **not intended for direct consumption**.

This format best preserves the human evidence's liquid/rapidly available acid architecture while reducing shipping volume and allowing dilution to be part of the product design.

---

## 11. CONSUMER-EXPERIENCE TARGET

The intended eventual experience is:

1. open one measured serving,
2. add to water,
3. mix briefly,
4. consume promptly,
5. eat the normal meal within the validated meal-proximal window.

No shaker bottle.

No thick texture.

No large powder scoop.

No prolonged sipping.

No multi-pill regimen.

---

## 12. WHY THIS IS DIFFERENT FROM R1

R1 requires the complete active mixture to be prepared in 350 mL water.

Candidate 1's selected format separates:

**portable active concentrate**

from

**consumer-provided dilution water**

That gives R2 a potentially stronger consumer-product architecture even before efficacy is established:

- lighter distribution,
- simpler active system,
- easier portability,
- preserved liquid delivery,
- flexible manufacturing options.

These are design advantages, not proof of physiological superiority.

---

## 13. OPEN VARIABLES — DELIBERATELY NOT OVERENGINEERED

The following remain open for ordinary formulation development:

- exact acid source,
- exact active exposure within the evidence-derived region,
- concentrate volume,
- final dilution volume,
- flavor/aroma system,
- sweetness strategy if any,
- package material,
- shelf-life,
- final meal timing.

These should be resolved with a qualified beverage/formulation manufacturer after the development architecture is stable.

They do not require another large theoretical research program.

---

## 14. NONNEGOTIABLE PRODUCT RULES

The eventual Candidate 1 product must:

- require dilution,
- make direct concentrate consumption clearly inappropriate,
- remain very low calorie,
- preserve liquid acid availability,
- avoid unnecessary electrolytes/micronutrients,
- avoid protein/fiber-scale burden,
- avoid prolonged sipping,
- retain a meal-proximal use case,
- undergo appropriate formulation/safety review before human testing.

---

## 15. KILL CONDITIONS FOR SELECTED FORMAT

Abandon the single-serve concentrate format if ordinary professional formulation work shows that:

- required dilution is impractically large,
- acceptable flavor cannot be achieved without undermining mechanism,
- concentrate packaging cannot safely/reliably contain the system,
- direct-consumption misuse cannot reasonably be mitigated,
- dental/GI concerns remain unacceptable after dilution,
- manufacturing economics eliminate the portability/logistics advantage.

If killed, the ready-to-drink format becomes the first fallback.
