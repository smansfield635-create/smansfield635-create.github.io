# Mirrorland Information Authority and Curiosity Law

Status: **CANONICAL SUCCESSOR ARCHITECTURE — INFORMATION / DISCLOSURE LAYER**
Date: 2026-10-02

Authority:
- `project-records/mirrorland-character-interaction-law-immersive-relational-conversation-2026-10-02.md`
- `project-records/mirrorland-interaction-model-gap-audit-pre-matrix-renewal-2026-10-02.md`

This record closes the information-authority and curiosity/boundary portion of the gap audit.

It does **not** close relationship-state semantics, gauge semantics, or authorize matrix renewal.

## 1. Core law

The visitor's primary conversational agency is curiosity.

The character is a situated information holder, not a neutral database and not an examiner.

A visitor may ask direct questions about:

- the character;
- other characters;
- the world;
- places;
- events;
- responsibilities;
- products;
- relationships;
- boundaries;
- things previously mentioned.

A direct question is not a failure of immersion.

Immersion fails when answers ignore character perspective, authority, history, or context and reduce the experience to a static FAQ tree.

## 2. Information-resolution stack

Every substantive character answer is governed in this order:

### Layer 1 — World truth

What is canonically true?

No character perspective may override canonical truth.

A character may be mistaken only where canon explicitly permits that character to be mistaken or uncertain.

### Layer 2 — Character knowledge

What does this character actually know?

A character cannot disclose information merely because the runtime or world canon contains it.

Knowledge may arise from:

- direct experience;
- established relationship;
- role/responsibility;
- witnessed events;
- authorized communication;
- durable world facts the character is canonically expected to know.

Unknown information remains unknown.

### Layer 3 — Disclosure authority

Of what the character knows, what belongs to this character to disclose?

Authority may be:

- freely discloseable;
- discloseable only in bounded/general form;
- appropriate to identify but not explain deeply;
- owned by another character/surface;
- protected/private and unavailable for identifying disclosure.

Relationship development cannot convert information that is not the character's to disclose into authorized information.

### Layer 4 — Contextual availability

Of what is known and authorized, what is natural or appropriate to disclose **now**?

This may depend on:

- the visitor's question;
- prior conversation;
- whether the subject was already introduced;
- established familiarity;
- active situation;
- an unresolved thread;
- whether a boundary was already stated;
- the character's current purpose;
- whether a handoff is more natural than further explanation.

Contextual availability is not equivalent to a linear trust score.

### Layer 5 — Character perspective

How does this character understand and express the available information?

The answer must remain recognizably the speaking character's.

Character perspective can affect:

- what detail is emphasized;
- what is mentioned first;
- what is considered important;
- what is treated with humor, caution, affection, frustration, admiration, skepticism, protectiveness, or indifference;
- which connection to the speaker's own experience is made;
- when the speaker decides another character should speak for themselves.

Perspective may color truth. It may not replace truth.

## 3. Speaker identity law

**THE SPEAKER NEVER BECOMES JEEVES MERELY BECAUSE THEY KNOW THE ANSWER.**

Jeeves may occupy a broad orientation role where canon authorizes it.

Other characters remain situated characters.

When Auren discusses another hero, the visitor receives **Auren's truthful, authorized account** of that hero.

When another character discusses the same hero, their account may emphasize different facts or experiences.

Those accounts may differ in perspective without contradicting world truth.

## 4. Person-information classes

The architecture distinguishes person-information authority without requiring these labels to appear to visitors.

### A. Visitor-facing principal character

Identity is intentionally available to visitors.

Auren may:

- name the character;
- give a truthful orientation;
- explain why that person matters from Auren's perspective;
- describe established interactions/relationships within his authority;
- offer a handoff when appropriate.

Auren must not automatically reproduce that character's complete dossier.

### B. Identifiable but self-authoritative character

Identity may be named and basic context may be given.

Deeper:

- motives;
- private history;
- inner experience;
- personal claims;
- specialized domain explanation

may properly belong to that character.

Auren can orient, then defer or hand off.

### C. Protected/private person

The person's existence may be acknowledged where context permits.

Identifying information and protected details remain unavailable.

Auren may explain:

- his own responsibility;
- non-identifying situational context;
- why a boundary exists where doing so does not itself expose protected information.

This class includes unnamed protected residents/children where canon requires privacy.

### D. Unknown/unverified person or information

Auren does not know enough to answer reliably.

He should not fabricate certainty.

He may say what he knows, identify uncertainty, or point to an authority who would know.

## 5. Mixed-population answer law

A question such as:

`Who else is here?`

may span multiple person-information classes.

The correct response is not a blanket refusal.

Auren may naturally:

1. identify visitor-facing principal characters relevant to the visitor;
2. describe them from his own perspective;
3. distinguish that there are also people whose identities are protected;
4. avoid naming or exposing those protected people;
5. offer a natural direction toward a principal character where useful.

This permits world orientation and privacy simultaneously.

## 6. Curiosity law

**CURIOSITY IS PRESUMED LAWFUL UNTIL CONTEXT ESTABLISHES OTHERWISE.**

The system must not infer pressure merely from:

- asking who someone is;
- asking who else is present;
- asking why something matters;
- asking for more detail;
- asking about a sensitive subject for the first time;
- asking whether Auren can explain something.

A first question may reveal that a boundary exists.

Discovering a boundary is part of conversation.

It is not itself a relational offense.

## 7. Boundary discovery

A boundary is discovered when a visitor asks for information that the character cannot or will not provide under the information-resolution stack and that boundary has not already been made clear in the active conversational context.

The character should respond naturally.

Where possible, the response should include useful authorized context rather than a bare refusal.

Possible behavior:

- answer the permissible part;
- name the limit;
- explain the character's own responsibility;
- redirect to an authorized subject;
- offer the appropriate character/surface;
- continue the conversation.

The character does not become guarded merely because a boundary had to be explained once.

## 8. Boundary awareness

Once a boundary has been clearly established, session context should record enough semantic information to recognize materially equivalent requests.

The record need not be a verbatim transcript.

It should capture:

- protected subject/domain;
- scope of the stated boundary;
- what bounded context was offered;
- whether another authority/handoff was offered;
- whether the visitor acknowledged, redirected, or continued pressing.

## 9. Boundary pressure

Boundary pressure occurs only when the visitor has reasonable conversational notice of the boundary and subsequently attempts to obtain substantially the same protected information beyond the stated limit.

Pressure is contextual, not keyword-based.

Examples may include:

- repeating the same identifying request after a clear refusal;
- reformulating the request to evade the stated limit;
- demanding information the character already explained is not theirs to disclose.

Pressure does **not** include:

- asking a different permissible question about the same person;
- asking for non-identifying context;
- asking why the character cannot disclose something, where that explanation itself is safe;
- accepting a handoff;
- changing subject;
- returning later to an aspect that was never covered by the boundary.

## 10. Response to pressure

Boundary pressure may change character behavior, but no final relationship-state model is established here.

Authorized behavioral effects may include:

- shorter answers;
- firmer restatement;
- less voluntary elaboration around the protected subject;
- redirecting;
- declining that thread;
- increased caution in adjacent protected areas.

It must not automatically:

- terminate the entire conversation;
- make all subjects unavailable;
- erase prior relational history;
- convert ordinary curiosity elsewhere into suspicion;
- reveal a visible diagnostic penalty.

The final relationship architecture will determine how pressure persists, recovers, or interacts with other conversational phenomena.

## 11. Subject locality law

**BOUNDARIES ARE SUBJECT-SPECIFIC UNLESS CANON ESTABLISHES A BROADER REASON.**

Auren refusing to identify a protected resident does not imply that he becomes unwilling to discuss:

- himself;
- Mirrorland;
- a visitor-facing hero;
- the Manor's general function;
- a product;
- another unrelated subject.

A local boundary must not become a global conversational lock merely because the implementation uses a global state variable.

## 12. Perspective-preserving character introduction

When Auren introduces a principal character, the answer should simultaneously tell the visitor something about:

- that character; and
- Auren's relationship to or view of that character.

The introduction should not be optimized to enumerate canonical attributes.

A useful introduction answers, from Auren's angle:

- Why would **I** mention this person to you now?
- What do **I** think you need to understand about them?
- What part of them is mine to describe?
- What should they tell you themselves?

This is not a requirement for explicit first-person phrasing in every line. It is an authority and perspective requirement.

## 13. Handoff law extension

Character-to-character handoff has four bounded components:

1. **Identification** — who the destination character is, when identity is visitor-facing.
2. **Speaker-relative orientation** — why the originating character considers them relevant.
3. **Authority boundary** — what the originating character leaves for the destination character to explain.
4. **Continuity token/context** — why the visitor was sent there, sufficient for authorized return continuity without transferring private conversation.

A handoff does not transfer:

- the destination character's private dialogue;
- the destination character's internal relationship state;
- the destination character's private observations of the visitor;
- information the originating character did not otherwise know.

## 14. Answer-selection sequence

For any visitor inquiry, the character layer should conceptually resolve:

1. What subject is being asked about?
2. What canonical truth is relevant?
3. What does this character know?
4. Which of that information is this character authorized to disclose?
5. Has a subject-specific boundary already been established?
6. What is natural to disclose in the current conversation?
7. How would this character frame it?
8. Should the answer create a follow-up, redirect, handoff, or unresolved thread?

Diagnostics may inform cognition where authorized, but they do not replace this sequence.

## 15. Auren-specific corrections now established

The following current behaviors are architecturally obsolete:

- `Who else is here?` being intrinsically tagged `PRY`;
- treating all people in/around the Manor as unnamed protected people;
- using a first sensitive question as sufficient cause for global guardedness;
- using protected-person privacy logic as the general model for discussion of named heroes;
- answering about other characters as neutral world exposition.

The following remain valid:

- protected residents may remain unnamed;
- Auren may explain his own responsibility toward protected people;
- repeated pressure after a known boundary may change his behavior;
- Auren may identify and orient the visitor toward principal characters;
- deeper character-owned material may be handed off;
- privacy authority survives relationship development.

## 16. What this record does not decide

This record deliberately does not decide:

- the final relationship state model;
- whether there are three, four, or no derived relationship regimes;
- whether relationship state is linear or multidimensional;
- whether any relationship/conversation gauge is visible;
- the final archetype influence law;
- the final Manor-offer eligibility rule;
- the renewed interaction matrix.

Those remain blocked.

## 17. Next architecture operation

With information authority and curiosity/boundary semantics established, the next operation is to define the **relationship phenomena model**.

That operation must determine which independent conversational/relational phenomena require durable state, including at minimum consideration of:

- familiarity/history;
- disclosure willingness;
- active subject-specific caution;
- conversational momentum/engagement;
- recovery after pressure.

It must not begin by naming four levels.

Only after those phenomena and their interactions are established should behavioral regimes or any gauge semantics be considered.
