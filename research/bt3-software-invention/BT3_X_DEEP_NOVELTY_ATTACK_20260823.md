## Material prior art found

### 1. Proof-Carrying Authorization

Proof-carrying authorization already binds named principals, policies, judgments, certificates, and machine-checkable proof. A reference monitor reconstructs and verifies a proof that a principal is entitled to establish an authorization judgment. This is materially relevant because it demonstrates that computational authority can be represented as typed/provable judgments rather than merely coarse role permissions.

Representative source:
- An Introduction to Proof-Carrying Authorization — https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=34299869cd1ccf8b911fdc03598a027e79b53a9f&repid=rep1&type=pdf

### 2. Information-flow control with explicit authority/trust types

Information-flow and decentralized authorization systems have long represented authority, trust, endorsement/declassification, principals, and admissible information movement in the type system and execution semantics. These systems show that authority labels and typed constraints over who may establish or transform information are established architecture.

Representative source:
- Trust, Authority, and Information Flow — Cornell / Fabric lineage — https://www.cs.cornell.edu/andru/papers/mdg-thesis.pdf

### 3. Provenance + information-flow audit

Information-flow audit research explicitly combines runtime enforcement decisions with provenance-like graphs that retain who, when, why, how, declassification, endorsement, and security-context changes. This materially weakens any novelty argument based simply on preserving authorization defects after later system evolution.

Representative source:
- Towards Practical Information Flow Control and Audit — Cambridge — https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/techreports/UCAM-CL-TR-893.pdf

### 4. Correct outcome / wrong authority distinction

A directly dangerous 2026 match explicitly argues that correct outcomes can still be ungoverned because they rely on the wrong authority, unsupported completion claims, or stale dependencies. Its Matrix architecture records authority and fact dependencies in deterministic causal state and preserves governing evidence even when direct and governed workflows reach the same outcomes.

Representative source:
- Jesus Salas, `Correct Is Not Governed: Provenance Integrity in Agentic Workflows`, arXiv:2608.12761, 2026-08-13.

This source is especially material because the central BT3-X experimental observation — equal outcome does not imply equal authority/provenance validity — is independently and explicitly represented.

### 5. Commit-time / reconstructive authority

Recent execution-governance work independently formalizes the principle that endpoint success does not imply authorized completion and that authority must remain valid at the durable execution boundary. Runtime enforcement, dependency reconstruction, and authority freshness are explicit concerns.

Representative sources:
- `Temporary Authority, Permanent Effects: Commit-Time Authorization for LLM Agents`, arXiv:2607.10487.
- `Operationalizing Reconstructive Authority: Runtime Construction, Dependency Resolution, and Execution Gating in Autonomous Agent Systems`, arXiv:2605.23935.

### 6. Typed action claims checked against policy and provenance

Explanation-Bound Tool Execution converts rationale content into typed action claims and checks those claims against server-held intent, policy, payload, tool, risk, provenance, and freshness facts. That demonstrates contemporary explicit typed-claim governance rather than merely action-level RBAC.

Representative source:
- `Explanation-Bound Tool Execution for AI Agents: Server-Verified Action Claims Without Trusting Model Rationales`, arXiv:2607.25364.

### 7. Policy-consistent change provenance and semantic authorization

Recent provenance work explicitly distinguishes successful or technically valid changes from changes that are authorized by the correct approval intent and scope, combining artifact-level evidence, provenance, policy admissibility, and machine-checkable approval semantics.

Representative source:
- `Policy-Consistent Change Provenance for Legitimacy Validation of Enterprise Configuration Modifications`, IEEE Access DOI 10.1109/ACCESS.2026.3708695.

None of the sampled patent claims was identical to the frozen BT3-X law, but the neighborhood is sufficiently dense that absence of an exact phrase cannot support novelty.
