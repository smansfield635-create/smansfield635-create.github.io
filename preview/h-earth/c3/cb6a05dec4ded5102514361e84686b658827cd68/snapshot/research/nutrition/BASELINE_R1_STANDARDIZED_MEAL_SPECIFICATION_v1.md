## 1. PURPOSE

Define one reproducible mixed breakfast for the R1-versus-350-mL-water crossover pilot so the meal does not become an uncontrolled experimental variable.

The meal is not designed as a sodium, glucose, fat, fiber, or calorie challenge. It is a standardized mixed-meal exposure used to measure hunger, fullness, eating behavior, intake, and post-meal comfort after R1 versus matched water.

R2 remains **NOT YET AUTHORIZED**.

---

## 2. DESIGN REQUIREMENTS

The meal must:

- contain carbohydrate, protein, and fat,
- be ordinary and tolerable for the screened population,
- avoid deliberately extreme sodium,
- avoid unusually high fiber,
- contain no caffeine or alcohol,
- avoid spicy/challenge ingredients,
- permit objective gravimetric intake measurement,
- be large enough that a participant can voluntarily stop before finishing,
- be prepared identically across paired sessions.

Nutrient composition must ultimately be calculated from the exact commercial labels and/or USDA FoodData Central entries tied to the actual foods used. USDA identifies FoodData Central as its food-composition database for researchers and professionals.

---

## 3. STANDARDIZED BREAKFAST — LOCKED ARCHITECTURE

### Component A — scrambled egg

- 2 large Grade A shell eggs
- no added salt
- cooked using 5.0 g unsalted butter

### Component B — toast

- 2 slices standardized commercially packaged whole-wheat bread
- exact brand/SKU frozen before participant 001
- no additional butter, jam, nut butter, or spread

### Component C — banana

- 1 standardized peeled banana portion
- edible portion target: **120 g**
- tolerance: **±5 g**

### Component D — plain Greek yogurt

- plain, unsweetened Greek yogurt
- target serving: **170 g**
- exact brand/SKU and fat percentage frozen before participant 001
- no fruit, granola, honey, sweetener, or flavoring added

This creates a mixed breakfast without deliberately turning the pilot into a high-sodium, high-sugar, high-fat, or high-fiber challenge.

---

## 4. OFFERED PORTION

The complete offered meal consists of:

- 2 cooked eggs,
- 5.0 g unsalted butter used in cooking,
- 2 slices whole-wheat bread,
- 120 ±5 g peeled banana,
- 170 g plain Greek yogurt.

The final offered edible mass is recorded after preparation and immediately before service.

No participant is required to finish the meal.

Neutral instruction:

> Eat as much or as little of the meal as you want. Stop when you feel comfortably satisfied.

Do not mention a predicted R1 effect.

---

## 7. NUTRITIONAL BOUNDARY

This meal must remain a **normal mixed breakfast**, not a mechanistic challenge meal.

Do not intentionally modify it to produce:

- very high sodium,
- very high carbohydrate,
- very high fat,
- unusually high protein,
- unusually high fiber,
- unusually high caloric density.

If exact product selection causes the meal to become materially extreme in one of those dimensions, the products must be reconsidered before participant enrollment, not after outcomes are observed.

---

## 8. ALLERGEN PROFILE

Expected major allergen exposure includes:

- egg,
- milk,
- wheat.

Depending on manufacturing statements, selected commercial products may carry additional allergen/cross-contact warnings.

The exact allergen statements from every commercial package must be transcribed into the study meal-source record before recruitment.

A participant with a relevant known allergy/intolerance is not exposed to the meal under this protocol.

## 9. PREPARATION — EGGS

For each session:

1. crack exactly 2 large eggs into the standardized preparation vessel,
2. mix using the same procedure,
3. use exactly 5.0 g unsalted butter,
4. add no salt, pepper, seasoning, cheese, milk, cream, oil, or other ingredient,
5. cook using the same appliance/pan and predefined heat procedure where practical,
6. serve promptly within the locked serving-temperature procedure.

The cooking SOP must be rehearsed before participant 001 so material cooking differences are minimized.

---

## 10. PREPARATION — TOAST

Use exactly 2 slices from the frozen bread SKU.

Bread may be served toasted, but the toast setting/time must be fixed prospectively and applied identically across sessions.

No toppings.

If toast is used, all sessions use toast; do not alternate toasted versus untoasted bread.

---

## 11. PREPARATION — BANANA

Remove peel before weighing.

Serve:

**120 ±5 g edible banana.**

If one banana does not produce the required edible mass, trim or supplement from the same source batch as necessary.

No toppings or processing.

---

## 12. PREPARATION — YOGURT

Weigh:

**170 g**

into the standardized serving vessel.

Serve plain.

No sweeteners or additions.

---

## 13. FOOD TEMPERATURE / SERVICE CONTROL

### Hot components

Eggs and toast should be served freshly prepared using the same timing sequence across visits.

Record service time.

A practical serving-temperature band for the egg component must be validated during the pre-pilot rehearsal and then frozen in the kitchen SOP before participant 001.

### Cold components

Yogurt remains refrigerated until plating.

Banana is served at normal room/holding temperature under the same procedure across visits.

The purpose is reproducibility, not sensory optimization.

---

## 14. PLATING

Use the same:

- plate/bowl types,
- utensils,
- physical arrangement where practical,
- serving environment.

All edible components are weighed/recorded according to the gravimetric SOP.

Do not present extra food.

---

## 15. GRAVIMETRIC INTAKE PROCEDURE

Immediately before service:

1. tare/record serving vessels according to the validated weighing method,
2. record total edible mass offered,
3. record meal start time.

At participant stop:

1. record meal stop time,
2. collect all remaining edible food,
3. weigh remaining edible mass using the same procedure,
4. exclude non-edible waste from the remaining-edible calculation,
5. calculate consumed mass.

**Consumed mass = offered edible mass − remaining edible mass.**

Primary eating rate:

**consumed mass (g) / meal duration (min).**

Use the same calibrated digital scale for paired visits where practical.

---

## 16. MEAL DURATION

Maximum observation window:

**30 minutes.**

Participant may stop at any time before 30 minutes.

At 30 minutes, the meal observation ends under the protocol.

Do not encourage faster or slower eating.

---

## 17. BEVERAGE RULE

No routine beverage is served with the meal.

This preserves the matched 350-mL preload comparison.

Any fluid required for a predefined safety/medication exception is measured and recorded as a protocol deviation.

---

## 18. PALATABILITY CONTROL

The meal should be acceptable but deliberately plain.

Do not alter seasoning or add condiments because a participant dislikes the meal during one session.

If a participant cannot reasonably tolerate the standardized meal, this protocol is not suitable for that participant.

Paired-session meal exposure takes precedence over culinary preference.

---

## 19. SESSION COMPARABILITY GATE

Before a paired session is accepted as clean, verify:

- same egg count/size class,
- same butter mass,
- same bread SKU and slice count,
- same banana mass tolerance,
- same yogurt SKU and mass,
- same cooking SOP,
- same toast state,
- same plating procedure,
- same meal-observation rule,
- no unplanned beverage/condiment.

A material meal mismatch is handled under the parameter lock's deviation/exclusion rules before unmasking.

---

## 20. FOOD SAFETY

Preparation, holding, service, and storage must follow applicable food-safety requirements for the study setting.

Do not serve:

- visibly spoiled food,
- improperly stored perishable food,
- cracked/unsafe eggs,
- expired or compromised commercial products.

Record product dates/lot information where available.

Food-safety failure cancels the session rather than being treated as an experimental exposure.

## 23. CLAIM CONTROL

The standardized meal is an experimental control object.

Its presence does not establish that Baseline:

- balances an inadequate meal,
- protects against sodium,
- controls glucose,
- protects vascular function,
- buffers dietary fat,
- treats dehydration,
- suppresses appetite,
- causes weight loss.

Those remain outside the R1 pilot claim boundary.
