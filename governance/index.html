PL4 · Governance · Planetary Attachment

Governance keeps decision pressure bounded.

Governance is the public decision surface for Diamond Gate Bridge. It separates policy language, compliance review, public risk, authority boundaries, and decision records so the estate can act without pretending every statement has the same status.

Public Status

  • Surface Public-summary governance chamber
  • Authority Bounded decision routing and review language
  • Evidence Receipts visible as structural markers, not certification
  • Boundary No legal, compliance, production, or scientific validation claim

Expandable Governance Chambers

Open only the layer you need.

Each chamber contains three sub-layers: practical, engineering, and evidence. This keeps the page from forcing a full governance brief into one continuous scroll.

Policy Engine

Rules must remain readable before they become actions.

Policy Engine organizes language used for boundaries, permissions, restrictions, escalation, and public commitments.

Practical What policy means to visitors, builders, reviewers, and collaborators.

Practical governance tells people what kind of statement they are reading. A rule is different from a proposal. A proposal is different from a result. A result is different from a validated claim.

Visitor Can see what the estate is and is not claiming.
Builder Can act without overstating authority.
Reviewer Can separate language from proof.
Engineering What policy does as a state-bearing system layer.

Engineering governance treats policy as a state-bearing layer. Rules should identify scope, permission, restriction, dependency, escalation path, and receipt requirement without taking over measurement, narrative, product, or runtime authority.

State Draft, review, held, approved, blocked, or retired.
Scope Route, product, instrument, world, claim, or public copy.
Receipt Decision marker, review marker, or public-summary marker.
Evidence What this public page supports and what it does not prove.

The public page can show that governance structure exists. It does not prove legal compliance, regulatory certification, production readiness, scientific validation, or implementation correctness by itself.

Evidence remains bounded to visible markers, route structure, published language, and later review records when those records are explicitly provided.

Compliance Grid

Checks must be visible without becoming certification.

Compliance Grid gives review language a place to stand while preserving the difference between internal checks and external compliance status.

Practical How compliance language prevents confusion.

Compliance language should prevent confusion. A page can be reviewed for clarity, safety, routing, claims, accessibility, and public boundaries without implying that a regulator, court, lab, or external authority has certified it.

Engineering How review classes remain separated.

The grid separates review classes: claim review, route review, copy review, evidence review, accessibility review, runtime review, and deployment review. A pass in one class does not automatically propagate to all classes.

Copy Review Checks whether public language overstates its standing.
Evidence Review Checks whether support exists for the stated level of claim.
Runtime Review Checks whether behavior is supported by execution evidence.
Evidence Why an internal check is not an external certificate.

Current public standing is a governance surface with visible markers. It is not an external compliance certificate, legal opinion, production authorization, or completed third-party audit.

Public Risk Meter

Risk should be classified before it is minimized.

The meter gives public-facing risk language a restrained set of categories.

Practical How readers understand risk without panic or false certainty.

Public risk categories tell the reader whether a surface is informational, under review, held, blocked, or escalated. This protects the estate from presenting uncertainty as confidence.

Engineering How risk states can affect routing, copy, and controls.

Risk states should be machine-readable where appropriate and human-readable everywhere. A risk state can inform copy, navigation, disabled actions, receipts, review banners, and downstream routing.

Watch The surface is active, but future changes should be monitored.
Review The surface needs inspection before stronger claims or decisions are attached.
Hold The surface should not advance until the relevant issue is resolved.
Block The proposed action is not allowed under the current boundary.
Evidence Why risk classification is not proof of safety.

Risk states are governance classifications. They are not proof that no risk exists, not a safety certification, and not a substitute for domain-specific review.

Decision Index

Decisions need class, reason, boundary, and receipt.

Decision Index turns action into a traceable record instead of a loose instruction.

Practical How a decision communicates what happened and what did not happen.

A decision should tell people what happened and what did not happen. Approved does not mean universally validated. Blocked does not mean permanently impossible. Review required does not mean failed.

Engineering How a record preserves decision meaning for later systems.

Decision records should preserve identifier, class, scope, dependency, reason, allowed action, prohibited inference, and receipt surface. Later systems may consume the record without rewriting its meaning.

Evidence What the public decision-index model does not publish by itself.

This public page shows the decision-index model. It does not publish a complete governance database, historical audit log, or external review record unless those artifacts are separately provided.

Review Chambers

Supporting governance layers remain collapsed until needed.

Governance Chain

Observation moves through rule, review, decision, receipt, and return.

The chain keeps decision stages separated enough to audit without forcing the visitor to read every stage by default.

Observation Something is noticed, reported, measured, or proposed.
Rule The relevant boundary or standard is identified.
Review The issue is compared against scope, evidence, and authority.
Decision The action is classed without overstating its reach.
Receipt The record preserves what was decided and what was not.
Return The estate returns to the right room, route, or review queue.

Boundary: Governance may organize decision flow, route authority, public language, and review status. It does not replace evidence, law, scientific validation, runtime testing, or domain-specific professional review.

Authority Boundaries

Governance has power only where its authority is declared.

This chamber separates what governance may decide from what it may not claim.

Governance may decide

  • Whether public copy needs a boundary statement.
  • Whether a claim requires review before publication.
  • Whether a route should be held, revised, or escalated.
  • Whether a decision needs a receipt or visible status marker.
  • Whether a page should distinguish practical, engineering, and evidence interpretations.

Governance may not decide

  • That a scientific claim is validated without supporting evidence.
  • That a legal or regulatory requirement is satisfied without appropriate review.
  • That a runtime system works without execution evidence.
  • That a production deployment is authorized by public copy alone.
  • That one room absorbs the authority of the entire estate.
