---

## 1. PURPOSE

Test whether the existing Baseline R1 formulation produces a measurable improvement in the fasted-to-meal transition beyond the effect of consuming the same volume of water alone.

This protocol is deliberately narrow. It does not test, imply, or authorize claims for:

- high-sodium buffering,
- glycemic buffering,
- high-fat vascular buffering,
- nutritional balancing of an inadequate meal,
- disease treatment or prevention.

R2 remains **NOT YET AUTHORIZED**.

---

## 2. PRIMARY RESEARCH QUESTION

> After a defined food-free interval, does R1 improve the transition into a standardized meal compared with 350 mL matched water?

The pilot is designed to separate effects attributable to R1 from ordinary fluid-preload, stomach-volume, pause, and study-ritual effects.

---

## 3. R1 INTERVENTION — FROZEN

R1 is not reformulated for this pilot.

Per test serving:

- sodium chloride: 750 mg
- potassium chloride: 250 mg
- magnesium citrate material: 100 mg
- dextrose: 1.5 g
- water: 350 mL

The exact magnesium-citrate specification and elemental magnesium contribution must be recorded from the actual raw-material certificate/specification before dosing.

No additional active ingredients are authorized.

### Comparator

- 350 mL water
- same serving temperature range
- same container format
- same consumption window
- same pre-meal delay

The comparator is intended to isolate whether the dissolved R1 components add measurable value beyond matched water.

---

## 4. STUDY CLASSIFICATION

This is an exploratory pilot, not a confirmatory clinical trial.

Recommended architecture:

**Randomized, counterbalanced, within-subject crossover pilot.**

Each participant completes both conditions:

- Condition A — R1 in 350 mL water
- Condition B — 350 mL water comparator

Condition order is randomized/counterbalanced.

The same participant therefore serves as their own comparator, reducing between-person variability in hunger, meal tolerance, eating behavior, and subjective response.

---

## 5. PARTICIPANT SAFETY GATE

Before human execution, the protocol, formulation specifications, consent language, recruitment method, and data handling should receive appropriate qualified human-subject/safety review for the setting in which the pilot will be conducted.

### Candidate adult population

Initial feasibility testing should use adults able to provide informed consent and comply with the standardized food-free interval and meal protocol.

### Exclusion / clinician-review triggers

Do not enroll without appropriate medical review when a candidate has a condition or treatment that could materially alter safety or interpretation, including:

- known kidney impairment,
- clinician-directed sodium or potassium restriction,
- known electrolyte disorder,
- diabetes or clinically significant glucose-regulation disorder,
- use of medication known to materially alter potassium, sodium, glucose, fluid balance, or gastric function,
- pregnancy or breastfeeding,
- known allergy/intolerance to any test material or standardized-meal component,
- active gastrointestinal disease likely to affect meal tolerance or gastric behavior,
- current acute illness,
- history or current presentation of an eating disorder where fasting or intake measurement would be inappropriate,
- any other condition identified by the reviewing clinician/research professional as making participation inappropriate.

This list is a pilot safety screen, not a complete medical eligibility standard.

---

## 6. STANDARDIZED PRE-TEST STATE

The defining exposure is a controlled food-free interval.

The exact duration must be selected and frozen before recruitment/data collection. It must be long enough to generate a meaningful pre-meal transition without imposing an unnecessarily prolonged fast.

Once selected, the interval cannot vary by condition for a participant.

During the interval:

- no caloric food or drink,
- no alcohol,
- no discretionary electrolyte products,
- no unrecorded supplements intended to affect appetite, hydration, glucose, or digestion,
- water policy must be standardized and identical before both conditions,
- strenuous exercise immediately before testing should be restricted or standardized,
- caffeine and nicotine policy, if applicable, must be predefined and identical across visits.

Participants record:

- last meal time,
- last caloric intake,
- water intake during the controlled interval,
- sleep duration,
- unusual exercise,
- relevant protocol deviations,
- baseline symptoms.

A session failing the prespecified food-free interval or hydration-policy requirements is flagged before outcome interpretation.

---

## 7. STANDARDIZED MEAL

The post-preload meal must be identical across both conditions for each participant.

Before execution, freeze:

- meal composition,
- total amount offered,
- serving temperature where relevant,
- eating environment,
- utensils/container format,
- permitted beverages,
- meal start time relative to preload,
- maximum observation window.

The meal should be ordinary enough to support tolerability and behavioral measurement without being deliberately extreme in sodium, carbohydrate, fat, or calories.

This pilot is not a challenge test for high-sodium, high-carbohydrate, or high-fat buffering.

---

## 8. TEST-DAY SEQUENCE

### T-0: baseline

Immediately before intervention, record:

- hunger,
- thirst,
- fullness,
- GI comfort,
- general pre-meal comfort,
- protocol compliance/deviations.

### Intervention

Participant consumes the assigned 350 mL condition within the same predefined consumption window.

Record:

- start time,
- finish time,
- incomplete consumption,
- immediate intolerance.

### Pre-meal transition window

Use one fixed delay for both conditions.

During this interval, no additional food or caloric drink is permitted.

Immediately before the meal, repeat:

- hunger,
- thirst,
- fullness,
- GI comfort,
- general comfort.

### Meal

Record:

- meal start time,
- meal stop time,
- total meal duration,
- amount consumed,
- optional eating-rate observations if the measurement method is standardized.

### Post-meal

At predefined intervals, record:

- fullness,
- GI comfort,
- post-meal comfort,
- thirst,
- adverse symptoms.

All measurement timepoints must be frozen before the first analyzable participant.

---

## 9. OUTCOME SET

### Primary pilot outcomes

The pilot's core discrimination set is:

1. pre-meal hunger change,
2. pre-meal thirst change,
3. pre-meal fullness change,
4. meal duration,
5. eating rate where reliably measurable,
6. total meal intake,
7. GI comfort,
8. post-meal comfort.

### Supporting outcomes

May include:

- water consumed with the meal,
- participant preference after both conditions are completed,
- immediate palatability/tolerability,
- adverse-event frequency.

### Explicitly non-primary in v1

The following are not required to establish the first R1 signal:

- continuous glucose monitoring,
- blood pressure response,
- vascular measures,
- triglycerides,
- urinary sodium/potassium,
- laboratory hydration biomarkers.

These belong to later mechanism-specific work only if the R1 pilot justifies escalation.

---

## 10. MEASUREMENT RULES

Subjective measures should use the same scale, anchors, instructions, and interface at every timepoint.

The scale and direction must be frozen before data collection.

Meal intake should be measured by a predefined reproducible method, preferably direct mass or otherwise a documented quantitative method.

Eating rate should not be inferred casually. If measured, define it prospectively, for example:

- consumed mass / meal duration, or
- standardized bite-count method / meal duration.

Protocol staff must not reinterpret scales or endpoints after observing results.

---

## 11. RANDOMIZATION AND COUNTERBALANCING

Participants are assigned to one of two sequences:

- R1 → Water
- Water → R1

Sequence assignment should be generated before the participant's first condition.

The washout interval must be sufficient to prevent one session's immediate meal/hydration state from carrying into the next test session. Test sessions should be scheduled under comparable conditions, including approximately comparable time of day where practical.

Order must be retained in the dataset so sequence/order effects can be inspected.

---

## 12. BLINDING

Full sensory blinding may be difficult because R1 can differ from water in taste.

Do not describe the study as blinded unless blinding is actually implemented and tested.

At minimum:

- use neutral condition identifiers in the dataset,
- use standardized instructions,
- avoid telling participants the predicted direction of effect,
- keep outcome collection as mechanically identical as possible.

Any detectable taste difference is recorded as a limitation.

---

## 13. DATA STRUCTURE

Each analyzable record should contain at minimum:

- participant code,
- session number,
- randomized sequence,
- condition,
- date/time,
- food-free interval achieved,
- relevant pre-session water intake,
- baseline outcome values,
- pre-meal outcome values,
- meal start/stop times,
- meal amount offered,
- meal amount consumed,
- post-meal outcome values,
- adverse symptoms,
- protocol deviations.

Personally identifying information should be separated from the analytic dataset where applicable.

---

## 14. ANALYSIS PLAN

The primary comparison is **within participant: R1 versus matched water**.

For each prespecified outcome:

- calculate the relevant change from baseline where appropriate,
- calculate the within-person R1-minus-water difference,
- report the distribution of individual responses,
- report central tendency and uncertainty,
- report missing observations and protocol deviations.

Because this is an exploratory pilot, do not convert a small or underpowered sample into a binary proof/no-proof claim based only on statistical significance.

Interpret:

- direction,
- magnitude,
- consistency,
- uncertainty,
- tolerability,
- feasibility.

A later confirmatory study requires a prospectively justified sample size and analysis plan informed by pilot variance/effect estimates.

---

## 15. ADJUDICATION GATES

### A. SIGNAL FOR FURTHER R1 INVESTIGATION

R1 shows a coherent, reproducible, tolerable advantage over matched water on one or more prespecified transition outcomes of meaningful magnitude.

This authorizes further testing.

It does **not** prove a broad Baseline mechanism or authorize excluded claims.

### B. NO DETECTABLE ADDED ADVANTAGE

R1 and matched water are materially similar across the prespecified outcome set, with uncertainty narrow enough to make a practically important R1 advantage unlikely.

Interpretation:

The pilot does not establish that R1's dissolved components add useful transition benefit beyond 350 mL water under the tested conditions.

### C. TOLERABILITY FAILURE

R1 produces meaningfully worse GI comfort, adverse symptoms, poor acceptability, or another safety/tolerability signal relative to water.

Interpretation:

Do not advance R1 unchanged without review.

### D. UNINTERPRETABLE

Examples:

- inadequate adherence to the food-free interval,
- inconsistent meal exposure,
- uncontrolled water intake,
- substantial missing data,
- measurement failure,
- systematic order effects,
- sample too small/variable to distinguish plausible effects.

Interpretation:

Repair the protocol or measurement system before making a mechanism decision.

---

## 16. KILL CRITERION

The R1 active-ingredient advantage for the tested fasted-to-meal use case is not supported if a properly executed pilot shows no practically meaningful advantage over matched water across the prespecified transition outcomes.

A null R1-versus-water result does not mean that pre-meal water is ineffective.

It means the additional R1 components have not earned attribution for an added effect under this protocol.

---

## 17. CLAIM BOUNDARY

Even a favorable pilot result supports only a bounded statement such as:

> Under the tested conditions, R1 produced a measurable difference from matched water on specified meal-transition outcomes.

It does not establish:

- disease prevention or treatment,
- correction of dehydration,
- electrolyte deficiency treatment,
- sodium-load protection,
- glycemic control,
- cardiovascular protection,
- fat-load buffering,
- nutritional completion of an inadequate meal,
- generalized benefit across meals or populations.

---

## 18. STOP / SAFETY RULE

Any serious or unexpected adverse event requires suspension of further dosing pending appropriate review.

Participants must be free to stop participation at any time.

Symptoms requiring medical evaluation should be handled through appropriate clinical/emergency channels rather than interpreted inside the research protocol.

## 20. DETERMINISTIC INTERPRETATION

The protocol asks one question only:

> **Does R1 add measurable value beyond 350 mL water during a controlled fasted-to-meal transition?**

Possible exits:

**R1 > WATER WITH ACCEPTABLE TOLERABILITY**  
→ replicate/refine R1 testing before expanding mechanism claims.

**R1 ≈ WATER**  
→ attribute the observed preload effect primarily to water/volume/ritual unless later evidence demonstrates otherwise; do not invent an R1-specific mechanism.

**R1 < WATER OR TOLERABILITY FAILURE**  
→ stop advancement of unchanged R1 and investigate the cause before any new formulation work.

**UNINTERPRETABLE**  
→ repair the experimental system and repeat; do not advance to R2 on ambiguous evidence.
