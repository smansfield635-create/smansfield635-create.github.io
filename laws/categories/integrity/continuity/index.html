Plain-language reading

Continuity is integrity under time. It asks whether identity, standards, correction, evidence, and essential service remain intact after interruption, context change, and delay.

Memory

What state and evidence remain available.

Transfer

What survives movement between holders or contexts.

Trajectory

Whether direction remains intelligible over time.

Decay

What is lost, degraded, or no longer recoverable.

Engineering controls

Expose redundancy, fallback, reserve, recovery time, state restoration, dependency identity, evidence retention, and the point at which degradation becomes loss.

Failure behavior

Continuity is not preserved merely because some output remains visible. Essential identity or service must persist, or recovery must be bounded and verified.

Reading layers

Practical, Engineering, and Empirical claims remain separate.

Practical reading

Show what people or operations needed to keep working and the consequence of interruption.

Engineering reading

Expose redundancy, fallback, reserve, recovery time, state restoration, and dependencies.

Empirical reading

Show uptime, interruption duration, recovery time, lost function, and evidence of restored service.

Method

Track retained state, transfer identity, trajectory, interruption, fallback, recovery, and decay in one time-ordered record.

Audit boundary

Potential early warning, redundancy, or retained data does not establish continuity benefit until essential function or bounded recovery is actually measured.
