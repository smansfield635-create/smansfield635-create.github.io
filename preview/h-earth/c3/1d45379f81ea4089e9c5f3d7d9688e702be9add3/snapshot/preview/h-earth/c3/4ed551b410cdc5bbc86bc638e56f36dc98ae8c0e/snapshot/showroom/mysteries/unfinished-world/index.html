The Unfinished World.

Hearth showed the work behind the window. Now the world refuses to look complete.

You leave the facility with the wrong kind of relief. You expected Hearth to prove that Audralia was ready. Instead, the next view opens into a map that keeps stopping. Some terrain resolves. Some routes begin and halt. Some regions stay veiled. Audralia is not a finished paradise waiting to be entered. It is a living frontier whose incompletion must be faced honestly.

The First West Condition

Incompletion is not automatically failure.

The West path begins by refusing false closure.

01 · Broken

Some things stopped because they could not survive.

A route ends where the ground buckled. A structure leans toward a purpose it never reached. A system exceeded its limits and left evidence in the shape of its fracture.

A broken thing still records the pressure that broke it.
02 · Interrupted

Some things stopped because another pressure took priority.

Work may have been redirected, delayed, evacuated, or postponed. The unfinished section is not proof that the mission was abandoned. It may be proof that the world demanded triage.

What stopped may still matter even when no one returned to finish it.
03 · Deliberately Open

Some things remain open because closure would be dishonest.

A responsible builder can leave a gap visible when the available evidence cannot support a final structure. The absence protects a later generation from inheriting false certainty.

Restraint can look unfinished from the outside.
The Repairer’s Temptation

You want to complete what the map leaves open.

Completion feels like progress. But repair without diagnosis can destroy the very evidence that tells you what kind of world you are standing in.

The Map of What Is Missing

Absence has structure.

A gap becomes intelligible only when its place in the larger system remains visible.

Location Where is the gap?

A missing bridge, district, route, archive, body, or system means something different depending on where it belongs.

Intention What was supposed to exist?

Recover the plan without pretending the planned future was inevitable, complete, or safe.

Interruption Why did continuation stop?

Identify failure, scarcity, uncertainty, restraint, displacement, prohibition, or a pressure that forced priority elsewhere.

Consequence What would completion change?

A repair may reduce danger, erase evidence, create new pressure, close choice, or open a path the system is not ready to survive.

The Rule of the Unfinished World

Do not complete what you have not understood.

Audralia’s incompletion is not a defect to hide. It is the frontier condition. Some gaps mark damage. Some mark restraint. Some mark future work not yet safe to close. The next chamber turns the unfinished map back toward you and asks what your own questions reveal.
