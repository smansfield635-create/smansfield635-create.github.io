Failure Boundary

Shared premise

Transport, arrival, acceptance, preserved meaning, and successful continuation are separate states; failure can occur between any two of them.

  1. Separate send from arrival

    Story focus. A send event proves attempted transfer; it does not prove that the intended receiver obtained the object intact.

    Practical

    Lens frame. Judge a handoff by whether the receiver can continue correctly, not merely by whether an object changed location or an acknowledgement appeared.

    What this selection adds. Do not tell the sender's story as if it were the receiver's. Check destination, arrival, and integrity before treating the transfer as delivered.

    Engineering

    Lens frame. Expose send, transport, arrival, validation, acceptance, receipt, and next-state transition as independently observable states with separate failure outputs.

    What this selection adds. Preserve independent send and arrival identifiers, timestamps, destination identity, retry state, and integrity result so transport loss cannot masquerade as completion.

    Empirical

    Lens frame. No admitted study currently validates this route. Evidence must therefore be stated as the records required to distinguish each handoff state, not as a completed empirical result.

    What this selection adds. Required evidence is a matched send-and-arrival trace with unmatched, duplicated, delayed, or corrupted transfers retained. No current admitted study supplies that trace here.

  2. Separate arrival from acceptance

    Story focus. Arrival proves presence at an interface; acceptance proves that the receiver validated and took responsibility for the delivered state.

    Practical

    Lens frame. Judge a handoff by whether the receiver can continue correctly, not merely by whether an object changed location or an acknowledgement appeared.

    What this selection adds. A package, instruction, or responsibility can arrive and still be unusable or refused. Ask whether the receiver understood and accepted what arrived.

    Engineering

    Lens frame. Expose send, transport, arrival, validation, acceptance, receipt, and next-state transition as independently observable states with separate failure outputs.

    What this selection adds. Record validation outcome, schema or protocol compatibility, accepting owner, rejection reason, and acknowledgement separately from transport arrival.

    Empirical

    Lens frame. No admitted study currently validates this route. Evidence must therefore be stated as the records required to distinguish each handoff state, not as a completed empirical result.

    What this selection adds. Required evidence pairs arrival records with acceptance or rejection outcomes. An arrival log alone cannot support a claim that the handoff was accepted.

  3. Separate acceptance from continuity

    Story focus. Acceptance proves receipt and responsibility; continuity proves that the receiver could use the preserved state to produce the correct next action.

    Practical

    Lens frame. Judge a handoff by whether the receiver can continue correctly, not merely by whether an object changed location or an acknowledgement appeared.

    What this selection adds. Ask whether work actually continued without re-creating missing meaning. A polite acknowledgement can conceal a failed operational handoff.

    Engineering

    Lens frame. Expose send, transport, arrival, validation, acceptance, receipt, and next-state transition as independently observable states with separate failure outputs.

    What this selection adds. Link the acceptance receipt to the next valid state transition and expose missing context, incompatible state, duplicate execution, or stalled continuation.

    Empirical

    Lens frame. No admitted study currently validates this route. Evidence must therefore be stated as the records required to distinguish each handoff state, not as a completed empirical result.

    What this selection adds. Required evidence connects acceptance to a subsequent correct action under the received context. Without that continuation trace, empirical standing remains open.

  4. Name the claim boundary

    Story focus. The claim boundary states exactly which handoff state was observed so transport evidence is not promoted into acceptance or continuity evidence.

    Practical

    Lens frame. Judge a handoff by whether the receiver can continue correctly, not merely by whether an object changed location or an acknowledgement appeared.

    What this selection adds. Say “sent,” “arrived,” “accepted,” or “continued” according to the last state actually established. Do not compress the sequence into “handoff succeeded.”

    Engineering

    Lens frame. Expose send, transport, arrival, validation, acceptance, receipt, and next-state transition as independently observable states with separate failure outputs.

    What this selection adds. Emit a separate receipt for each completed state and stop the success classification at the first missing or failed transition.

    Empirical

    Lens frame. No admitted study currently validates this route. Evidence must therefore be stated as the records required to distinguish each handoff state, not as a completed empirical result.

    What this selection adds. The current route is conceptual and procedural. A future study must preserve state-by-state records before claiming observed handoff continuity.

    Claim boundary. Movement alone is not a successful handoff; meaning, state, custody, and acceptance must be preserved.
