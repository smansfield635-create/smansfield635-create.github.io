# Laws Geometric Grammar Laboratory

Prototype ID: `DGB_LAWS_GEOMETRIC_GRAMMAR_SYNTHETIC_COMMONS_CROSSING_v1`

Status: isolated delivery research. Non-production. Synthetic fixture only.

## Purpose

This prototype tests whether the Laws Chamber can use geometry, interaction, motion, mathematics, and progressive disclosure to tell a practical and profound story while preserving engineering transparency and strict empirical boundaries.

It is **not a tutorial about constructing 3D objects**. The geometry exists only to support the supplemented utility and collaborative primitives of Diamond Gate Bridge: pressure, process, admission, typed support, available futures, recurrence, contradiction, invariant identity, and custody.

The practical question is fictional:

> Can a shared crossing reopen without sacrificing safety, trust, or traceability?

No real bridge, community, certification, dataset, investigation, or validated law is represented.

## What the visitor experiences

The default surface is the Practical lens. It explains what each relationship means, why it matters, and where the trajectory leads if the condition persists.

The Engineering lens explains the typed primitive, declared relationship, motion purpose, and static equivalent.

The Empirical lens states what the prototype does and does not establish. Visual settlement never changes evidence status.

The nine canonical primitives are:

1. Field — pressure and environmental condition
2. Path — process and trajectory
3. Gate — admission and threshold
4. Lattice — typed support and dependency
5. Volume — bounded available state space
6. Orbit — recurrence and return
7. Fracture — explicit contradiction or lost continuity
8. Crystal — stable identity under transformation
9. Ledger — custody and exact transition history

## Architecture

- `index.html` — semantic content, lens controls, MathML, static equivalent, and audit depth
- `styles.css` — responsive visual language and reduced-motion handling
- `scene-spec.js` — declarative geometric meaning authority
- `renderer-webgl2.js` — native WebGL2 presentation adapter implementing the bounded renderer contract
- `app.js` — DOM controller, stage traversal, parameters, lifecycle, and equivalence coordination
- `verify.mjs` — static contract verification

Authority separation:

`SEMANTIC DOM = CONTENT AND ACTION AUTHORITY`

`SCENE SPECIFICATION = GEOMETRIC MEANING AUTHORITY`

`WEBGL2 ADAPTER = PRESENTATION AUTHORITY ONLY`

`MATHML = FORMAL MATHEMATICAL SURFACE`

## Motion and fallback

Three coequal presentation modes are available:

- Full motion — bounded stage settlement and explanatory deformation
- Reduced motion — direct state substitution with minimal transition
- Static — labeled SVG relationship map

The prototype stops rendering when outside the viewport or when the document is hidden. It uses `ResizeObserver`, explicit resource disposal, bounded pixel ratio, and a readable render receipt.
