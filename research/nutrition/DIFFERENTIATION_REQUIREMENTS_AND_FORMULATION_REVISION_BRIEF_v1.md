**REVISE**

# OBJECT_A_AMPM

## 2. DIFFERENTIATION FAILURE TO AVOID

The revised AM/PM product must not rely on any of the following as its primary differentiator:

- morning/evening timing by itself
- personalization by itself
- daily packetization
- magnesium in the evening
- broad multivitamin coverage
- “energy AM / recovery PM” marketing
- natural ingredients
- convenience alone

These are already represented in the current market.

---

## 3. REQUIRED DIFFERENTIATION STACK

OBJECT_A_AMPM should only earn PASS if the product demonstrates a combined architecture built around:

### A. COMPACT ESSENTIAL COVERAGE

Target:
- cover a deliberately selected essential micronutrient foundation
- avoid unnecessary botanical/adaptogen expansion
- avoid ingredient-count inflation for marketing purposes

Required proof:
- every active ingredient has an explicit nutritional or engineering reason
- every excluded nutrient has an explicit reason if omission is material

### B. MINIMUM PRACTICAL UNIT BURDEN

Historical target:
- approximately 2 AM capsules
- 1 omega-3 softgel
- approximately 2 PM capsules
- approximately 5 total units/day

Revision requirement:
- confirm actual raw-material mass and capsule/softgel feasibility
- compare total daily unit count against direct AM/PM competitors
- retain the historical target only if physically feasible

Preferred differentiation target:

**<=5 total physical units/day**

for the common adult foundation, including omega-3, unless manufacturing reality demonstrates that a slightly higher count produces a materially better product.

### C. INTERACTION-DRIVEN TIMING

Every AM/PM allocation must be assigned one of these explicit reasons:

- competitive absorption
- food dependence
- fat dependence
- GI-tolerance management
- dose splitting
- stimulant/non-stimulant effect
- drug/nutrient separation
- physical unit-burden balancing

Generic “morning energy” and “night recovery” explanations are insufficient.

### D. ANTI-BLOAT RULE

No ingredient may be added merely to increase label complexity.

New ingredient admission requires:

1. clear physiological purpose,
2. evidence state,
3. safety review,
4. physical-burden impact,
5. timing consequence,
6. manufacturer feasibility.

### E. BOUNDED PERSONALIZATION

Personalization may vary only predefined elements.

The common foundation must remain dominant.

Personalization must not turn the product into a large custom supplement stack.

---

## 4. OBJECT_A PASS CONDITIONS

OBJECT_A_AMPM may move to PASS only when all of the following are true:

1. common adult foundation is defined,
2. exact chemical forms are specified,
3. raw-material mass is calculated,
4. physical unit count is demonstrated,
5. unit burden compares favorably with direct competitors,
6. AM/PM assignments have mechanistic or tolerance-based reasons,
7. unnecessary ingredient proliferation is absent,
8. the product remains clearly different from generic AM/PM vitamin packs.

### Current state

**REVISE**

### Immediate revision work

1. exact chemical-form completion
2. mass/volume calculation
3. direct unit-burden competitor crosswalk
4. timing justification table
5. essential-only ingredient challenge

---

## 6. ELEMENTAL RECONCILIATION

### Sodium chloride

Molecular-weight fraction of sodium in NaCl:

**~39.34%**

Therefore:

750 mg sodium chloride
→ approximately **295 mg elemental sodium**

### Potassium chloride

Molecular-weight fraction of potassium in KCl:

**~52.44%**

Therefore:

250 mg potassium chloride
→ approximately **131 mg elemental potassium**

### Magnesium citrate

The exact elemental magnesium contribution cannot be locked from the phrase “magnesium citrate 100 mg” alone because commercial magnesium citrate may use different chemical/hydration forms and specifications.

Required next step:
- identify exact magnesium citrate material
- obtain elemental magnesium specification / COA
- then calculate label-level magnesium

### Dextrose

Recovered amount:
- 1.5 g carbohydrate source per 500 mL

---

## 7. CRITICAL BASELINE CORRECTION

The historical formula is not strongly “potassium-forward” when evaluated from the recovered sodium chloride and potassium chloride compound masses.

Recovered elemental approximation before magnesium reconciliation:

- sodium: ~295 mg
- potassium: ~131 mg

Therefore the earlier phrase **potassium-forward** must remain:

**UNPROVEN / REQUIRES FORMULATION REVISION OR TERMINOLOGY CORRECTION**

Do not market or canonize that characterization unless a revised formulation supports it.

## 8. BASELINE DIFFERENTIATION FAILURE TO AVOID

The revised product must not rely on:

- low sugar
- simple ingredients
- natural ingredients
- hydration
- potassium
- magnesium
- pre-meal timing alone

as sufficient differentiation.

## 9. REQUIRED BASELINE DIFFERENTIATION STACK

Baseline should earn PASS only if the exact formulation expresses a purpose-built meal-transition architecture.

### A. PRE-MEAL FUNCTION MUST DRIVE THE FORMULA

Each ingredient must answer:

> Why is this amount useful before a meal rather than merely useful in a hydration drink?

Required rationale fields:

- intended pre-meal function
- dose rationale
- expected physiological role
- timing rationale
- reason this dose differs from sports/recovery hydration

### B. FLUID VOLUME

Historical volume:
- 500 mL

This must be challenged.

Revision must determine whether the intended pre-meal serving should remain:

- 500 mL,
- a lower volume,
- or another defined volume.

Decision criteria:

- stomach burden before eating
- hydration objective
- concentration/osmolality
- palatability
- adherence
- intended meal timing

### C. CARBOHYDRATE BURDEN

Historical:
- 1.5 g dextrose

Required determination:

- retain,
- reduce,
- increase,
- or remove.

The decision must be based on the desired meal-transition mechanism rather than imitation of oral rehydration products.

### D. ELECTROLYTE RATIO

The revised formula must intentionally define:

- elemental sodium
- elemental potassium
- elemental magnesium
- sodium:potassium relationship
- total ionic burden

The ratio must have a documented reason related to the pre-meal use case.

### E. OSMOLALITY / CONCENTRATION

Before PASS, the formula needs:

- calculated first-pass osmotic contribution,
- manufacturer/bench confirmation where required,
- confirmation that concentration is compatible with intended pre-meal use.

### F. MINIMALISM

Every additional ingredient must survive the question:

> Does this materially improve the pre-meal transition objective?

If not, exclude it.

Historical optional L-theanine, taurine, glycine, and zinc states remain outside the first formula unless they independently earn admission.

---

## 10. DIRECT COMPARATOR REQUIREMENT

Baseline revision must be explicitly compared against four product classes:

### Hydrant-type
Electrolyte + carbohydrate hydration.

### LMNT/Purelyte-type
High/minimalist electrolyte hydration.

### BOOST pre-meal-type
Protein-based pre-meal satiety products.

### Water
No-active comparator.

The revised Baseline product must be able to answer:

1. why it is not simply Hydrant with different ratios,
2. why it is not simply a lower-sodium LMNT/Purelyte,
3. why it is not competing on protein/fiber appetite suppression,
4. why water alone is not functionally equivalent to its intended use.
