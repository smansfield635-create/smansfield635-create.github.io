What the battery result establishes—and what it does not.

The test used 1,653 cell-disjoint held-out cycles from three batteries excluded from development. It supports transfer-to-unseen-battery inference inside this domain, not universal-law inference.

Scientific meaning

The same result, separated by purpose.

The result matters because the model recognized near-term degradation in batteries it had not seen during development. That is stronger than fitting familiar batteries, but it is still a domain-specific result.

The cell-disjoint split supports held-out transfer across CS2_34, CS2_36, and CS2_38. Axis AUROC 0.9394 indicates strong ranking performance. Causal or mechanistic interpretation remains blocked by conventional degradation confounds and absent external replication.

Why the held-out split matters

A model that works on unfamiliar batteries provides stronger evidence than one tested only on batteries used during development.

Cell-disjoint evaluation reduces direct cell-identity leakage and supports transfer inference within the tested battery family. It does not establish chemistry-general or manufacturer-general transfer.

Why burden dominance matters

The strongest single signal may be describing accumulated use rather than a new mechanism. That does not erase the prediction result, but it changes what can be claimed about why it works.

Because burden AUROC exceeds the combined axis model, the decisive test is incremental performance after controlling cycle number, cumulative throughput, current capacity, and recent degradation slope.

What would elevate the claim

Independent predictive value after conventional controls; raw-plus-axes incremental improvement; transfer across cell families and chemistries; external dataset replication; threshold recalibration without leakage; and preregistered replication.
