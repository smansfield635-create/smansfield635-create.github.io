## Purpose

Build a complete cross-compass calibration chamber before any further ARCHCOIN implementation.

The chamber compares mechanisms by capability rather than by page identity. It separates reusable invariants, calibration parameters, optional capabilities, and page-specific identity before admitting any behavior into ARCHCOIN.

## Controlling sequence

1. MAIN COMPASS
   - extract physics, navigation, selection, settlement, interruption recovery, and passage custody;
2. LAW COMPASS
   - extract fluid motion, interaction sensitivity, interpolation, hit corridors, and responsibility separation;
3. SHOWROOM COMPASS
   - extract public-stage scale, camera behavior, scene integration, readability, and presentation behavior;
4. ARCHCOIN COMPASS
   - extract literal cardinal semantics, current cluster behavior, center authority, labels, fixed-axis deficiencies, selection problems, and preserved successful behavior;
5. CROSS-COMPASS COMPARISON
   - compare every mechanism by capability rather than by page identity;
6. UNIVERSAL COMPASS STANDARD
   - establish reusable laws, optional profiles, prohibited couplings, interfaces, and acceptance gates;
7. ARCHCOIN CALIBRATION
   - apply only admitted portions of the universal standard to the restored functional ARCHCOIN baseline.

## Evidence classes

1. `SOURCE_EVIDENCE`
   - behavior directly supported by inspected source;
2. `EXTRACTED_CAPABILITY`
   - a bounded mechanism recovered from source without page identity;
3. `CANDIDATE_STANDARD`
   - a proposed reusable rule not yet admitted;
4. `ADMITTED_STANDARD`
   - a rule that has passed compatibility, conflict, behavioral, visual, accessibility, and preservation review;
5. `REJECTED_MECHANISM`
   - a mechanism or coupling explicitly prohibited from reuse.

## Standard layers

### Universal invariants

Rules every Compass must satisfy.

### Calibration parameters

Values that may differ by page, device, scale, cluster size, input method, reduced-motion state, and presentation environment.

### Optional capabilities

Examples include a planet participant, Mirrorland passage, panel descent, ambient motion, narrative presentation, and literal cardinal semantics.

### Page-specific identity

Names, routes, copy, colors, room records, law records, financial domains, and visual themes cannot enter the reusable core.

## Candidate universal invariants

- controller owns canonical state and navigation authority;
- interactions own pointer interpretation and gesture proposals;
- world or geometry records remain distinct from projection;
- preview, commit, cancel, selection, and navigation remain separate;
- one active gesture transaction exists at a time;
- orientation records are complete, normalized, and revisioned;
- constellation and cluster orientation custody remain independent;
- cluster members preserve a coherent shared spatial relation;
- visual and semantic targets derive from the same projection facts;
- rear/front priority is explicit;
- selection does not require chasing continuous ambient motion;
- cancellation restores a deterministic committed state;
- reduced motion preserves usability;
- pointer interruption cannot produce accidental navigation;
- page-specific identities cannot enter the reusable core.

These remain candidate invariants until the four source-family extractions and cross-compass conflict review are complete.

## Calibrated profiles

- `MAIN_PROFILE` — strong navigation, selection, settlement, and passage custody;
- `LAW_PROFILE` — fast fluid motion and strong interaction separation;
- `SHOWROOM_PROFILE` — public-stage scale, visual readability, and scene integration;
- `ARCHCOIN_PROFILE` — literal cardinal semantics, financial-domain navigation, fixed-axis cluster discipline, stable selection, and center globe.

No profile is the universal template. Profiles are calibrated assemblies under one standard.
